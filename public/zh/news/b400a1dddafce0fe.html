Investing.com -- SpaceX shares rose by more than 2% in premarket U.S. trading on Friday after Bloomberg News reported that the rocket company is in discussions to secure access to at least 80,000 cell sites across the United States.

Bloomberg reported that the move would signal a concrete step toward establishing a fourth nationwide wireless carrier. The satellite giant is said to be negotiating with major tower operators, including Crown Castle International Corp (NYSE:CCI), as it gathers the physical infrastructure required to complement its orbital network. Crown Castle shares surged by more than 7% premarket.

During an earnings call on Wednesday, Crown Castle Chief Executive Officer Chris Hillabrant declined to address specific client negotiations but acknowledged broader conversations across the sector. He noted that the company is actively engaged in discussions with all major satellite-based telecommunications providers exploring ground-level deployment.

Mobile networks rely heavily on ground-based tower antennas to route signals between hand-held devices and core network hubs. While traditional carriers such as AT&T Inc., Verizon Communications Inc., and T-Mobile US Inc. lease space on shared infrastructure to manage capital costs, SpaceX is leveraging a hybrid model that pairs terrestrial cells with its satellite constellation.

The potential leasing agreements are expected to reach into the billions of dollars, reflecting the vast scale of the physical network required. Chief Executive Officer Elon Musk has positioned the initiative as a direct challenge to incumbent carriers, supported by recent regulatory milestones and strategic spectrum acquisitions.

The Federal Communications Commission granted SpaceX approval to launch 15,000 specialized satellites engineered to stream mobile connectivity directly to standard smartphones. Integrating tens of thousands of ground sites will allow the company to manage signal density and deliver reliable broadband across dense suburban and urban corridors.

Additionally, SpaceX recently acquired low-band spectrum assets, establishing the foundational airwaves necessary to transform its Starlink constellation into a standalone commercial network. This low-frequency spectrum provides superior building penetration and broad geographic reach, serving as a critical bridge between orbital satellite signals and ground-level cellular infrastructure.

Equity markets reacted swiftly to the prospect of massive new capital deployment across the tower sector.
