FCC警告TP-Link违规

✍️ 记者 / 供稿: Bloomberg News

FCC向TP-Link发出警告，称其部分路由器功率超标，并质疑其提交虚假信息

⚡ 核心要点提炼与关键事实

  • 【核心事件】FCC与TP-Link在通信设备合规市场：因产品功率违规及涉嫌提交虚假资料被警告
  • 【起因与机理】为了保障无线频段秩序与市场准入合规性
  • 【核心事实细节】美国联邦通信委员会（FCC）向TP-Link发出警告，指出其三款路由器产品违反了功率限制标准，且涉嫌提交错误或伪造信息。
  • 【各方立场与态度】在大国博弈的背景下，想在海外安稳卖路由器，合规比信号强度重要得多。

Bloomberg 权威实录

NCTA and Vantiva gained fresh FCC waivers to update previously approved routers under the Commission's ban on foreign-made routers. TP-Link, meanwhile, remains in limbo despite pledge to bring manufacturing to US shores.

As some router makers and industry organizations cut a path through the FCC's ban on foreign-made consumer routers, others remain stuck in the regulatory weeds.

In the latest round of relief from the ban, the NCTA – The Internet & Television Association – secured its second expedited waiver while Vantiva obtained conditional approval for a fiber router that's tailored for AT&T. Meanwhile, there's been no public action on a pending approval request submitted by TP-Link.

Expedited waivers are generally focused on routers that have previously received approval by the FCC, but must undergo a fresh review due to minor hardware changes (for new components, including memory, and changeouts of components that are reaching end-of-life). Conditional approvals generally apply to new router products, including Wi-Fi extenders, that haven't yet gone through the FCC's certification process.

On November 5, the FCC granted NCTA's request for a waiver to make what it believes are necessary Class I and Class II permissive hardware changes to previously authorized consumer-grade routers. Generally, Class I changes include minor modifications that don't change the RF characteristics of a router, and Class II includes more substantive modifications that may alter RF performance or device emissions.

NCTA filed this request in September as its members and router partners sought to make changes to previously approved devices that are now on the FCC's "Covered List," which includes products deemed to pose unacceptable risk to US national security. Beyond national security, the ban also aims to nudge router makers to shift some manufacturing to the US.

NCTA reasoned that router changes are necessary as its members (which include major cable operators such as Comcast and Spectrum) grapple with changeouts of components that are reaching end-of-life or as suppliers try to use different types of memory or source them from new partners in the face of ongoing supply chain constraints. NCTA told the FCC that any changes would not involve substituting a US-produced component with a foreign-made component.

The FCC grant to the NCTA's waiver request is good until Nov. 5, 2027, and generally applies to NCTA members and their suppliers.

In June, the FCC's Office of Engineering and Technology approved a separate and more narrowly focused expedited waiver sought by the NCTA that focused on relatively small changes to routers, including alterations to memory modules and other types of substitute "substrate materials." The FCC has previously approved similar expedited waivers sought by AT&T and Sercomm.

Vantiva, a supplier of broadband and video customer premises equipment (CPE) for multiple industries, secured a waiver to make Class I and Class II hardware changes to the BGW620, a previously-approved consumer-grade fiber router for AT&T. AT&T, which offers that router under the "All-Fi Hub" brand, was granted a one-year, expedited waiver for router hardware on May 15.

Vantiva's desired changes for the BGW620 include the removal of the device's barometer and global navigation satellite system circuitry and related components, holding that it's been unable to re-source those elements in the face of global supply chain shortages. While removing those components would disable the BGW620's hardware-based positioning solution, Vantiva said the geolocation positioning functionality can be replicated via a software-based option. Vantiva's waiver for the BGW620 is good through Nov. 9, 2027.

Getting clearance for those changes should help AT&T to maintain its pace of fiber subscriber growth. AT&T added 442,000 fiber connections in Q3 (including adds originating from Lumen fiber markets that AT&T acquired in January), up from 288,000 a year earlier, for a total of 13.31 million.

In contrast, TP-Link's attempt to gain conditional approval for a pair of Wi-Fi routers is in a holding pattern amid resistance from the US government and a handful of individual states for its purported ties to China.

TP-Link, a company founded in China that is now headquartered in Irvine, California, claims the allegations are "baseless."

As a carrot to regulators reviewing its application, TP-Link has pledged to make a nearly $1 billion investment in US-based R&D and manufacturing capabilities. TP-Link currently manufactures all products it sells in the US in Vietnam.
