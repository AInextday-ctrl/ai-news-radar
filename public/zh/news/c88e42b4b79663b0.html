📰 深度报道原文实录

Bloomberg 权威实录

Despite all the uncertainty surrounding the artificial intelligence (AI) trade, Anthropic's initial public offering (IPO) is barreling ahead and looks like it will get out the door this week.

Citing anonymous sources, various media outlets have reported that Anthropic will price its IPO on Wednesday, Nov. 18, and then begin trading on the NASDAQ under the ticker ANT the following day.

Anthropic is reportedly set to price between $70 and $78 per share and issue 825 million shares. At the high end of that range, Anthropic would raise $64.35 billion and be valued around $1.4 trillion.

The Anthropic IPO is undeniably another huge milestone for the AI trade and another big test. I predict the IPO will be successful, but Anthropic will then trade similarly to Space Exploration Technologies Corp.

It's hard to believe that large language models (LLMs) really only went mainstream in 2022, because it feels like they've been around forever. That said, it also seems like the frontier models, such as Anthropic and OpenAI, have been gearing up for IPOs for years.

For a while, it seemed like the frontier models were cautious about giving investors a real look under the hood because of how much they were spending on compute to power their models. But Anthropic, which targets enterprise relationships, was believed to have a better financial profile than others like OpenAI.

Anthropic's prospectus showed strong revenue growth in the second quarter, with revenue of nearly $11.6 billion, up nearly 147% from the first quarter of the year. Furthermore, the company generated an operating profit of 974 million. However, in the third quarter, while revenue grew to nearly $17 billion, the growth rate was much lower than in the second quarter.

Additionally, investors may be worried about how much Anthropic is spending. In the first nine months of 2026, the company spent over $19 billion on research and development to continuously train its models, and $6.6 billion on sales and marketing.

On compute, Anthropic has entered into long-term agreements that some analysts estimate to be around $800 billion, although only about $266 billion of that is fixed or guaranteed, according to Evercore ISI analyst Mark Mahaney.

Anthropic has also had to navigate investor doubts about the returns on all this AI spending, significant competition for capital amid other hyperscalers and AI companies that have raised hundreds of billions this year, and now the Jensen Huang scandal at Nvidia.

When I say that Anthropic will trade like SpaceX, I mean I expect it to complete its IPO and maybe even pop out of the gate. However, I don't expect the stock to generate significant gains for shareholders in its first six months of trading, as has been the case for SpaceX.

SpaceX priced its IPO at $135 and went public on June 12. As of this writing on Nov. 13, the stock trades at roughly $139 per share.

I suspect Anthropic will be similar. Like SpaceX, the company is hitting the public markets with a high valuation that has been heavily run up in the private markets. If Anthropic prices at the high end of its range, the company would be valued at over 316 times revenue, assuming you annualize 2026 revenue. Obviously, the company is expected to grow revenue quickly, but even so, that's a high valuation that will leave little margin for error.

Furthermore, like SpaceX, Anthropic is selling only a very small number of shares to the public in its IPO, in the range of 5% to 7%, according to Vanguard. That means lots of shares from existing shareholders will likely flood the market over the next six months to one year following the IPO as lockup periods expire.

For instance, after Anthropic releases its 2026 full-year results, likely some time in early 2027, non-founders, officers, and certain employees will be able to sell up to 45% of their shares. Co-founders can sell up to 29% of their shares 180 days after the IPO or following the release of first-quarter earnings results in 2027, whichever occurs first.

This should keep shares under pressure. For all of these reasons, my recommendation to retail investors is to not jump the gun too early. I suspect investors will have time to digest a few quarters of earnings and still purchase shares of Anthropic at a similar price to its IPO.
