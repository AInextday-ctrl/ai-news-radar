Anthropic PBC shares erased much of their first-day jump on Friday, testing investors’ faith after the AI lab delivered the biggest initial public offering in history.

Shares in the company behind the Claude chatbot plunged as low as $87.26 each in early trading in its second day as a public company, trimming the gain from the IPO price of $83 to as little as 5.1%. The stock popped just after opening on Thursday, briefly lifting Anthropic’s market value above Amazon.com Inc.’s, before beginning its slide.

The drop came at the same time as the so-called over-allotment option, also known as a greenshoe, was exercised, allowing the underwriting banks to sell additional shares. The over-allotment lifted the total raised by Anthropic and the co-founders selling shares in the offering to $119.5 billion, with $93.5 billion in the IPO and $26 billion from the concurrent private placement.

The trading gives Anthropic a market value of about $1.5 trillion, ranking it among the 15 biggest publicly traded companies in the world. Stocks were roiled by a selloff that spread from Asia to the US, with the Nasdaq 100 Index falling 1.5% and the S&P 500 Index sinking 0.5%.

“There’s a buyers’ strike for Anthropic as well as the wider AI and tech trade,” according to Emmanuel Valavanis of Forte Securities.

“There are many clients who appear to have been left without allocations and, having been left out of the party, aren’t prepared to pick up the pieces from the hangover. With no skin in the game, and no index considerations for risk of relative performance, investors can sit on the sidelines and wait for longer term price discovery rather than risk getting caught out.”

Anthropic’s IPO made history not only because of its size, but also because the company is the first frontier AI model maker to go public. The trading represents a test of whether investors have appetite for companies pioneering the technology itself, rather than the tools to develop it such as chips and data centers. It also sets up a debut for rival OpenAI, which isn’t planning to go public before 2027.

The IPO delivered outsize returns to its backers and pushing up the combined wealth of its seven founders to $166 billion. The fundraise eclipsed the record set in June by SpaceX’s IPO, which ultimately totaled $86.2 billion. Goldman Sachs Group Inc., Morgan Stanley and 20 other banks earned a record $550 million in fees for their work on Anthropic’s IPO.

“We are impressed by the wealth creation this company is generating, in a matter of a few years and with reasonable free cash flow burn,” said Raphael Thuin, head of capital markets strategies at Tikehau Capital.

“To get exposed to AI though, we tend to favor more diversified businesses, and in any case we would like to see the post IPO volatility fade before engaging further.”

Anthropic won over investors with a focus on enterprise customers, propelling its run-rate revenue — a measure annualized from a shorter period — to $81 billion as of Oct. 31, up $10 billion from the end of September, its regulatory filings show.

Claude Code, the company’s coding agent for software developers, is widely adopted in the industry and has even been used by NASA engineers, the filing shows. Anthropic has more than 11,900 customers delivering $100,000 of revenue each on a trailing 12-month basis as of the end of September, up from about 1,500 in 2025.

Investors considering taking stakes may decide to wait out the market turmoil first. The Cboe Volatility Index, Wall Street’s so-called fear gauge, spiked to more than 29 on Friday morning, well above the level where companies typically consider the market welcoming to IPOs.

“We would look at buying in the open market if the stock get close or even below the IPO price,” said Olivier David, a fund manager at Vega IS.

Anthropic’s listing joins companies linked to AI that have pushed US IPO volume to an all-time high. Including Anthropic, first time stock sales raised $258 billion this year, beating 2021’s $195 billion haul, excluding financial vehicles such as blank-check firms, data compiled by Bloomberg show.
