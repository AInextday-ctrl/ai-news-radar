Dublin, Nov. 16, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- "Walk Assist Robot Market Research Report 2026-2031" has been added to ResearchAndMarkets.com's offering.

The global walk assist robot market is projected to grow at a CAGR of 15.01% from 2024 to 2031, supported by advances in rehabilitation robotics, increasing demand for mobility assistance, rising stroke incidence, and rapid growth in the elderly population. The report highlights expanding adoption across hospitals, rehabilitation centers, and home-care settings as healthcare providers seek technology-enabled solutions that improve patient outcomes and therapy efficiency.

Artificial intelligence and machine learning are reshaping walk-assist robots through real-time adaptation, personalized therapy, advanced motion control, and improved safety. These capabilities enable robotic systems to adjust assistance according to user movement and rehabilitation progress. Improvements in AI-based motion control, battery performance, and lightweight materials are also supporting gradual expansion from clinical environments into personal and home use.

Hybrid wearable walk assist robots are another important trend. By combining rigid structural components with soft, compliant materials, these systems balance strength, torque, comfort, and natural movement. Their versatility is expanding the potential of walk-assist technology for rehabilitation and everyday mobility.

The rising incidence of stroke remains a major growth driver for the walk assist robot market. Stroke survivors frequently require long-term rehabilitation for partial paralysis, muscle weakness, balance limitations, and gait impairment. This creates sustained demand for wearable exoskeletons and robot gait trainers across acute and post-acute care.

Population ageing is also increasing demand for robotic mobility solutions. Reduced muscle strength, joint degeneration, fall risk, arthritis, Parkinson's disease, and other age-related conditions are contributing to greater use of walk assist robots. These systems can support independence, improve quality of life, and reduce the physical demands placed on therapists and caregivers.

High purchase and operating costs remain a key market restraint. Powered medical exoskeletons and advanced treadmill-based gait trainers require significant capital investment, limiting adoption among smaller hospitals and rehabilitation clinics. Uncertain reimbursement policies can further delay purchasing decisions.

By product type, the market is segmented into wearable exoskeletons, robot gait trainers, and soft exosuits. Wearable exoskeletons hold the largest market share in 2025, driven by portability, clinical effectiveness, and applicability in both rehabilitation and personal mobility. These systems support repetitive, controlled gait therapy while reducing physical strain on therapists.

By application, post-surgical rehabilitation is expected to record the fastest CAGR of 15.18% during the forecast period. Walk-assist robots are increasingly used following hip replacement, knee replacement, spinal surgery, and other orthopaedic procedures requiring controlled mobility. Guided mobilization can support gait recovery while reducing muscle weakness, joint stiffness, and post-operative complications.

Rehabilitation centers represent the largest end-user segment. Demand is supported by the need for intensive, task-specific gait training among patients recovering from stroke, spinal cord injuries, and traumatic brain injuries. Robotic systems help facilities standardize therapy, monitor progress, manage larger patient volumes, and deliver longer treatment cycles.

North America accounts for more than 39% of the global walk assist robot market in 2025. Regional leadership is supported by strong healthcare spending, early adoption of medical technology, veterans' rehabilitation programs, and growing demand related to stroke, Parkinson's disease, spinal cord injuries, and age-related mobility limitations. The U.S. remains the largest North American market.

Europe is expanding steadily as demographic pressure, clinical demand, and established research-to-clinic pathways encourage adoption. Robot-assisted gait trainers and wearable exoskeletons are widely used for neurological rehabilitation following stroke and spinal injury.

The walk assist robot market is moderately competitive, combining established medical robotics companies with specialists in rehabilitation systems and wearable exoskeleton technology. Leading participants include:

Lifeward holds a leading market position, supported by the ReWalk exoskeleton system and its adoption by rehabilitation institutions. Ekso Bionics maintains a notable presence through FDA-cleared robotic exoskeleton systems for stroke and spinal cord rehabilitation, alongside partnerships with rehabilitation centers.

Other prominent companies include Bionik Laboratories, Rex Bionics, Wandercraft, Tyromotion GmbH, Toyota Motor Corp, ExoAtlet, Wearable Robotics, Gogoa Mobility, Panasonic Corp, B-Temia Inc., Bionic Power Inc., Angel Robotics, Marsi Bionics, Fourier Intelligence, FREE Bionics Inc, and Human in Motion.
