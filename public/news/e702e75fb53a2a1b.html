Wall Street Journal Full Report

Jensen Huang is willing to return to Nvidia as chief executive should he be asked by directors to do so, the former CEO of the tech giant said Tuesday morning in his first public remarks since stepping down.

“Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO,” Huang said in a short statement. “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”

Jensen Huang said on Tuesday that he will come back as chief executive of Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) if the company's board wants him. It is the first time he has spoken publicly about a return since he resigned on Thursday, November 12.

The statement is five sentences long. Huang opened with an apology. He said he did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia and that he created problems for the board that he deeply regrets.

“I own my mistakes completely,” he said.

He then said he has seen calls for his return from Nvidia's employees, investors, business partners and customers since he left. He did not ask for the job back. He left the decision to the directors.

As of Monday the board was not looking at a return. CNBC reported that directors are not considering bringing Huang back and are focused on their investigation of him, citing one person familiar with the matter. We covered that report this morning. Nvidia has not said on the record that a return is ruled out, and it had not responded publicly to Huang's statement when this story was published.

Huang resigned as president, chief executive and director after 33 years running the company he co-founded. CNBC reported that the board hired the law firm Wachtell Lipton to examine his conduct and whether it compromised national security or company secrets. That review is still open.

Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim chief executive. Harvey C. Jones became the first board chair in Nvidia's history. A board representative said on Friday that the company was still choosing a search firm for a permanent chief executive.

An online petition asking the board to reinstate Huang had about 180,000 signatures on Monday, according to CNBC. Elon Musk, Y Combinator chief executive Garry Tan and Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney are among the names on it. Two large shareholders, Brad Gerstner of Altimeter Capital and Gavin Baker of Atreides Management, sent the board a letter asking it to move slowly and keep every option open, including Huang's return. We reported on the letter and the employees who signed the petition on Saturday.

President Trump was asked on Monday about Huang coming back. “I would certainly not mind seeing that,” he said.

Huang is also due in Washington. Reuters reported on Tuesday that he will appear before congressional committees in December.

Nvidia closed at $156.14 on Monday, its lowest close since July 1, 2025 and 34.7% below the record close of $239.24 set on October 6. At 10:54 a.m. ET on Tuesday, minutes before the statement, the shares were at $150.58, down 3.6% for the day and 37.1% below the record. By 11:04 a.m., after the statement, they had risen 2.5% to $154.38, down 1.1% for the day and 35.5% below the record.

The timing is hard to miss. Nvidia reports third-quarter results after the market closes on Tuesday, and Kress will lead the call. Analysts expect earnings of $2.47 a share on revenue of $109.15 billion. Nvidia's own forecast was $108.0 billion.

Boards have reversed themselves before. Steve Jobs went back to Apple in 1997, and $10,000 of Apple stock bought then grew to $18.6 million, as we reported on Sunday. The record for chief executives who are pushed out is less kind, and we looked at a study of 187 forced exits the day after Huang resigned.

The next move belongs to Nvidia's directors. They can answer Huang before tonight's earnings call, during it, or wait for the Wachtell review to finish.
