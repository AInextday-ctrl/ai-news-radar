Oura to Buy AI Doctor Startup for $625 Million; Posts 153% Sales Jump

✍️ By Bloomberg News

Smart ring manufacturer Oura has agreed to acquire the AI-driven on-demand doctor service platform Counsel Health for $625 million. This acquisition aims to bolster Oura's medical diagnostic capabilities beyond mere data tracking, backed by a strong financial performance of 153% YoY growth in Q4 and a 92% increase in total 2026 revenue.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Oura and Counsel Health (M&A market): Acquisition for $625M and announcement of $1.75B annual revenue
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Transitioning from hardware-only to AI-assisted diagnostic medical services
  • [Key Metrics] Impressive financial growth with $1.75B revenue in 2026.
  • [Key Position & Outlook] Shifting from hardware maker to comprehensive digital health platform.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Bloomberg Full Report

Oura, the company behind the Oura smart ring, has agreed to buy Counsel Health, a startup that pairs a medical chatbot with licensed doctors, for $625 million in cash.The companies announced a definitive agreement on November 10. The price is subject to adjustments, and the deal is expected to close before the end of the calendar year if customary conditions are met, according to the announcement published by Counsel Health.The purchase changes what Oura sells.

Until now the company has mainly told its members what is happening in their bodies, using data on sleep, heart rate and temperature. With Counsel, it will be able to connect a member to a doctor who can give advice, write a prescription or order a test. That is a move from health tracking into health care.What Oura Is BuyingCounsel Health describes itself as an AI-native primary care company.

A user starts by chatting with its medical AI and can be handed to a physician when needed. Inside the Oura app, a member's ring data can be brought into that conversation.The two companies already work together. Counsel's service has been available to Oura members since June.

Buying the company brings the technology, the doctors and the roughly 70 employees in-house. Counsel's chief executive, Muthu Alagappan, will become Oura's chief clinical AI officer, TechCrunch reported, citing Bloomberg for the headcount.Counsel will not serve only Oura members.

It will keep working with its existing business customers, which include health plans, health systems and employers, and it plans to add more, according to Fierce Healthcare. The companies said they want to expand in three areas: preventive care, urgent needs, and long-term care for conditions tied to reproductive health, heart and metabolic health, and aging.The Price Compared With What Counsel RaisedThe price is large for a company of Counsel's age.

Counsel raised a $25 million Series A round about a year ago, led by Andreessen Horowitz and GV. PitchBook estimates the company raised $36 million in total, TechCrunch reported.On that estimate, Oura is paying about 17 times the money Counsel took in from investors. This is a comparison with funding, not with the company's valuation at its last round, which was not disclosed.

Still, it points to a strong return for early backers in a short time.Oura has not disclosed Counsel's revenue, so there is no way to judge the price against sales or profit. The companies also did not say how the purchase price might be adjusted. A reader should treat $625 million as the announced figure before those adjustments.Paying entirely in cash is also a choice worth noting.

Buyers that are private often use their own shares to pay for acquisitions. Oura is spending cash instead, which avoids putting a value on its stock at a time when its public listing is on hold.A Deal That Follows a Postponed IPOThe timing stands out. A few weeks before the announcement, Oura postponed a stock market listing that could have raised as much as $2.2 billion.

The company cited uncertainty in the market for new listings, according to TechCrunch.Oura had reached an $11 billion valuation after a $900 million funding round in October 2025. Companies in that position often avoid large purchases while they wait for a listing window. Oura went the other way.The company pointed to its own results as the reason it could do so.

Revenue for the quarter that ended September 30 was $533 million, up 153% from a year earlier, and it now has more than six million members. David Shuman, Oura's executive chairman, said in the announcement that those figures gave the company "the confidence to invest $625 million cash to acquire Counsel Health."Chief Executive Tom Hale said the combination lets members act on their health data, from quick questions and prescriptions to lab results and referrals.Readers should note what the revenue figure does and does not show.

It is a company-reported number for one quarter. Oura did not disclose its profit or cash balance in the announcement, so the effect of a $625 million cash payment on its finances cannot be measured from public information.What Could Go Right, and What Could Go WrongThe business case is simple to state.

A ring is sold once, and the membership fee is modest. Medical services could give Oura another source of recurring income and a reason for members to keep paying. Selling Counsel's service to employers and insurers could also bring in customers who do not own a ring at all.The risks are just as clear.

Medical care is regulated state by state in the United States, and a company that employs doctors takes on legal and safety duties that a device maker does not. Using AI to guide patients before a doctor steps in will draw attention from regulators and from physicians. Health data from a wearable is sensitive, and combining it with medical records raises privacy questions that the companies will need to answer in plain terms.There is also a question of focus.

Running a consumer hardware company and a medical practice at the same time is hard, and the deal arrives just as Oura is trying to show public market investors a clean growth story.The next confirmed milestone is the closing, which the companies expect before year-end. After that, the things to watch are how many members use the care service, whether Oura reports revenue from it separately, and whether the company sets a new date for its listing.Key Questions AnsweredQ: How much is Oura paying for Counsel Health?
