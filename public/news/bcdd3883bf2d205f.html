📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Financial Times Full Report

Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as its chief executive in a stunning reversal of its position on the disgraced co-founder, sending the AI giant’s shares soaring more than 30 per cent in pre-market trading.

In a statement on Monday, Nvidia said its board had voted unanimously to reinstate Huang “following a series of discussions and meetings . . . after which the board was satisfied with respect to Huang’s candor and ability to lead Nvidia”.

The Nvidia board said it received input from “senior levels” of the US government. “Based on that input, the board is confident that Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk,” the company added.

“The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” said Harvey Jones, chair of the board.

The company added that an investigation into Huang’s conduct was ongoing.

Until Monday, Nvidia’s stock had lost more than $2.5tn in value since its all-time high in October, before a series of revelations emerged about Huang’s affairs with at least one subordinate and with a client whom US intelligence suspects is a Chinese spy.

On Monday, Huang said he was “honoured to return to Nvidia”, which he co-founded 33 years ago. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” he said.

Despite the allegations against Huang and a series of state and federal investigations into Nvidia, US President Donald Trump has continued to stand by the AI company’s co-founder, saying he would like to see his friend and donor return as chief executive.

Trump said on Monday that Nvidia’s board made the “correct and proper decision” to return Huang to the role of CEO.

“The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” Trump wrote in a social media post following the board’s announcement.

He added that the US government “has no reason” to believe that Huang’s return poses any risk to national security, adding that it “does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct”.

Trump said he that he has “gotten to know Jensen” and described him as a “Great Patriot and National Treasure”, adding that Nvidia should continue its investigation of its compliance with US export controls.

On Sunday, US Treasury secretary Scott Bessent seemingly reversed his position on Huang’s reappointment, refusing to rule out such a move after previously calling on the company’s board to implement a “dramatic shake-up” and look “outside” for new leadership.

The abrupt turnaround comes less than a week after Jones had told investors on Nvidia’s quarterly earnings call that the board had “unanimously accepted [Huang’s] resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”.

Several Silicon Valley investors and founders, including SpaceX and Tesla chief Elon Musk, investor Gavin Baker and former Uber chief Travis Kalanick, have pushed for Huang’s reinstatement. A petition to “bring Jensen back” has received more than 200,000 signatures, including from some Nvidia staff.

Nvidia shares opened 34 per cent higher on Monday morning, which, if sustained, would be the stock’s biggest one-day jump since March 2000.

Such a move would add about $1tn in market capitalisation, a record increase for any company, and follows a 14 per cent drop on Friday that wiped $492bn from the company’s valuation.

Huang thanked Nvidia’s board and Colette Kress, its finance chief who became interim CEO, as well as “employees, shareholders, leaders across the industry and partners around the world for their extraordinary support”.

“Above all, I am grateful for the love and support of my family. I can’t wait to get back to work,” he said.
