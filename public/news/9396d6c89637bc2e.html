Lovable Wants You to Vibe Code Your Dream Job

✍️ By Reece Rogers

Lovable CEO Anton Osika and CTO Fabian Hedin announced a strategic pivot for their $13B-valued startup, evolving their 'vibe coding' platform into an automated business management engine. With 80% of users seeking financial gain but only 35% currently monetizing, the company aims to bridge this gap by integrating automated business strategy, web development, and customer retention tools.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Anton Osika (CEO), Fabian Hedin (CTO), and Lovable: Expanding from a code-generation platform to a full-stack automated business operation engine
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Addressing the gap between 80% user demand for monetization and only 35% actual revenue generation
  • [Key Metrics] Monetization challenge: Bridging the gap where 80% of users seek profit but only 35% succeed.
  • [Key Position & Outlook] Technical roadmap: Implementing proactive business dashboards, marketing automation, and CRM integration.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Wired AI Full Report

I squint my eyes in confusion when Anton Osika asks if I like cakes. The CEO of Lovable was in the middle of explaining how his AI startup, flush with investment cash and recently valued at over $13 billion, is ready to shift its focus after two years. Why does the vibe-coding king care if I like sweet treats? He thinks that if I do, I might be able to vibe code a living out of that.

The generative AI revolution completely flipped coding. Rather than a niche skill set where humans had to learn how to talk like computers, software development could be done by computers generating code in response to natural-language requests from humans.

Osika was early to this shift, launching a viral open-source tool in 2023, a precursor to Lovable. His mission? “Reduce barriers to build.” That initial spark ignited what is now one of Europe’s most dynamic startups.

Tech insiders talked nonstop last year about the transformative power of “vibe coding” that could empower anyone to build their own software. Parents could whip up custom apps to help track demanding club sports schedules. Picky eaters could create dynamic meal plans that adapt to whatever’s in their fridge. Polyamorous people could build software to manage their relationship hierarchies. Whatever random, niche software you can imagine, you can build it.

But Osika isn’t satisfied with helping millions of people vibe code apps for their everyday life—he also wants Lovable users to make more cash. The startup claims that around 80 percent of users arrive at the platform with a financial incentive, like building customer-facing platforms for online sales. That said, Lovable also reports that only 35 percent currently earn revenue with their projects.

That brings us back to the cake question. Lovable’s plan for next year is to help you run a small business based on your passions, like opening a cake shop. Fabian Hedin, Lovable’s other cofounder and chief technology officer, says the startup is building automations to help with everything from initial business strategy and website creation to long-term customer retention and marketing plans.

For example, a cake shop owner receives an AI-generated morning briefing through a chatbot-style, Lovable dashboard. “You set some goals, and then the AI is proactive in helping you reach those goals,” Hedin says. This briefing analyzes the number of cake boxes sold through a holiday promotion, customer emails it automatically answered, and reminders to make financial deposits on time.

Lovable has been a boon for savvy social media content creators, who can leverage their platforms better through vibe-coded sites. “People who have audiences themselves are building larger businesses around that,” Osika says. He recently went to a dinner in New York with creators, one of whom uses Lovable to help sell their book. Others have used it to help hawk merch.

Lovable earning the trust of burgeoning entrepreneurs is critical to the success of this new endeavor, though it may be an uphill battle. Vibe-coded projects were notorious for not necessarily being secure and sometimes exposing user data.

The startup was one of many vibe-coded services that came under fire this year for facilitating user-generated apps that exposed data on the open web. Earlier this month, Lovable revamped its security approach as part of its effort to make sites generated through its platform more secure by default.

“The moment real people use what you build, security is the product,” Igor Andriushchenko, Lovable’s head of security, tells WIRED. “That's why it's one of the areas we continue investing in the most and why we’ve built it directly into the platform experience.”

Osika will see Lovable as successful next year if he can help a new wave of entrepreneurs start sustainable businesses. Why just vibe code an app when you can vibe code a living?

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

Lovable's pivot signifies a shift from lowering coding barriers to creating operational business loops, capturing value by integrating AI-driven business logic. By anchoring low-code development to operational dashboards, the firm aims to capture the SMB market segment and disrupt incumbent SaaS providers.
