Singapore-Based DayOne Is Said to Pause US IPO Plans on Data Center, Market Concerns

✍️ By Bloomberg News

Singapore-based DayOne Data Centers has shelved its planned US IPO amid cooling market valuations for AI infrastructure and intensifying regulatory scrutiny. Despite efforts to navigate the AI compute demand surge, the company faces significant headwinds from investor skepticism regarding data center asset sustainability and strict US regulations on the export of advanced AI hardware to entities with China-linked operations.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Bloomberg Full Report

SINGAPORE – DayOne Data Centers has put plans for its multibillion-dollar US initial public offering on hold, sources familiar with the matter said, as investors question the sustainability of frothy valuations for digital infrastructure supporting the artificial intelligence boom.

But it started to reassess options including a delay or a smaller listing as volatility increased around data centres and AI stocks.

Singapore-based DayOne may still pursue an IPO in 2027 if the situation and market sentiment improves, the sources said, asking not to be identified because the information is not public.

The company filed for the IPO in October and has been working with Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup and BNP Paribas on the listing.

Deliberations are ongoing and no final decisions on the IPO have been made, the sources said.

A representative for DayOne declined to comment.

Doubts about the potential payoff from massive investments into AI have fuelled volatility in global markets.

The Nasdaq 100 has dropped more than 6% in November, heading for its worst November performance since 2008.

Still, Anthropic staged a record-breaking US$107.3 billion IPO and private placement and rose 17% on its trading debut on Nov 19, becoming the world’s seventh-most valuable company.

Other data centre-related companies have either pulled or delayed their IPO plans amid investor concerns in the sector.

In October, Firmus Grid abandoned its attempt at one of Australia’s biggest-ever initial public offerings, while Blackstone-backed AirTrunk is delaying a listing of a real estate investment trust in Singapore until 2027.

The US in November put interim rules on those four countries to restrict China’s access to advanced AI chips and servers, telling data centre operators they will need a license to purchase American tech hardware.

DayOne’s backers include Coatue, Hillhouse, GDS Holdings, SoftBank Vision Fund and Citadel’s Ken Griffin. BLOOMBERG

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

This case reflects a correction in capital markets regarding the 'blind expansion' phase of AI compute infrastructure, where data center operators are caught between mounting regulatory compliance risks and unsustainable capital expenditure. With US export controls on AI hardware becoming a normalized barrier, firms with cross-border compute leasing structures are facing severe valuation discounts and financing bottlenecks.
