Sources: Samsung and SK Hynix agree to adopt parts of YMTC's "Xtacking" tech for 3D NAND flash via cross-licensing deals, signaling YMTC's rapid tech progress (Nikkei Asia)

✍️ By Nikkei Asia

Global memory leaders Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have integrated Yangtze Memory Technologies Co.'s (YMTC) Xtacking architecture via cross-licensing deals, utilizing independent CMOS and cell wafer bonding to optimize 3D NAND performance. This adoption confirms YMTC’s rapid technical maturation and patent portfolio depth, marking a significant milestone in China's semiconductor industrial self-sufficiency.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Nikkei Asia Full Report

TAIPEI/PALO ALTO/SEOUL -- The world's top memory chipmakers, South Korea's Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, are adopting chip-stacking technology first developed by Yangtze Memory Technologies Co., signaling how one of China's national champions has rapidly caught up with its larger semiconductor rivals and has built a substantial intellectual property portfolio.

Samsung and SK Hynix have been exploring stacking technologies for 3D NAND flash memory and, through cross-licensing deals, agreed they could adopt parts of related tech in Xtacking— a stacking technology developed by YMTC — three people briefed on the matter told Nikkei Asia.

Their move recognizes a significant breakthrough by YMTC, founded just a decade ago and now seen as playing a key role in helping China overcome its reliance on foreign chipmakers as the U.S. restricts exports of advanced products.

In Xtacking, first unveiled in 2018, memory cells and the "CMOS" circuits that control them are manufactured separately on two wafers before being bonded and connected together to form a 3D NAND flash memory chip.

This approach means each part can be produced and optimized independently, with the CMOS circuits manufactured using methods similar to those used for processor chips. YMTC has said it began mass-producing 3D NAND flash chips using Xtacking technology in 2019.

The Chinese company has filed more than 4,000 patent applications in the U.S. since 2018, with most covering 3D memory fabrication, semiconductor manufacturing processes and advanced chip packaging technologies, according to public records from the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO). YMTC says it has filed more than 12,000 patents globally and has more than 8,000 engineers.

NAND flash memory is widely used in consumer electronics, computers and storage systems. With high storage capacity in small chips, it suits electronic devices like smartphones, laptops and game consoles that require fast, durable memory.

YMTC did not respond to requests for comment, and SK Hynix declined to comment. Samsung declined to comment on cross-licensing agreements, but told Nikkei Asia that bonding technology offered many advantages over conventional NAND flash manufacturing.

It “reduces structural limitations that may arise during stacking, enabling higher memory density” and “enables performance improvements and lower power consumption through the adoption of processes optimized for each component,” it said. The company added that “if applied to a wider range of products, it could enable large storage capacities even in thinner and lighter devices.” The world's top memory chipmaker said its latest V10 BV-NAND technology, unveiled last year, uses advanced wafer bonding to improve chip performance and manufacturing efficiency.

According to multiple industry experts, YMTC's approach gives chipmakers greater manufacturing flexibility by reducing the complexity of building both memory cells and logic circuits on a single wafer, and allowing them to outsource.

"Memory chipmakers can manufacture memory arrays in-house while outsourcing the logic portion of the chips to contract chipmaking partners," said Claire Wen, a senior analyst at the Omdia research firm. "This gives them greater flexibility in organizing production and allows them to tap more advanced logic chipmaking technologies without having to invest in all the necessary manufacturing capabilities themselves."

In the past, leading chipmakers saw little need to stack and bond two wafers together. But that thinking has gradually shifted, with such technologies increasingly seen as a key part of the future of NAND flash memory manufacturing, three people familiar with the matter told Nikkei Asia.

"Xtacking technologies are actually not that hard. The main reason the top chipmakers do cross-licensing with YMTC is just to avoid any legal disputes and because China's YMTC already filed a lot of patents around its stacking technologies," a chip industry executive told Nikkei Asia. "In a way, YMTC was fortunate to have adopted this technology, which has made it less vulnerable to U.S. export controls on advanced chipmaking equipment. Now, more leading chipmakers are moving toward similar approaches, with some even choosing to license parts of the technology from YMTC."

A manager at a component supplier to YMTC and Micron told Nikkei Asia that the Chinese memory chipmaker was smart to build a patent wall around its Xtacking technologies, even though manufacturing did not require advanced chipmaking equipment.

"It's not like Xtacking technologies are a must-go technological roadmap; however, if a memory chipmaker would like to take this approach, it would be hard to bypass YMTC's patents," said the source, who has direct knowledge of the cross-licensing matter.

The main global players in NAND are Samsung, SK Hynix, Japan's Kioxia, SanDisk and Micron in the U.S., and YMTC. It held a 14% market share in the April-June quarter, up from 11% a year earlier, and trailed only Samsung (28%) and SK Hynix (19%), while nearly matching Micron (15%) and Kioxia (14%), according to Counterpoint Research.

Longtime partners Kioxia and SanDisk unveiled their "CMOS directly Bonded to Array" (CBA) technology in 2023, a concept similar to YMTC's approach.

SanDisk chief technology officer Alper Ilkbahar told Nikkei Asia that rather than building all the structures on a single wafer, fabricating the two parts separately means each wafer can be optimized for its specific functions before being bonded, improving overall performance.

"By using CBA, we are able to really drive and optimize the memory and then the circuits [performance] independently," Ilkbahar said. "We could get very reliable, high-performance memory array, and we could get a very high-performance CMOS [logic chips]." He said he believed all of its rivals were looking into hybrid bonding technologies.

YMTC has become more aggressive in asserting its intellectual property rights in recent years, filing lawsuits against Micron for allegedly violating its patents involving NAND flash memory and DRAM technologies in both the U.S. and Europe. The American memory chipmaker has challenged the validity of YMTC's claims.

The Chinese company sued Micron in California over eight 3D NAND patents in 2023, and by July this year, it had filed U.S. suits covering 27 patents, along with 295 patent claims in courts in China, the U.S., the U.K., Germany and elsewhere in Europe against its U.S. rival.On Sept.18, the Munich I Regional Court found Micron infringed two YMTC German utility models and ordered an injunction covering related product sales in Germany. It was YMTC's first substantive injunction win, although Micron is appealing. Also in September, Micron sued YMTC in its home state of Idaho, alleging the Chinese company poached Micron engineers and turned their know-how into patents later used against Micron. Micron will start using its own wafer-on-wafer bonding technology in its next-generation 3D NAND flash memory G11, scheduled to enter mass production in the second half of 2027.In an interview with Nikkei Asia in late October, Scott DeBoer, Micron's president and chief technology officer, said, "Every company will implement and has had the opportunity to choose to implement wafer bonding on NAND for the past 10 years; Micron will implement wafer bonding at the time when it actually provides a strategic advantage. With growing demand to meet AI workloads, wafer bonding would provide that advantage for G11 products"

YMTC filed for an initial public offering in August through its parent holding company, CCSH Corp. It is also building new facilities in the Chinese city of Wuhan, with plans to expand NAND flash memory production and also enter the market for dynamic random access memory (DRAM) chips, which are used in a wide range of electronic devices.

Nikkei Asia previously reported that YMTC was embarking on its most aggressive expansion to date, alongside fellow Chinese memory chipmaker CXMT. YMTC has also been among the most aggressive Chinese chipmakers in localizing procurement of its semiconductor equipment, seeking to reduce its reliance on U.S. technology and minimize the risk of production disruptions caused by Washington's export restrictions, Nikkei first reported.

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

The industry's embrace of Xtacking highlights that in the post-Moore era, storage innovation has shifted from pure scaling to heterogeneous integration. While this collaboration aids incumbents in optimizing performance, it underscores that YMTC’s IP moat is now structurally essential, signalling a shift toward technical standard integration despite overarching geopolitical constraints.
