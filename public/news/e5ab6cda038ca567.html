‘The king is back!’ Huang’s return to Nvidia is greeted with relief in Asia.

✍️ By Sammy Westfall, Lyric Li, Huiyee Chiew, and Joshua Yang

The reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO triggered a strong rally in Asian markets, particularly in Taiwan, Japan, and South Korea. Despite prior controversy surrounding his personal life and geopolitical ties, the regional tech ecosystem views his return as a critical signal for the continued stability of the global AI supply chain.

  • [Core Event] Jensen Huang (Nvidia CEO), Asian tech investors and associations: Market rally and positive reception of reinstatement
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Huang is viewed as the linchpin of the global AI supply chain
  • [Market & Financial Detail] Strong market rally across Asia following Huang's return.
  • [Key Position & Outlook] Focus on supply certainty over compliance concerns.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Washington Post Full Report

As Asian stocks rallied over the decision by Nvidia’s board to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, nowhere were the celebrations more ebullient than in China and Taiwan, where tech leaders, investors and even street market vendors hailed the AI executive as a hometown hero on whom the success of the tech industry — and global markets — relies.

Huang’s reinstatement, under direct pressure from President Donald Trump, seemed to avert a short-term diplomatic crisis — Beijing had forcefully denied allegations that his paramour Alice Huang was a Chinese operative — but left open serious questions about the White House’s handling of serious espionage concerns and potential investigations by the Democratic Congress that is set to take office in January.

News of Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy seemed to do little to dim the adoration many across Asia had for him.

“The king is back!” read one headline by China’s state-affiliated financial news outlet Star Market Daily.

At least three Huang-related hashtags topped trends on the Chinese social media platform Weibo on Tuesday, as Japan’s Nikkei rose nearly 4 percent, Korea’s Kospi index gained more than 6 percent and Taiwan’s main stock market index gained nearly 9 percent.

“I’m so happy that [Huang] is making a comeback,” Duan Yongping, a Chinese American billionaire entrepreneur and value investor nicknamed the “Chinese Buffett,” posted on Xueqiu, a major Chinese investment social network and wealth management platform.

In the nearly two-week period of upheaval — from Huang’s resignation Nov. 12 to the board’s announcement Monday — people in Asia sent wishes for his return.

The message was particularly clear in Taiwan, where Huang was born before moving to the United States at age 9. Taipei 101, Taiwan’s tallest building, lit up with messages of support: “Taiwan ♡ Jensen,” “Family ♡ Friendship,” and “Thrive Together.” Representatives of Taiwan’s technology industry associations organized and funded the display.

At a temple to the matchmaker god Yue Lao in Taiwan’s Tainan, lanterns were lit for Huang to “cut off bad romantic relationships,” according to a local news station. The reason for this: “to save Taiwan’s stock market supply chain.”

On the other side of the world, Silicon Valley tech leaders including Elon Musk had also been backing Huang as the scandal unfolded. But his biggest cheerleader, it seemed, was Trump, who said he personally orchestrated the effort to reinstate Huang over the past several days.

Nvidia said in announcing Huang’s reinstatement that its board received input from “senior levels of the United States government.”

“The Trump administration’s intervention mattered enormously. If anything, it shows just how important Nvidia, and Huang at the center of it, have become to America’s AI ambitions,” said Lizzi C. Lee, a fellow on the Chinese economy at the Asia Society Policy Institute’s Center for China Analysis.

Huang’s return is good news for China’s AI industry, given its dependence on Nvidia and the importance of American technology to Chinese models, Lee said. Beijing’s leadership may also see the reinstatement “as reassurance that practical and commercial interests still carry the day in Washington, particularly with Trump.”

Others on Chinese social media met Huang’s reinstatement with celebratory humor.

One Shanghai-based user of China’s Jike social networking site wrote that “I wouldn’t be surprised if Jensen Huang announced a comeback in Avengers 5.”

“He took 3 days off to play [‘Grand Theft Auto VI’] and beat the game. Time for him to go back to work,” a commentator from Sichuan wrote on Weibo.

Many compared the fall and rise of Huang to the typical melodramatic underdog storylines of male leads in China’s viral short-form drama platform Hongguo. “I don’t watch Hongguo, but I bet even Hongguo can’t write a story as wild as his,” wrote a commentator from Beijing.

For many people in China, Huang’s return is seen “as a victory against those seeking to smear China,” said Zichen Wang, deputy secretary general of the Center for China and Globalization, a Beijing think tank. Huang had fought hard in Washington to secure the company’s right to sell its powerful artificial intelligence chips there, and his dismissal was widely perceived as politically motivated and “closely tied” to that advocacy, he said.

Huang’s reinstatement appears to have “galvanized” scrutiny of Nvidia among Democrats as they prepare to take control of Congress, Wang said. “Although their motivations may have more to do with domestic partisan battles, the practical consequence could be intensified congressional scrutiny of semiconductor exports, Nvidia’s operations and Huang himself,” he said.

The scandal showed that “even someone as formidable as Jensen Huang is still not trusted in America,” said Zhao Minghao, professor and deputy director of the Center for American Studies at Fudan University in Shanghai. “Chinese tech elites in America are merely needed, but not trusted.”

Trump’s apparent willingness to look past the allegations that Huang and Nvidia violated U.S. export controls on China stands in contrast to the tough line that the first Trump administration took when the Chinese firm Huawei Technologies was accused of breaching sanctions on Iran, as well as committing bank and wire fraud.

Trump on CNBC on Monday called Huang “a national treasure” and brought up the “vast amount of knowledge in his brain.”

“Nobody’s ever come back like this. … I’ve seen some unbelievably talented CEOs get thrown out because they were kissing somebody inappropriately at a game … and I’ve seen the company go through hell for 10 years, 15. They never got somebody back who was this good,” Trump said.

China is closely watching how Trump treats “genius entrepreneurs,” Zhao said. From Beijing’s perspective, what it saw as an attack on Huang was a combination of “anti-China, anti-Trump, especially Democratic, and anti-AI/Big Tech forces.”

Huang’s reinstatement means that the “U.S.-China tech rivalry will probably enter a more precise, more targeted phase,” he said.

“China won’t harbor any illusions, and won’t feel optimistic just because Jensen Huang has returned to Nvidia,” Zhao said. “Democrats may target China as a tool for striking at Trump. This is what Beijing is most worried about right now.”
