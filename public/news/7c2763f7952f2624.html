To address global safety concerns, the company proactively filtered training data to strip offensive cyber capabilities and implemented specific post-training mitigations against CBRN risks.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

South China Morning Post Full Report

Alibaba Group Holding has launched its most advanced artificial intelligence model to date, Qwen4-Max, with strict cybersecurity guardrails, as the tech giant balances safety with a continued commitment to open sourcing amid growing global concerns over AI-enabled cyberattacks.

The release highlights a growing focus on safety in China’s AI industry as Alibaba said it intentionally filtered the model’s training data to remove offensive cyberattack capabilities while retaining capabilities useful for cyber defence such as vulnerability detection.

The Hangzhou-based company also introduced dedicated data sets and post-training enhancements to mitigate chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRN) risks, it said on Monday.

Alibaba also highlighted the model’s improvements in scientific research, including the completion of a continuous 48-hour run to discover an experimentally validated coolant molecule for next-generation semiconductor chips, as well as autonomous research on Alzheimer’s disease.

There is heated debate about whether open-weight models, which are favoured by Chinese companies, could heighten CBRN risks more than the closed-source models championed by their US rivals such as OpenAI and Anthropic.

Some experts have warned that AI systems could eventually be powerful enough to enable malicious actors or states to develop CBRN weapons.

While Qwen4-Max is a closed model, Alibaba reaffirmed its open-source strategy by noting that smaller-scale, open-weight versions of the Qwen4 series would be released to global developers soon.

Alibaba’s Max series of models had always been closed-source until Qwen 3.8, which was made available as open source in August. The Max series is also the company’s largest model by parameter size. However, the company highlighted its achievements in making its latest model more cost-efficient when served to users.

Qwen4-Max boasts 2.4 trillion parameters, the same size as its previous Qwen3.8-Max, though it activates only 71 billion parameters per token – 25 per cent less than its predecessor.

That allows it to deliver superior reasoning capabilities while bringing down inference costs, cutting the average cost per task by 60 per cent, according to Alibaba, citing an internal benchmark called Work360, which evaluates model performance on various work tasks.

The model is currently available via QwenCloud, with input pricing set at 7.5 US cents per 1 million tokens.

Alibaba’s AI efforts have focused heavily on capturing the enterprise AI market and cultivating an open-source community to outpace local rivals like DeepSeek and Z.ai, as well as US competitors.

Liu Dayiheng, head of Alibaba’s Qwen large language model team, presented a road map outlining plans for the Qwen 4, Qwen 4.5 and Qwen 5 series in September, while stressing the team’s commitment to the open-source community earlier this month at the World Internet Conference in Wuzhen.

The company has said that its next-generation model, Qwen 5, would be scaled up to between 5 trillion and 10 trillion parameters.
