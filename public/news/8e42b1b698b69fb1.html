📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

OpenAI Full Report

The new partnership will help young people build AI skills for the future workforce, prioritizing those not in education or employment.

The King’s Trust⁠(opens in a new window) has announced a major partnership with OpenAI designed to help up to 25,000 young people build AI skills for work, prioritizing those not in education, employment or training.

OpenAI is making a £1m donation to the youth charity to support this work.

This is part of a wider program of nationally recognized AI qualifications that The Trust will launch in the coming months, designed to help young people stay ahead of a changing labor market.

The announcement comes as The King’s Trust marks its 50th year and looks to the future, helping ensure young people are supported to succeed in an increasingly AI-enabled world.

The King’s Trust and OpenAI have launched a £1 million partnership to help young people across the UK build AI skills for work, prioritizing those who are not in education, employment or training (NEET). The program aims to support up to 25,000 young people over the next 18 months, helping them develop practical skills and confidence to move toward education and employment in an AI-enabled economy.

The program will help address a gap in young people’s experience of AI while responding to their appetite to learn. The Open University’s Business Barometer 2026⁠(opens in a new window) found that 68% of 16–24-year-olds surveyed who were not in education, employment or training would be willing to train or upskill to improve their job prospects.

The partnership will help prepare young people for the opportunities and challenges of a rapidly changing world of work by combining opportunities to learn AI skills with recognized qualifications and practical experience. It forms part of a wider program of nationally recognized AI qualifications being developed by The King’s Trust, designed to equip young people with the skills employers need as AI transforms the world of work.

Working with employers, AI specialists and young people, The King’s Trust is exploring the skills young people will need in a world of work where AI is becoming part of everyday life, and how these can complement the employability skills already embedded across its programs.

Building on a 50-year legacy of supporting more than 1.3 million young people, The King’s Trust wants to ensure the next generation gains the skills and confidence needed to thrive in an AI-enabled economy.

Ahead of the final report of the independent Young People and Work review, the Rt Hon Alan Milburn welcomed the partnership, reflecting on the shared ambition to improve young people’s opportunities to learn and earn.

For OpenAI, the partnership forms part of a broader commitment to helping young people develop the skills and confidence to benefit from AI. It builds on wider efforts to make practical AI learning more accessible, including through OpenAI Academy, which provides free learning resources for students, job-seekers and other learners.
