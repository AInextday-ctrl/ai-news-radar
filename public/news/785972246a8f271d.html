US judge clears MIT-educated brothers of $25 million cryptocurrency heist charges

✍️ By Nate Raymond/Reuters

A US federal judge in Manhattan has acquitted two MIT-educated brothers accused of stealing $25 million in cryptocurrency in 12 seconds, ruling prosecutors failed to prove that exploiting an MEV-Boost software bug on Ethereum constituted wire fraud without material misrepresentations.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Reuters Full Report

U.S. District Judge Jessica Clarke granted a judgment of acquittal for Anton Peraire-Bueno and James Peraire-Bueno on November 16, 2026, ending a prosecution that tested whether exploiting vulnerabilities in decentralized blockchain trading infrastructure could constitute criminal fraud.

The decision follows a November 2025 mistrial, when jurors could not reach a unanimous verdict after nearly four weeks of testimony. Prosecutors subsequently sought another trial, but Clarke concluded that the evidence presented could not legally support convictions for wire fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud or conspiracy to commit money laundering.

The central issue was whether the brothers made materially false representations when executing a trading strategy that exploited Ethereum‘s transaction-ordering infrastructure.

Prosecutors alleged that the brothers manipulated the system used to construct and validate Ethereum transactions, allowing them to intercept private trading information and replace transactions submitted by automated trading bots.

Clarke concluded that the government had not demonstrated that the brothers made a legally actionable misrepresentation during the operation.

The judge examined whether registering a validator, requesting transaction blocks, submitting invalid block information or proposing modified transactions constituted deceptive representations. She found that none of those actions established the necessary element of wire fraud.

The ruling also rejected the argument that violating the intended design of the MEV-Boost system was sufficient to establish criminal deception.

The judge distinguished between potentially objectionable trading behavior and conduct prohibited by the specific criminal statutes under which the brothers were charged.

Because the money laundering conspiracy charge depended on the proceeds of the alleged wire fraud, the failure of the underlying fraud case also required acquittal on that charge.

As FinanceFeeds previously reported, prosecutors had challenged efforts by the DeFi Education Fund to submit arguments concerning the wider implications of criminalizing blockchain trading strategies.

The ruling limits the government’s ability to prosecute this particular blockchain exploit under traditional wire fraud provisions without evidence of material deception. However, the decision does not establish that exploiting software vulnerabilities is universally lawful or prevent enforcement under other applicable statutes.

The case originated from an April 2023 operation involving maximal extractable value, or MEV, a mechanism through which traders and validators can profit from the ordering of blockchain transactions.

Ethereum validators are responsible for proposing transaction blocks, while specialized builders assemble transactions to maximize potential returns. MEV-Boost, open-source software introduced by Flashbots, allows validators to outsource block construction through intermediaries known as relays.

Prosecutors alleged that the Peraire-Bueno brothers identified a vulnerability in this arrangement and developed a strategy called Omakase to target automated traders conducting sandwich transactions.

Sandwich trading involves placing transactions immediately before and after another trader’s order to profit from changes in the asset’s price.

According to the trial evidence, the brothers created transactions designed to attract these automated strategies. They then exploited a relay vulnerability to access transaction information that the bots expected to remain private before submitting replacement transactions.

The operation generated approximately $25 million in profits within a single Ethereum block.

The brothers were indicted in May 2024. Prosecutors maintained that the operation amounted to fraud, while defense lawyers argued that their clients had executed a trading strategy without making false representations or violating applicable trading rules.

Following the deadlocked jury, prosecutors requested a retrial for early 2026. The defense instead pursued a judgment of acquittal, which Clarke ultimately granted.

The case demonstrates that technical vulnerabilities in transaction-ordering systems can expose sophisticated trading operations to substantial losses. For institutional DeFi participants, protections around private transaction information and relay infrastructure remain important regardless of whether a particular exploit meets the legal definition of fraud.

The decision adds to a series of federal court rulings examining whether unconventional decentralized trading strategies can be prosecuted under existing financial fraud statutes.

In May 2025, another Manhattan federal judge overturned fraud and market manipulation convictions against Mango Markets trader Avraham Eisenberg, whose trading strategy extracted approximately $110 million from the Solana-based decentralized finance platform.

That ruling involved separate questions concerning jurisdiction, market rules and whether the defendant’s transactions amounted to fraudulent representations.

Clarke’s decision likewise focused on the legal requirements for proving deception rather than determining whether the trading strategy was commercially acceptable.

The similarities do not make the cases interchangeable. Both decisions arose from their particular facts, charges and trial records, and neither creates a general exemption for blockchain manipulation.

The November 16 judgment closes the criminal case against the Peraire-Bueno brothers in the Southern District of New York, eliminating the retrial prosecutors had previously sought.

The decision may also inform future arguments about how fraud statutes apply to automated trading, transaction ordering and decentralized market infrastructure.

For prosecutors, the ruling demonstrates the importance of identifying specific deceptive conduct rather than relying solely on evidence that a trading strategy violated a system’s expected operation.

For blockchain developers, exchanges and institutional trading firms, the distinction between software specifications, enforceable contractual obligations and criminal law remains central to assessing operational and legal risk.

The acquittal removes immediate criminal exposure for the two defendants but leaves broader questions about MEV-related market conduct unresolved. Institutional participants still need to assess smart-contract security, counterparty exposure and the legal treatment of their trading activities independently of this ruling.

This landmark decision by Judge Jessica Clarke establishes an essential boundary for crypto law: exploiting an open-source smart contract or MEV protocol vulnerability without false statements does not constitute criminal wire fraud under US federal law. It highlights the challenge prosecutors face applying traditional fraud statutes to decentralized on-chain arbitrage.
