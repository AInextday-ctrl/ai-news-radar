📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Bloomberg Full Report

The United States has proposed tighter rules for companies sponsoring foreign workers for permanent residency, a move that could make the green card process more demanding for employers hiring overseas talent.

The US Department of Labor has unveiled changes to the Permanent Labor Certification programme, commonly known as PERM, which is an important step in securing employment-based green cards.

The programme requires companies to demonstrate that qualified American workers are not available for the positions they intend to fill with foreign employees.

The proposed changes seek to give American workers priority in recruitment, strengthen protections for employees affected by layoffs and make it harder for companies to bypass existing hiring requirements.

The move could have implications for technology companies and other employers that rely on foreign professionals to fill specialised positions.

Under the proposed changes, companies would have to follow stricter recruitment standards before sponsoring foreign workers for permanent residency.

Employers would be required to advertise vacancies for at least 90 calendar days and show that they made genuine efforts to find qualified American candidates.

They would also have to use the same recruitment channels and methods when advertising positions.

The rules would also prevent employers from designing job requirements or opportunities in a way that favours a particular foreign candidate over qualified American applicants.

US Labour Secretary Keith Sonderling said the proposed changes would ensure that American workers receive priority for high-skilled and well-paid jobs.

One of the major aspects of the proposal concerns American employees who lose their jobs while companies continue seeking foreign workers.

Under the proposed rules, employers would have to continue considering qualified American workers who have been laid off during the labour certification and immigrant petition processes.

Companies that have laid off American employees within the previous two years could also be barred from seeking permanent labour certification for the same or similar positions under certain circumstances.

The department said the changes would strengthen safeguards for workers affected by job cuts, including those employed through staffing companies.

The proposal would also remove certain administrative exemptions that have allowed employers to bypass the usual labour market test for specific occupations.

This would mean companies recruiting physical therapists, nurses and individuals with exceptional abilities in the sciences or arts would also have to demonstrate that they considered qualified American workers before hiring foreign professionals.

The Labour Department described the proposal as the first comprehensive overhaul of PERM regulations in nearly two decades. It said the changes were aimed at modernising the programme, improving record-keeping and ensuring that American workers receive fair consideration.

The proposal is not yet a final rule. If implemented, it could change how companies recruit foreign professionals and sponsor them for employment-based green cards.
