Anthropic Is About to Become the World’s Most Important AI Stock

✍️ By Bloomberg News

AI startup Anthropic has officially initiated its IPO process, aiming to raise $90.4 billion through a combination of an initial public offering and a concurrent private placement. The deal is currently oversubscribed by 7 times, with the company targeting a fully diluted valuation of $1.4 trillion, marking one of the largest capital raises in AI history.

  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] High market demand for generative AI infrastructure and model development
  • [Key Metrics] Anthropic aims to raise $90.4 billion via IPO and private placement.
  • [Official Stance & Demand] Investor demand is currently 7 times the available share allocation.

Bloomberg Full Report

At the $74 midpoint, each founder's stake is worth roughly $17.8 billion to $18.1 billion before accounting for the shares they plan to sell in the offering. One cofounder, little known outside the company, owns more stock than CEO Dario Amodei. Large founder allocations are not unusual in Silicon Valley.

Snap's Evan Spiegel and Bobby Murphy, for example, retained enough super-voting stock to control about 89% of the voting power after the company's 2017 IPO. What makes Anthropic unique is the number of founders with huge stakes and how evenly those stakes are divided. Anthropic cofounder and Chief Compute Officer Tom Brown owns about 244 million shares of the AI startup, according to its S-1.

That narrowly tops the stakes of Anthropic's six other cofounders, who each own roughly 232 million to 243 million shares. At the $74 midpoint of Anthropic's expected IPO range, Brown's stake would be worth about $18.1 billion. The filing shows that Brown plans to sell 3.38 million shares in the offering, worth roughly $250 million at that price, leaving him with stock worth about $17.8 billion.

Brown co-founded Anthropic with the Amodei siblings and four others in January 2021. He now serves as chief compute officer, where he leads the technical organization responsible for "securing, scaling, and effectively using" Anthropic's computing resources, according to the filing.

Brown also played an important role in the technology that led to today's generative-AI boom. Before Anthropic, he worked at OpenAI, where the S-1 says he led the engineering of GPT-3. Brown and Dario Amodei also co-invented what Anthropic calls "deep RLHF," which the company said was central to transforming base large language models into conversational assistants such as Claude.

After Brown, Sam McCandlish has the next-largest disclosed stake, with about 243.4 million shares, worth roughly $18 billion at the midpoint. Christopher Olah holds about 242.9 million shares, followed by chief science officer Jared Kaplan with 242.4 million shares and Jack Clark with 242.3 million shares.

At the midpoint, their stakes are each worth about $17.9 billion before their planned IPO sales. Anthropic president Daniela Amodei owns 241.4 million shares, worth about $17.9 billion at the midpoint. CEO Dario Amodei owns 240.6 million shares, worth about $17.8 billion, and also controls voting proxies over another 4.6 million shares.

The founders' influence will extend far beyond their economic stakes. Immediately before the IPO, Anthropic plans to issue a special Class F share to an LLC controlled by the seven cofounders. That single share will carry 50.1% of Anthropic's total voting power on matters submitted to stockholders generally, including the election of directors other than those selected by Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust.

In another unusual twist, Anthropic says each of its cofounders has pledged to dedicate 80% of their personal equity in the company to philanthropic causes. Some of Silicon Valley's biggest companies and venture firms also stand to make huge windfalls from Anthropic's public-market debut.

Amazon and Google will each own more than 5% of Anthropic after the IPO conversions, making them two of the company’s largest strategic shareholders. At the midpoint of Anthropic's expected IPO range, Amazon's 1.92 billion shares would be worth about $142 billion, while Google's stake would be worth about $132 billion.

Among traditional venture firms, the filing provides unusually detailed numbers for Spark Capital because Spark partner Yasmin Razavi sits on Anthropic's board and was the first firm to invest when Anthropic was valued at $4.5 billion in 2023. Spark-affiliated funds have invested across at least six Anthropic financings since 2023.

Menlo Ventures led a round in 2024 when Anthropic was valued at about $18 billion. They have now invested over $1.3 billion in the company, also earning them a stake between 1.5% and 2%, according to a person familiar with the matter. Lightspeed Venture Partners is the largest venture shareholder, owning almost 2% of Anthropic, according to a person familiar with the matter.

They led a round at a $61.5 billion valuation in late 2024 and continued to double down. Iconiq also amassed a large stake by investing $6 billion in the company, according to a person familiar with the matter, giving the firm an ownership stake between 1.5% and 2%. Bessemer Venture Partners first invested in the Series D in 2024 and continued to write checks in every round since.

It has spent around $800 million on Anthropic, making it the firm's largest investment to date. Bessemer has an almost 1% stake in Anthropic, according to a person familiar with the matter. Other major winners include Coatue, which has invested $4.75 billion, and Sound Ventures, which invested more than $400 million, according to people familiar with the matter.
