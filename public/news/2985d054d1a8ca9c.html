US Blacklists Alibaba-Owned Company in Setback to AI Ambitions

✍️ By Bloomberg

The U.S. government has added Alibaba's Singapore-based subsidiary, Apex Enterprise Solutions, to the Entity List due to its financial ties with Megaspeed, a cloud firm suspected of circumventing AI chip export restrictions. Alibaba denied the allegations of unauthorized chip diversion and pledged legal challenges, while Singaporean Prime Minister Lawrence Wong confirmed local investigations into Megaspeed are underway.

Alibaba Group (BABA) stock fell overnight after the U.S. blacklisted its Singapore unit over alleged AI chip transfers to China. Alibaba denied the claims and vowed to fight the decision through legal action.

Alibaba stock dropped 2% overnight ahead of Tuesday, after a 6% rally in regular session. The stock edged 0.2% lower in Hong Kong.

According to a Reuters report, Alibaba plans to challenge the U.S. government's decision to place its Singapore subsidiary, Apex Enterprise Solutions, on a trade blacklist, arguing that the company has not violated U.S. export restrictions.

"Adding Apex Enterprise Solutions to the Entity List is erroneous because Apex has never diverted advanced computing chips subject to US export restrictions," said Alibaba spokesperson.

The move comes amid an expanding investigation into Megaspeed, an Nvidia-linked cloud computing firm suspected of helping restricted artificial intelligence chips reach China.

Apex's inclusion on the blacklist follows its financial relationship with Megaspeed. The Alibaba subsidiary extended credit to the cloud computing company through its Malaysian arm, Speedmatrix, in 2024, the report said.

Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said Tuesday that authorities would continue examining whether Megaspeed violated local laws. The blacklisting follows criticism from U.S. lawmakers and administration officials over how the Megaspeed investigation was handled.

Alibaba reported third-quarter revenue of $40.5 billion as its AI cloud and computing business grew 53% year-on-year. However, adjusted earnings of $1.21 per ADS missed estimates of $1.65. Meanwhile, higher spending on AI infrastructure pushed capital expenditure up 60% and widened its free cash flow outflow to $5.14 billion.

Susquehanna analyst Shyam Patil cut Alibaba's price target to $145 from $190 but maintained a Positive rating. The analyst said that although cloud growth was encouraging, U.S. export restrictions could slow expansion in FY28 by limiting the supply of computing chips.
