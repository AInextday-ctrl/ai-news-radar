Wallets linked to Trump Media (NASDAQ: DJT), labeled as “2028 Convertible Notes Collateral,” transferred 4,259 Bitcoin (CRYPTO: BTC) to Crypto.com, according to Arkham data. While no sale has been confirmed, blockchain data shows the coins’ movement without clarifying whether they were sold. These Bitcoins are tied to a debt, and creditors can demand repayment of about $1 billion by November 30.

Arkham tracked the transfer of the coins in two batches, 2,140.03 BTC and 2,119.31 BTC, logged within an hour on that day. Subsequently, Whale Alert reported that 4,259 BTC left Crypto.com for an unidentified wallet. Crypto.com operates both an exchange and custody service, so the coins might be headed for either a trading desk or a secure vault.

At present, Bitcoin trades at $76,517, down 4.8% over the last 24 hours, back below $80,000 after closing above that level on November 4. Trump Media shares are trading at $4.54, up 1% but down 24% over the past week.

Convertible notes are essentially loans that can be converted into company stock at a later date. Trump Media has pledged Bitcoin as collateral for its 2028 convertible notes. This means that if the company fails to repay its lenders, those lenders can claim the Bitcoin. The number of Bitcoins transferred aligns with the amount pledged as collateral in a recent quarterly report.

Although the notes mature in 2028, holders can demand repayment of about $1 billion on November 30. This looming deadline has raised eyebrows, especially after the recent collateral transfer.

Despite reporting a surge in revenue, with Trump Media generating $1.7 million in the second quarter—an 89% year-over-year increase—this amount pales in comparison to its $1.9 billion in financial assets. Non-cash losses during the same quarter hit $190.4 million due to unliquidated holdings.

Market analyst Charlie Bilello pointed out that Trump Media’s stock has plummeted 97% from its peak, reaching another all-time low. With cumulative losses of $1.8 billion since its inception, shares have dropped by 11% following the Democrats’ control of Congress, putting DJT down 66% for the year.

The company is also pursuing a merger with TAE Technologies, expected to close in the fourth quarter. Both the merger and the impending repayment deadline will be significant factors for management as the year wraps up.

Trump Media has not publicly addressed the Bitcoin transfers, nor has it filed any new Form 8-K with the SEC to report significant developments. Without a named buyer, sale price, or counterparty, the purpose of the Bitcoin transfer remains uncertain.

Whale Alert’s information further complicates matters because it cannot distinguish whether the transfer was an over-the-counter sale, a custody transfer, or an internal wallet shuffle. While deposits to exchanges often precede sales, the trail lacks clarity at this unknown address.

With Bitcoin priced at $76,517, the 4,259 BTC are valued at about $326 million. However, even if the Bitcoin were sold, the amount wouldn’t fully cover the $1 billion debt if all lenders requested cash.

While a 4,259 BTC transaction is significant for one wallet, it represents only a small fraction of the overall market. On November 4, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs took in a net $74.6 million, ending a three-day streak that pulled $1.3 billion from Bitcoin and Ethereum funds. Trump Media’s Bitcoin represents 4.4 times the amount brought into the ETFs that day.

This 4,259 BTC transfer signifies about 0.02% of Bitcoin’s circulating supply of roughly 20.1 million coins. Although a sale could momentarily affect the price, it remains a minor part of the total market. The name “Trump” also appears in connection with American Bitcoin (NASDAQ: ABTC), a distinct mining company that reported net income of about $126 million in the third quarter, although this data pertains to a different entity.

While no definitive sale has occurred, the transfer does not confirm one either. Moving coins from a collateral wallet to an exchange, then to an unknown address, could indicate a sale, a custody shift, or an arrangement with lenders—but without more information from Trump Media, the narrative remains open-ended.

Regardless of the path Trump Media chooses, a sale or transfer to lenders could help reduce its debt, while repayment in cash would let the company keep the Bitcoin on its books. For Bitcoin, the implications are minimal, as a corporate debt decision wouldn’t create new buyers, and a sale would only add to the supply.

A sale of 4,259 BTC seems unlikely to crash Bitcoin’s price, since it accounts for only about 0.02% of the total supply. Bitcoin’s broader issue lies in the ongoing trend of its value, as it currently trades 39.3% below its all-time high of $126,080. It remains below its 20-day and 50-day moving averages but stays above its 200-day average.
