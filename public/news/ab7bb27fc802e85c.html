Jensen Huang discusses comeback as Nvidia CEO with allies

✍️ By Financial Times

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is attempting to orchestrate a return to the company with support from Silicon Valley allies. However, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has publicly opposed the move, demanding that Nvidia’s board provide a detailed compliance report within 10 days regarding alleged illicit sales to Singapore-based entity Megaspeed.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Jensen Huang (Former Nvidia CEO), Scott Bessent (US Treasury Secretary), Howard Lutnick (US Commerce Secretary): Huang seeking reinstatement; Bessent pushing for boardroom shakeup and compliance investigation
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Scandal involving potential illicit sales to Singapore-based Megaspeed and alleged compromise by foreign intelligence
  • [Key Metrics] Nvidia's stock plunged 13% at the market open Thursday.
  • [Scope & Next Steps] Treasury mandates a compliance report within 10 days; Commerce Secretary scheduled for Senate hearing on Dec 10.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Financial Times Full Report

Jensen Huang is considering a return as Nvidia's chief executive, days after he resigned over reports that he had affairs with a suspected Chinese spy and a subordinate, the Financial Times reported Sunday.

Huang has discussed the possibility with confidants, according to the FT, which cited people familiar with the matter. One ally told the paper they were putting together a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby Nvidia's board for his return and would try to win support from the White House. The ally said Huang backed the effort.

Elon Musk and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney have backed a public letter calling on Nvidia's board to "Bring Jensen Back." On Friday, Nvidia shareholders Brad Gerstner of Altimeter Capital and Gavin Baker of Atreides Management urged the board in an open letter to keep "all options on the table, including the potential return of Jensen Huang."

A spokesperson for Nvidia and a spokesperson for Huang declined to comment to the FT. Huang doesn't hold a controlling stake in Nvidia and can't overrule its board.

The push runs into opposition from Washington. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Sunday called for a "dramatic shakeup" at Nvidia and urged its board to look outside the company for a new CEO.

"I want to keep coming back to what was the culture at Nvidia, and were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?" Bessent said on Fox News, CNBC reported. He said he wants "answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel," according to Axios.

"If this were a bank and they hadn't complied like this, the OCC would be in there right now, and a lot of the executives would be removed," Bessent said. Nvidia has said it complies with U.S. export laws.

Bessent has also blamed the Commerce Department, led by Secretary Howard Lutnick, for not restricting Megaspeed, the Singapore-based firm at the center of the scandal. "Treasury, War, USTR and State, they were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the Entity List for months, but Commerce has the pen," Bessent told CNBC on Friday. Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 10, CBS News reported.

Nvidia reports quarterly earnings Tuesday, its first since Huang's resignation. Interim CEO Colette Kress has scheduled a company-wide meeting for Wednesday, CNBC reported. The board said Friday it has not yet hired a search firm or "formally engaged any potential candidates" to replace Huang, according to CNBC. The FT reported Friday that the board had approached Arm CEO Rene Haas.

Huang resigned Thursday after revelations that he had extramarital affairs with multiple women, one of whom was an alleged Chinese spy. The company said in a statement that Kress, Nvidia's executive vice president and chief financial officer, will serve as interim CEO. Harvey C. Jones was appointed chair of the board.

Nvidia's stock plunged 13% at the market open Thursday. The company has lost more than $1.2 trillion in market value since the scandal broke in late October, Axios reported.

In its statement, the board thanked Huang for his "immeasurable contributions to the company." The chipmaker is at the center of the global AI economy and has multiple agreements with the U.S. government. Huang, who founded Nvidia in 1993, led the company through the AI boom that made it one of the world's most valuable publicly traded companies.

Last Sunday, Nvidia's board of directors said it was launching an investigation into Huang being the target of an alleged Chinese spy, with whom he had an affair.

Huang admitted to the affair in an email to Nvidia employees and said he is cooperating with the investigation. Reports have identified Alice Huang (no relation), a former executive at tech firm Megaspeed, as the woman he had the relationship with.

"I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry," Jensen Huang said in a statement.

The New York Times reported Wednesday that Huang also had an affair with Kimberly Powell, a married Nvidia executive who reported directly to him. Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, joined the company in 2008.

On Thursday, Reuters reported that Huang also had an "intimate relationship" with another Nvidia employee. Reuters did not identify the woman, and Nvidia declined to comment.

Rep. Brian Mast, the Republican chairman of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, told reporters Thursday that Huang had agreed to testify before Congress after being threatened with a subpoena. House Republicans have asked Huang to sit for a closed-door interview on Dec. 1, CNBC reported.

Here's what we know so far, and why the scandal matters.

Jensen Huang founded Nvidia, one of the biggest makers of specialized computer chips in the world. Its chips were first designed for computer graphics, but demand for Nvidia's hardware has skyrocketed with the AI boom of the past few years. China and the U.S. are in a heated competition for AI technology, and that includes access to chips like the ones Nvidia makes.

Huang is an American citizen who was born in Taiwan. He founded Nvidia in 1993 and served as its president and CEO until Thursday.

According to the New York Times, it's unclear when Jensen Huang met Alice Huang, but the two were photographed together in 2024 at a bar in Taipei, Taiwan. That was roughly when Megaspeed, the AI company she worked for, began its push into the AI sector. News reports have not confirmed whether Alice Huang still works at Megaspeed. Megaspeed has touted its membership in Nvidia's partnership program, which would give it access to Nvidia's latest technology.

Huang apologized for the affair in a message to Nvidia employees and asked for privacy for his family. "I take full responsibility for what I did and the hurt I caused," Huang wrote, "and I accept the criticism that comes with it."

He pledged to earn back his employees' confidence and said he was cooperating fully with the board's investigation. "I am committed to protecting NVIDIA and supporting America's national security," he wrote.

The New York Times reported that U.S. intelligence officials believe Alice Huang was gathering information for the Chinese government. China denied that it was spying, calling the allegation unsubstantiated. "It is nothing but smears against China," a Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson said.

The New York Times also reported that Nvidia executives contacted the U.S. Commerce Department last year seeking information about the department's investigation into Megaspeed. The investigation was later transferred to the Justice Department, which is reportedly looking into whether Megaspeed illegally routed Nvidia products into China.

AI is the biggest thing in tech right now, from chatbots like ChatGPT and Claude to software development and bioengineering. Governments are trying to work out how to use AI for security and across the economy, and China and the U.S. are racing to lead its development. The technology has moved so fast that some CEOs of AI companies have suggested a pause before it develops beyond what humans can control.

Nvidia has contracts with the U.S. government to use its technology in several areas, and Huang has a close relationship with President Trump. U.S. officials are trying to determine whether the affair allowed the Chinese government to obtain information about Nvidia's technology and its agreements with the U.S. government. Nvidia's board said in its statement that it does not believe national security or company secrets were compromised.

Trump said last Sunday that he considers Huang "a fine person" who should stay on as CEO of Nvidia.

"I think he probably has an excuse for it. We'll have to hear it," Trump told reporters. "He's done an amazing job for our country."

On Tuesday, the New York Times reported that Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who is also national security adviser, told Trump in May that the U.S. believed Huang was involved with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Trump received the information aboard Air Force One en route to a summit in China, the Times reported. Huang went with the president on that trip.

The Times said it was not the first time Trump had been told about U.S. intelligence on the affair. But the president said this week he was not aware of Huang's affair when Huang joined him for the China summit.

Huang's struggle to return underscores Nvidia's transition from a technological powerhouse to a focal point of US national security strategy, severely constraining board autonomy. The Treasury's aggressive intervention against Megaspeed signals a shift in regulatory tactics from standard antitrust to intrusive supply chain auditing, casting a high risk of valuation volatility during this leadership vacuum.
