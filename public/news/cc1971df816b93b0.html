Analysis: companies are set for a massive acceleration of 50GW+ in data center capacity additions in 2027; 24GW+ across 40 campuses are in the advanced stages (Jeremie Eliahou Ontiveros/SemiAnalysis)

✍️ By SemiAnalysis

A deep-dive research briefing revealed that global data center additions are poised for a massive 50GW+ surge by 2027. Tracking data confirms over 24GW across 40 advanced-stage mega campuses is already halfway built with heavy cooling and electrical switchgear installed, rebutting narratives of infrastructure paralysis.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Semiconductor infrastructure research analysts: Reported 50GW+ additions by 2027, with >24GW already past mid-stage construction
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Refuting market fear of power grid halts and proving physical buildouts proceed
  • [Key Metrics] That’s typically a 15-20 year contract, with a TCV of $30-50B per GW.
  • [Key Position & Outlook] Hard engineering milestones counter pervasive power-constraint FUD.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

SemiAnalysis Full Report

It’s very tempting to cede to the FUD given all the constraints on the datacenter buildout: power, labor, supply chains, capital, and many more. But at SemiAnalysis, we care about the truth, not about narratives. And here’s the truth for you: one picture, >24GW of datacenters in advanced stage of construction, all to be delivered in 2027. Most of them are more than halfway done, with exterior cooling and electrical equipment already installed. That’s just 40 campuses, a tiny share of the multiple hundred sites in advanced stage of construction. We track them all, and for the full granularity, use our Datacenter Industry Model.

SemiAnalysis Datacenter Industry ModelOf course, there are plenty of challenges and shortages. Behind these pictures, there are labor issues, supply chain delays, fundraising challenges, among many others. We’ve written in-depth about Modular Datacenters and Labor, Datacenter Electrical systems, Cooling systems, Behind-The-Meter power, the US Grid Constraints, and Nvidia’s ecosystem financial support. But the fact is simple: gigantic sites are simply getting built, and many are on time. For example, just go look at what Crusoe built in Abilene, what AWS built in Indiana, or QTS/Microsoft’s giant site in Georgia. These are the sites that have produced SOTA technology like GPT-6 and Fable 5.5. Looking ahead, despite all the constraints, an accelerating number of giant sites keep getting built, as shown by our Top 40 picture

SemiAnalysis Datacenter Industry ModelIf you ask any datacenter builder, they’ll tell you how hard it is, how some workers aren’t showing up, how vendors are delayed, and many other issues. All of this is accurate. And that’s why we’ve successfully called out countless datacenter delays and keep calling out developer timelines that appear overly aggressive in our Datacenter model, including the Oracle New Mexico Force Majure site delay in April. To be fair, +50GW is essentially the global cumulative datacenter market up through 2024 being built in one year! But our Datacenter Industry Model adjusts for all of that and still points to a material acceleration in datacenter capacity in 2027. So, what are we seeing that others can’t?

Let’s dig in. We’ll explore the following topics in this report:

SemiAnalysis proprietary leading indicators: we’ll show a sneak peak of our proprietary data supporting our forecast, such as self-build construction starts and datacenter leasing (in MWs and USD). Year-to-date, slightly under USD 1T of binding / non-cancellable datacenter leases have been signed – that’s key to supporting our aggressive 2027-28 forecast.

The shape of the buildout: how can we support +50GW in a single year? Where, what design, what power solutions?

The “real” question: are we going to face a datacenter oversupply? As we explained in The Great Silicon Shortage, datacenters & power may be the tip of the iceberg, with memory and logic possibly becoming the new #1 constraint in 2027-28.

Overall, we are very excited about 2027. The natural implication of the acceleration will be significantly more compute supply. That means cheaper and smarter AI, faster pace of AI model progress, and likely material revenue acceleration for leading AI labs and other token vendors. That also means unthinkable capex numbers. Reflexively rates will have to shoot up to enable the buildouts from a capital demand perspective. Let’s dive in!

SemiAnalysis Datacenter Industry ModelThe second indicator is Datacenter Leasing. We’ve talked about this a lot in prior reports, but as a quick recap: given the capital-intensity of building multi hundred MW datacenters, developers typically only spend 5-10% of project capital without a customer. They need a “pre-lease” to fund and develop the whole project. That’s typically a 15-20 year contract, with a TCV of $30-50B per GW. Check out our HPC Lease Tracker as part of the Datacenter Model to dive into actual contract economics.

Since H2 2025, things have dramatically changed. Oracle kicked off the era of gigantic leases, after closing on ~4.5GW of datacenter IT capacity across three sites between August and October 2025. Everyone else followed, and the pool of buyers has never been broader, with Meta, AWS, Microsoft, Google, Nvidia, AMD, Anthropic and OpenAI all being in the market for GW-scale leases. There’s also a long tail of Neoclouds signing hundred-MW-scale deals.

In USD terms, this means an unprecedented amount of lease value that builders can use to raise debt. Just in 2026 year-to-date, we’ve identified over a trillion USD of lease value that was signed and closed. That trillion is supporting a gigantic datacenter buildout in 2027-28. Our new Compute, Capital & Markets Model is now tracking all the debt raises and can confirm that most of this debt has been raised.

SemiAnalysis Datacenter Industry ModelThe shape of the buildout: how to build 50GW in a yearTracking all of these leading indicators has enabled us to forecast, before anyone else, the upcoming surge in orders for many industrial suppliers like Vertiv, ABB, Sterling Infrastructure. Check out our Industrials Model for in-depth coverage. However, the challenge is now going from orders to delivery. And resources have obviously become constrained, as power, labor, and sheer regulatory overhead are enormous burdens for developers. But the industry has promptly reacted with innovation.

The first step is to simplify datacenter designs. We show below how Google has modularized their electrical architecture, removing a significant amount of outdoors cabling that requires onsite assembly, replacing them with prefabricated electrical modules. We track all the equipment changes (including suppliers) for datacenter designs and forecast orders in our Industrials Model

SemiAnalysis Industrials ModelOthers have opted for more radical design changes. One example is Meta with its tent design, that we exclusively unveiled over a year ago. Another one is AWS which has materially simplified their shell design and launched a new rapid design dubbed “SAMDC”. Data halls are also increasingly prefabricated, an effort called Project Houdini at AWS. Construction timelines are now under 10 months. Hence our “LEGO Datacenters” title for the modular movement, you literally slot them in and go, and doing so allows for MEP labor (7-11 months of work) to drop up to 63%! We will have a full deep dive on the labor of the 2027 buildout coming soon.

SemiAnalysis Industrials ModelIn addition to hyperscale selfbuild, datacenter developers promoting prefabricated designs have also been remarkably successful. DayOne is likely the best example. With a total of ~3GW leased since their first deal in early 2023, the Singaporean company has been a rocket ship in terms of construction speed. While traditional developers have >18 months build timelines, DayOne cut this to ~9 months, which was key to signing huge deals with Firmus, OpenAI and Nvidia.
