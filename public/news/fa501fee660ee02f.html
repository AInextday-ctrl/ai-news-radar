Google Embraces Geothermal Technology to Vaporize Rock for Clean Power

Geothermal startup Quaise Energy wants to use futuristic technology to vaporize stone, drill at superhot depths and produce clean power around the clock. Now, it has Google on board.

The tech company has signed a deal valued at more than $500 million to secure 50 megawatts of power from a geothermal project that Houston-based Quaise is developing in central Oregon. The electricity produced at the site will support Google's existing data-center operations in the state.

Goldman Sees Gold Rising Above $4,600 an Ounce by End-2027 on Central-Bank Buying

Gold's near-term outlook is clouded by expectations of higher U.S. interest rates, but central-bank buying is set to drive prices back above $4,600 a troy ounce by the end of next year, Goldman Sachs said in its latest report.

The bank expects gold to reach $4,650 a troy ounce by the end of 2027, about 13% above its October average, as central-bank purchases--particularly by emerging-market countries seeking to diversify their reserves--emerge as the main force supporting prices.

China Flexes Muscle in Iron Ore, Squeezing Australia and Its Miners

Four years ago, China created a single, state-owned buyer to gain more control over resources needed for its mammoth steelmaking sector. This year, it has been putting its newfound power to work.

The market for iron ore, the steelmaking commodity that ranks among the world's most heavily traded, is dominated by Australia and China. The pair account for more than half of all production and purchases, respectively.

Oil Prices Rebound as Middle East Shipping Risks Persist

Oil prices rose Tuesday, recovering from initial losses as traders weighed continued threats to Middle Eastern oil shipments against expectations of a global supply surplus.

Front-month Brent crude futures rose 0.5% to $92.90 a barrel, while front-month West Texas Intermediate crude futures gained 0.4% to $86.10 a barrel.

U.S. Adds More Battery Energy Storage, Supporting Solar Growth

U.S. utilities, homes and businesses installed 14.3 gigawatt hours of new battery storage capacity in the third quarter of the year to back growth in renewable energy generation, according to a report by the Solar Energy Industries Association and Benchmark Mineral Intelligence.

Utility-scale projects added 12.2 gigawatt hours, residential battery storage increased by 746 megawatt hours, while commercial and industrial users added 1.3 gigawatt hours, largely on demand from data centers.

BP Names New Directors as Restructuring Continues

British energy major BP appointed two independent nonexecutive directors as it continues to reshuffle its leadership team as part of a broader business restructure.

It said Tuesday that Andrew Bonfield, a former chief financial officer of Caterpillar, National Grid, Cadbury, Bristol-Myers Squibb, and SmithKline Beecham, will join the company on Dec. 1.

Oil Futures Gain on Continued Shipping Risks -- Market Talk

Wheat Futures Rise as Ukraine Strikes Key Bridge -- Market Talk

1019 ET - Wheat futures lead the market higher after a Ukrainian strike caused significant damage to the Kerch Bridge, which spans the strait connecting the Sea of Azov with the Black Sea. "Obviously, shipments have already been effectively nonexistent relative to pre-war norms, but this damage could mean a substantial delay to resumed commodity flow even if an agreement was somehow reached," Mike Castle of StoneX says in a note. He remains skeptical about the likelihood of an agreement between Russia and Ukraine. Speculative selling pushed wheat down on optimism around talks, "but on the ground we've seen nothing but escalation," he adds. Wheat is up 1.9% on CBOT. Soybeans are up 0.6% and corn is up 0.3%.(anthony.harrup@wsj.com)

Palm Oil Ends Lower as Weakness in Soybean, Crude Oil Weigh -- Market Talk

1039 GMT - Palm oil prices closed lower, weighed by weaker soybean oil and crude oil prices, alongside softer Dalian vegetable oils and concerns over slowing Malaysian export demand, says David Ng, a trader at Kuala Lumpur-based Iceberg X. Ng expects crude palm oil futures to find support at around 4,400 ringgit a ton and face resistance at 4,600 ringgit a ton. The market sentiment will likely remain cautious amid weaker competing vegetable oils and energy prices, he adds. The Bursa Malaysia Derivatives contract for February delivery ended 113 ringgit lower at 4,434 ringgit a ton. (jiahui.huang@wsj.com; @ivy_jiahuihuang)

Ample Inventories, Weak Industrial Demand Weigh on Silver Outlook -- Market Talk

0946 GMT - Silver prices are unlikely to stage a significant rebound as weaker industrial demand and ample inventories weigh on the market, according to ANZ. In the solar sector, technological advances and efforts to reduce silver use are expected to drive a 19% decline in consumption in 2026. Uncertainty surrounding the expansion of artificial-intelligence infrastructure is adding to concerns about future demand. Meanwhile, ample inventories have left the market well-positioned to absorb any sudden increase in demand, limiting the potential for a price squeeze. A sustained recovery will require stronger investment demand, which in turn depends on gold prices resuming their upward trend, ANZ analysts say. (giulia.petroni@wsj.com)

Oil Falls as Focus Shifts to Market Surplus -- Market Talk

0926 GMT - Oil prices fall on growing expectations of a widening supply surplus next year despite persistent concerns over continued attacks in the Middle East. Brent crude is down 0.5% to $91.93 a barrel, while WTI trades 0.5% lower at $85.29 a barrel. The benchmarks have fallen around 16% and 14% on the month, respectively. However, "the supply flows are unsustainable over the longer term," analysts at ANZ say. "Logistics are under stress, while supply, inventories, refining and effective spare capacity are vulnerable." According to the latest Kpler data, Middle East oil flows--including shipments through Hormuz and alternative routes across the region--averaged more than 15.7 million barrels a day in the week through Nov. 15, below prewar levels of 23.3 million. (giulia.petroni@wsj.com)

Gold Remains Pressured by Fed Rate-Hike Bets -- Market Talk

0906 GMT - Gold prices hold below $3,900 a troy ounce, pressured by expectations of a Federal Reserve interest-rate hike next month. "Sentiment remains fragile, with rising Fed rate-hike expectations and a firmer dollar outweighing support from falling energy prices," analysts at Saxo Bank say. "This divergence highlights concerns that inflationary pressures are becoming broader, stickier and more difficult to reverse." Investors now await the release of the Fed's October meeting minutes for more cues on the monetary policy outlook. In early European trading, gold futures rise 0.4% to $3,869 an ounce. Meanwhile, silver rises 1.2% to $47.45 an ounce and platinum is up 0.6% at $1,457.30 an ounce. (giulia.petroni@wsj.com)

Gold Seen Around $4,000 in Long Term, But Risks Loom on Both Sides -- Market Talk

0859 GMT - Gold prices are expected to remain well above pre-Covid levels in the coming years, hovering around $4,000 a troy ounce, according to BMI. The outlook, however, is subject to risks in both directions, with geopolitical developments and shifts in U.S. monetary policy likely to shape the metal's trajectory. A preliminary deal to end the U.S.-Iran conflict could ease oil prices and inflationary pressures, paving the way for earlier Federal Reserve rate cuts. A weaker dollar would provide further support, potentially lifting prices back above $4,500 an ounce, analysts at the firm say. But a renewed escalation in the conflict, or a sharper rise in the dollar and bond yields if the Fed resumes raising rates, could undermine demand. Higher yields would make non-interest-bearing gold less attractive, potentially triggering a selloff that pushes prices back to the $3,000-$3,500 range, BMI says. (giulia.petroni@wsj.com)
