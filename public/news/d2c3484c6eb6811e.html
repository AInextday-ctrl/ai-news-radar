‘Tech bros strike back’ as Donald Trump rescues Nvidia’s Jensen Huang

✍️ By Joe Miller, Alex Rogers, Ella Lee, Claire Jones, and George Hammond

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has been reinstated after Silicon Valley investors, including Marc Andreessen, lobbied Vice President JD Vance and President Trump. Despite national security concerns raised by Secretary of State Marco Rubio regarding alleged spy ties, Trump prioritized the AI-led economic boom and tech market stability, supported by Attorney General Todd Blanche’s confirmation of no evidence of wrongdoing.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Jensen Huang (Nvidia CEO), Donald Trump (US President), JD Vance (VP), Marco Rubio (Secretary of State), Todd Blanche (AG) (US White House and Silicon Valley): Reinstating Huang to CEO post, leveraging economic stability to override security concerns
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Administration fear of tech-driven recession; influence of Silicon Valley donor class
  • [Key Fact Detail] Core lobbying: Silicon Valley figures leveraged influence through VP Vance to the President.
  • [Industry Impact & Outlook] Outlook: Huang’s stability is tied to current political climate, leaving lingering compliance risks.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Financial Times Full Report

Silicon Valley’s alliance with Donald Trump’s White House looked broken this month, after voters punished Republicans and Big Tech donors, markets soured on AI and the boss of America’s biggest chipmaker went down in a spy scandal.

But in a single weekend, Trump’s tech allies reasserted their power in Washington, lobbying the commander-in-chief to defy his own intelligence agencies and lean on Nvidia to bring back Jensen Huang as chief executive.

“The return of Jensen is the tech bros striking back,” said a former Trump official.

Tech investors, including Gavin Baker, Brad Gerstner and Marc Andreessen, made entreaties on Huang’s behalf to officials including US vice-president JD Vance, who has deep ties to Silicon Valley.

Together they convinced the president that Huang was crucial to keeping the AI boom going, and Vance helped lobby Nvidia’s board to accept his return as CEO.

Silicon Valley figures proved they “still clearly have the president’s ear on these issues”, the person added.

However, Huang’s reinstatement could store up trouble for his allies in Trump’s orbit, since the Nvidia CEO still faces investigations that could unearth evidence that would tarnish his defenders.

Their victory came despite alarm among many Republicans about the national security implications of Huang’s affair with a suspected Chinese spy. It was a setback for secretary of state Marco Rubio, who reportedly issued a memo to Trump officials warning of Huang’s close relationship with the woman.

Rubio, also Trump’s national security adviser, did not respond to a shouted request for comment during a brief appearance with the Qatari prime minister on Monday.

Trump was convinced to back Huang’s reinstatement by the plunge in tech stocks and warnings the US risked recession without Huang, which overrode security considerations, people familiar with the matter said.

Attorney-general Todd Blanche also assured the president that the Department of Justice had “no evidence of any wrongdoing by either Jensen Huang or Nvidia”, according to a person familiar with the matter.

Trump then repeated tech billionaires’ talking points about Huang’s singular importance to the economy in an hour-long TV interview with CNBC on Monday.

As tech shares temporarily surged in the wake of the Nvidia news, people close to Vance — a Peter Thiel protégé who has publicly kept his distance from AI billionaires since assuming office — were keen to stress that his “stature” in the tech world helped seal the deal.

They said the White House came to believe that Huang had been targeted by opponents in the AI industry because he championed open-source AI models, which are a threat to the business models of OpenAI and Anthropic.

Trump, who has defended Huang throughout the scandal, was increasingly receptive to such arguments, according to people familiar with the matter.

“I think Jensen . . . has played Trump like a very adept fiddler — gifts and garlands and flattery — he’s lauded him and heaped him with the praise Trump likes so much,” said one veteran Silicon Valley investor.

Huang, who donated $25mn to the president’s ballroom project last year and credited him with fuelling the AI boom, “can thank Trump entirely for being reinstated”, the investor added. The White House did not respond to a request for comment.

Tech figures were quick to cheer Huang’s return, and dismiss fears about his conduct. “I hate to break it to you, but there are double standards for high performers,” venture capitalist and tech podcaster Jason Calacanis told Fox News.

Outside the Oval Office, however, anger was mounting among the Maga faithful.

“On Sunday, the Director of National Intelligence told the public there were multiple investigations into Nvidia and Jensen Huang. Monday morning, he is reinstated,” Steve Bannon, Trump’s former chief strategist, told the FT, referring to comments made by Jay Clayton over the weekend. “I think we may have just nationalised Nvidia.”

The whole Nvidia affair smacked of “absolute blazing corruption”, said Ryan Girdusky, a Maga-aligned political strategist and commentator. “Either we’re a country of laws or a country where billionaire tech people are allowed to do whatever they want.”

“We’re gonna have this bump in the market that could probably go away tomorrow,” he said of the surge in US stocks following Monday’s Nvidia news. “I don’t know why you would take ownership of [Huang’s reinstatement] . . . I would be distancing myself from it.”

His comments proved prescient as Nvidia stock closed down nearly 6 per cent on Tuesday, leading a 2 per cent drop in the tech-heavy Nasdaq 100.

Few senior Republicans besides Vance publicly backed Trump’s move to rescue Huang. Treasury secretary Scott Bessent, who previously accused Nvidia of trying to “goose their revenues and their earnings” via “seemingly illicit sales”, did not come out in support of Huang’s return, although he did appear to retract some of his criticism in a TV appearance on the Sunday before Nvidia’s announcement.

Huang’s friends in the administration also distanced themselves. A person close to Trump’s former AI tsar David Sacks — an important ally of Huang’s in Big Tech’s fight against stringent AI regulation — said he had not been involved “in any way” in the lobbying of the president over the weekend.

A person close to commerce secretary Howard Lutnick, who brokered Huang’s introduction to Trump in 2025 and has remained close to the Nvidia executive, said he too had played no part.

On Monday, the commerce department blacklisted Megaspeed, the company that formerly employed Huang’s paramour Alice Huang, citing “significant risk of [the company] being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized . . . end users”.

Huang’s few remaining allies in Washington also fear his resurrection may prove shortlived.

Congressional Republicans who once would have fallen into line behind the president’s decision made it clear that they would continue to investigate Nvidia and its chief executive. Several vowed to back tighter chip export controls in the must-pass defence budget bill, known as the NDAA.

If that fails, Democrats, who will be in the majority in both houses of Congress in January, might get enough Republicans onside for measures targeting Nvidia to override a veto from the president, a person working on legislation with Republicans said.

“You’re likely to see China hawk fury in Congress only grow,” the former Trump official said. “And they will lash out through things like the upcoming NDAA.”
