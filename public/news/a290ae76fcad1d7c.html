📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Nikkei Asia Full Report

TOKYO -- Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. plans to use a new $500 million venture fund to support promising chip startups that could become the next Nvidia or Broadcom, as the world's largest contract chipmaker seeks to broaden its ecosystem and "formalize" its investment activities amid the AI boom, a senior executive told Nikkei Asia.

Kevin Zhang, TSMC's deputy co-chief operating officer and senior vice president of business development and global sales, said the new fund, announced this week, could also invest in suppliers and other upstream partners, as the company aims to expand its ecosystem beyond its direct customers.

"We feel it's time to ...formalize this investment mechanism, and we also want to extend the scope [to] not only just target our immediate customers," Zhang said, revealing for the first time the rationale behind TSMC's decision to establish a dedicated investment arm. "We also want to use the opportunity to develop the broader ecosystem, potentially including our suppliers, for example, our upstream [partners]."

This would represent a change of approach for TSMC, which has not previously invested in its suppliers. The company relies on a vast supply chain that provides everything from specialty and advanced chemicals and materials to a wide range of chipmaking equipment and testing tools. Intellectual property providers are also classed as partners.

Zhang also explained the thinking behind TSMC's decision to invest up to $500 million each in Anthropic and OpenAI, two of the world's leading AI model developers, a move announced on Tuesday, the same day as the new investment arm.

"By investing in Anthropic or OpenAI, we create a reinforcing loop to basically build a richer and healthier and hopefully a bigger ecosystem," he said. "Our goal is to continue to drive the advancement of silicon technology to address the future computation requirement."

Both Anthropic and OpenAI are looking to develop their own custom chips, in some cases with key TSMC customers. OpenAI, for instance, is working with Broadcom on AI chips dubbed Jalapeno.

The new fund's initial $500 million is significantly larger than the roughly $200 million "emerging fund" TSMC established in 2021 to help startups tap the company's advanced chip manufacturing capabilities.

"The purpose of the [emerging] fund was really to help to lower the barrier for the startup company to be able to develop innovative products based on TSMC advanced node tech," Zhang said. TSMC's move to broaden its investment scope comes amid a massive AI infrastructure buildout that has led to prolonged shortages of critical materials and equipment.

Zhang said the decision to set up the venture investment arm underscores TSMC's commitment to supporting not only global chip giants through its manufacturing services but also emerging innovators across the semiconductor industry. TSMC is best known for manufacturing advanced chips for some of the world's leading chip designers, including Apple, Nvidia, Google and Qualcomm, among its more than 530 customers worldwide.

"We're not just working to support large customers," Zhang said. "When you talk about TSMC, you immediately think, you have Apple, you have Nvidia, you have AMD, you have Broadcom [as top clients]. Yes, those are great customers. But remember, when Nvidia started working with us, Nvidia was a startup."

"We not only support today's Nvidia, but we also want to support the future's Nvidia," the senior executive said.

TSMC has committed to invest up to $1.7 billion in total, which includes its 2021 emerging fund, the new investment arm and planned investments in OpenAI and Anthropic. It would not rule out expanding the investment fund further if needed, while returns from its original emerging fund had been "exceptional," according to Zhang.

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

TSMC’s move signifies a transition from a pure-play foundry to an ecosystem orchestrator, using capital to integrate upstream material and equipment suppliers to mitigate systemic failure risks. By investing in AI labs, TSMC is asserting its standard-setting influence over chip design, forcing designers to align with its process nodes early and deepening customer lock-in.
