Jensen Huang’s reported resignation as NVIDIA chief executive in November 2026 marked a dramatic turn in the career of the man who helped build the company into one of the world’s most valuable technology businesses. His departure followed reports concerning his personal relationships, questions about his professional associations and an investigation by the company’s board.

Huang’s journey from a difficult childhood in Taiwan to the leadership of a global semiconductor giant reflects decades of ambition, business setbacks and technological achievements. Under his leadership, NVIDIA became a central player in the artificial intelligence industry, reaching a valuation of $5 trillion in October 2025. However, the latest developments surrounding his personal life and corporate position have brought a different dimension to his legacy.

Born in Tainan, Taiwan, in 1963, Huang moved to the United States at the age of nine with his older brother. Their parents sent them to live with an uncle, who believed he had arranged places for them at a good boarding school in Kentucky. The institution, however, turned out to be a reform school.

Huang reportedly spent part of his daily routine cleaning toilets. His roommate was a 17-year-old with knife scars from fights. The two developed an unusual friendship. Huang taught him to read, while his roommate introduced him to weightlifting. Huang also developed his table tennis skills and became nationally ranked as a teenager.

His family later reunited in Oregon, where Huang worked as a dishwasher and busboy at Denny’s while attending high school. He skipped two grades and graduated at 16. In 1984, he earned an electrical engineering degree from Oregon State University. His laboratory partner was Lori, whom he later married.

Huang subsequently worked at AMD and LSI Logic before completing a master’s degree at Stanford University in 1992 while continuing to work full time.

While working in the technology industry, Huang began meeting engineers Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem at a Denny’s restaurant in San Jose. Their discussions eventually led to the establishment of NVIDIA in 1993, when Huang was 30.

The company’s early years were marked by commercial difficulties. Its first chip, the NV1, failed in 1995. A subsequent chip developed for Sega’s next gaming console also failed to meet expectations. Huang informed Sega that NVIDIA could not deliver the product but asked the company to invest in the business regardless.

Sega reportedly invested $5 million, which Huang later described as all the money the company had. Facing serious financial pressure, he reduced NVIDIA’s workforce from approximately 100 employees to 40. For several years, he reportedly opened meetings by telling employees that the company was 30 days away from going out of business.

The situation improved in 1997 when NVIDIA’s RIVA 128 chip sold one million units within its first four months. The company went public in 1999 at $12 a share and introduced the GeForce 256, which it described as the world’s first GPU. Microsoft subsequently selected NVIDIA to develop the graphics chip for its first Xbox console.

NVIDIA’s introduction of CUDA in 2006 marked an important shift in the company’s direction, allowing its chips to perform tasks beyond gaming. However, the market initially struggled to recognise the technology’s potential. During the 2008 financial crisis, NVIDIA’s market value fell below $5 billion.

The importance of its technology became increasingly apparent in 2012, when AlexNet, a model associated with the beginning of modern artificial intelligence, was trained using two NVIDIA gaming cards. In 2016, Huang personally delivered the company’s first AI supercomputer to OpenAI.

NVIDIA expanded its business through the $6.9 billion acquisition of networking company Mellanox in 2019. In 2020, it agreed to acquire Arm for $40 billion, but regulators blocked the proposed transaction in 2022.

That year, NVIDIA’s stock declined by approximately 50%. ChatGPT launched in November 2022, with its training relying on NVIDIA chips. The company subsequently reached a $1 trillion valuation in 2023 and crossed $3 trillion in 2024. In July 2025, NVIDIA became the first company to reach a $4 trillion valuation. It crossed the $5 trillion mark in October of the same year.

The latest chapter in Huang’s career began attracting attention in October 2026, when The New York Times reportedly published a report about his association with a woman whom US officials suspect of being a Chinese spy. NVIDIA responded that his dealings with the woman were “not unusual or unexpected”.

In November, three House committee chairs reportedly requested that Huang explain his connections with the woman during a closed-door briefing. Three days later, he admitted being unfaithful to his wife, according to the supplied account. NVIDIA’s board subsequently opened an investigation.

The New York Times then reported another alleged relationship involving an NVIDIA executive who reports directly to Huang. The Wall Street Journal separately reported that the company’s board was preparing to push him out of his leadership position.

On November 12, Huang resigned as NVIDIA’s chief executive, 33 years after co-founding the company. Within hours of the announcement, NVIDIA reportedly lost as much as $600 billion in market value before closing the day down 4.3%. Reuters also reported a third alleged relationship involving another NVIDIA employee.

Huang’s career raises questions about the relationship between professional achievements, personal conduct and corporate accountability. His contribution to NVIDIA’s technological growth remains distinct from the allegations and reports surrounding his departure. The question of whether to follow his example therefore extends beyond business success to the responsibilities that accompany leadership.
