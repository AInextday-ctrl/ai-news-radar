US tech stocks plunge into bear market in worst week since Covid-19 crisis

✍️ By George Steer

The Nasdaq 100 index fell 5.6% on Friday, bringing the total weekly decline to 10.4%, marking its worst weekly performance in over six years. This volatility was driven by significant sell-offs in leading AI infrastructure and model firms like NVIDIA and Anthropic, reflecting a shift in investor sentiment regarding the translation of heavy AI capital expenditures into sustainable corporate earnings.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Financial Times Full Report

US tech stocks plunged into a bear market in their worst week since the Covid-19 outbreak shook the global economy as fears of a slowdown in the AI investment boom triggered a wave of selling on Wall Street.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 tumbled 5.6 per cent on Friday, taking its loss for the week to 10.4 per cent, its worst run in more than six years. The index has dropped 20 per cent from its intraday high on October 6, the threshold typically associated with bear market territory.

The S&P 500 fell 3.7 per cent, taking its loss since an October peak to more than 10 per cent. Investors fled into haven assets, sending yields on 10-year Treasuries down 0.1 percentage points to 4.94 per cent.

Trading was frantic on Friday, with selling accelerating into the close and AI stocks sustaining the steepest losses. The Vix index — known as Wall Street’s “fear gauge” — soared to 40 points, its highest level since April 2025.

The rout is the latest sign of how enthusiasm over AI has rapidly morphed into deep angst over the huge amount of spending and borrowing by Big Tech companies to finance investments in chips and data centres.

Anthropic had surged 17 per cent on its market debut on Thursday after raising a record $120bn in its initial public offering and a concurrent private placement but dropped 9.1 per cent on Friday.

The AI lab’s listing had been billed as a defining moment for the AI industry and global markets, giving investors the chance to gain exposure to one of the world’s leading developers of the technology. But those hopes unravelled on Friday as the group’s shares fell back close to its listing price.

“It looks like Armageddon out there,” the chief investment officer of a US hedge fund told the FT, adding that he had decided against buying more Anthropic stock as Friday’s sell-off accelerated.

Nvidia also tumbled 14 per cent as the scandal that has engulfed the company at the heart of the AI fervour deepened. The Securities and Exchange Commission is probing Nvidia’s accounting, according to a person familiar with the matter. The news was first reported by Reuters.

The chip giant is already facing congressional hearings, and President Donald Trump has said the FBI is investigating the circumstances around ousted chief executive Jensen Huang’s affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy.

The SEC did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Nvidia declined to comment. The stock has fallen almost 50 per cent since early October, erasing $2.8tn of market value.

“Jensen and Nvidia are the embodiment of the AI build-out. To see that things were less than forthright has shaken everyone’s confidence,” said Mike O’Rourke at Jones Trading.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index of US chip stocks on Friday dropped 10.7 per cent, taking its decline since a peak in June to 43 per cent. “The AI bubble has been deflating for months,” O’Rourke added.

Shares in Sandisk and Western Digital dropped 16 per cent and 12 per cent, respectively. Broadcom and Marvell fell 12 per cent and Micron shed 14 per cent.

The Democrats’ resounding US midterm elections victory earlier this month has “absolutely” contributed to the latest stock market sell-off, according to Jim Caron, chief investment officer of portfolio solutions at Morgan Stanley Investment Management.

“All of the spending, AI capex-related, which we know generated this growth — will that now be curtailed by policies, various investigations that we expect to start to come up in January and get announced when the new Congress takes their seats? The market is trying to figure this out,” Caron said.

Volatility across stocks spread to other financial assets, with bitcoin sliding below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since July.

Gold offered little shelter. Spot prices lost almost 3 per cent to about $3,522 a troy ounce, the lowest level in more than a year.

Traders in the Treasury futures market, meanwhile, pulled back expectations of rate rises from the Federal Reserve as the equity market continued to slide.

Investors are no longer fully pricing in a quarter-point increase at the Fed’s meeting in December, days after governor Christopher Waller speculated that the central bank could deliver a jumbo 0.5 percentage point increase.

Hedge funds, pension funds and retail investors all appear to be dumping stocks on intensifying fears about higher interest rates and a slowing data centre build-out, according to Nomura strategists.

“The equity market has been focused on the Nvidia scandal [but] now we have a Fed primed to hike [rates] and commodities pricing in recession risk,” said Alex Witten, Nomura’s co-head of Americas equities.

This sell-off signals a transition from narrative-driven optimism to financial validation, where the gap between CAPEX intensity and marginal output becomes the primary metric. With key incumbents facing valuation pressure, market focus will shift from parameter-count arms races to the economic viability of AI inference and the speed of commercial closure in vertical enterprise software.
