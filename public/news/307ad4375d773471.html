Kalshi Buys Securities Firm to Expand Into Stock Trading

✍️ By Vicky Ge Huang

Prediction-market platform Kalshi acquired New York-based broker-dealer FB Securities in July as part of a strategic shift toward traditional financial assets. The company plans to launch stock and ETF trading services within the next two months to expand its multi-asset capabilities.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Wall Street Journal Full Report

Kalshi, a pioneer in the newfangled prediction markets, is now expanding into one of Wall Street’s oldest businesses: stock trading.

The company bought FB securities, a small New York-based broker, in July and plans to launch equity trading within the next two months, people familiar with the matter said.

The prediction-market platform plans to offer stocks and ETFs within two months as it builds toward a multi-asset exchange

Mon 16 Nov 2026 · 15:23 EST · 3 min read

Kalshi built its name on letting people trade on whether things will happen. Now it wants them trading things that already exist.

The CFTC-regulated prediction-market platform plans to launch stock and ETF trading within two months. The plan follows its acquisition of FB Securities, a broker-dealer based in New York.

Kalshi acquired FB Securities in July 2026. The two-month launch window surfaced in Wall Street Journal reports dated November 16, 2026.

Counting forward from those reports, the research points to a launch that could land around mid-January 2027. That date is an estimate based on the reported window, not a confirmed launch day from the company.

The financial terms of the deal have not been disclosed. Neither Kalshi nor any other party has published a price tag for FB Securities.

The value of the purchase lies less in the brand than in the plumbing. A broker-dealer brings brokerage infrastructure and the regulatory permissions needed to let customers buy and sell stocks and ETFs directly.

For Kalshi, that shortcut matters. Its existing business runs under CFTC oversight, which covers event contracts and derivatives. Offering ordinary equities requires a different set of approvals, and owning a broker-dealer is one way to bring those approvals in-house.

Kalshi frames the move as part of a broader effort to become a multi-asset financial exchange. That lineup would combine three categories under one roof.

First come its prediction markets, the core product where users trade contracts tied to real-world outcomes. Second are perpetual futures, derivatives that let traders take positions on price moves without a set expiry date.

Third, if the plan holds, will be traditional equity products: individual stocks and exchange-traded funds. ETFs are baskets of assets that trade like a single share.

The company has the money behind that ambition. Kalshi’s Series G funding round in October 2026 valued it at approximately $40 billion.

Kalshi’s identity has been tied to prediction markets, a format that turns questions about future events into tradable contracts. Prices on those contracts move as traders update their views on how likely an outcome is.

Operating under CFTC regulation has been central to its pitch. It positions Kalshi as a regulated venue rather than an offshore betting site, a distinction that matters to users and to the institutions backing it.

The later addition of perpetual futures already pushed the platform beyond pure event trading. Stocks and ETFs extend that drift further toward the territory of established retail trading platforms.
