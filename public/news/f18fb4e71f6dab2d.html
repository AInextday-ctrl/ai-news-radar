Wall Street Journal Full Report

Two years before she pulled off the largest public offering of all time, Daniela Amodei gathered everyone at Anthropic in a 16-acre garden on a sweltering fall day near the Santa Cruz mountains.

The AI company now with a market value of roughly $1.18 trillion was at the time still a young startup that Amodei and her big brother, Dario, had co-founded with five friends. It was undergoing a hiring spree and Daniela was worried. So they carted 10-foot-tall bookshelves onto the grass to make reading nooks, played games and made friendship bracelets. As the company’s president, and keeper of its culture, the younger Amodei wanted to deliver a message.

“Keep Anthropic weird,” Daniela told them.

Anthropic embraces being atypical because it believes the artificial intelligence future will be anything but normal. Many co-founders meet in college, or at a first job. This pair met when he was 4 and she was born.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and President Daniela Amodei have defended the artificial intelligence (AI) company's massive infrastructure commitments and reiterated their focus on responsible AI development, saying its record-breaking initial public offering (IPO) will not change its long-term mission.

Speaking to CNBC International ahead of Anthropic's Nasdaq debut, the siblings addressed the company's $900 billion AI commitments, its approach to balancing growth with safety, partnerships with major technology companies and its relationship with the US government.

Daniela Amodei described the IPO as a significant milestone but said the company would remain focused on its original objectives.

Addressing questions around Anthropic's substantial AI infrastructure spending, Dario Amodei defended commitments of around $900 billion, arguing that they should be viewed in the context of the company's cash reserves and growing revenue.

Amodei added that Anthropic tries to "err on the low side" when forecasting its computing requirements.

He also acknowledged the challenge of balancing the pace of AI development with safety considerations, describing it as one of the company's central tensions.

"What we've done with pacing is essentially said, look, we could drive at 200 miles an hour. Maybe let's only drive at 90 miles an hour. Spend time figuring out how to do that safely, and the rest of that surplus we spend building the model in the right way," he said.

On the expectations surrounding Anthropic's public market debut, Amodei said the company was keeping its focus on execution rather than the spotlight.

"We've been calm about it," he said, adding that Anthropic's approach was to "always want to under promise and over deliver".

Daniela Amodei said safety remained central to Anthropic's approach, arguing that the potential benefits of AI must be balanced against the risks associated with the technology.

"The promise is incredible, and the risks are real. And we were really founded on this idea that it is our job to mitigate those risks," she said, adding that the company remained deeply committed to safety.

She also highlighted Anthropic's public benefit structure, saying the company wanted its economic value and broader mission to be embedded in its organisational framework.

On data centre expansion, Daniela said Anthropic would need to listen to local communities and take their concerns into account.

Dario Amodei described Anthropic's relationship with Nvidia as a "long and productive partnership". He also expressed satisfaction with its work with SpaceX amid speculation about whether the company could scale back its use of the Elon Musk-led company's computing capacity.

"We're finding that compute to be high quality. We're enjoying working with SpaceX," he said.

On Anthropic's differences with the US Department of Defense, Daniela Amodei said there were no significant updates but expected the company's relationship with the government to continue.

She said Anthropic had worked closely with the US national security establishment since its founding and remained committed to ensuring that its Claude AI models could help protect the country.

Dario Amodei also said Anthropic hoped to secure an investment-grade credit rating in the future, adding that debt was an instrument the company would like to use at some point.

Anthropic is set for its Nasdaq debut on Thursday after raising more than $81 billion in a record-breaking IPO, surpassing SpaceX's offering in June.

The Claude creator sold 980 million shares at $83 apiece on Wednesday, taking its fully diluted market capitalisation to around $1.5 trillion.

The five-year-old company has seen rapid growth on the back of its Claude AI models and products. According to its prospectus, Anthropic generated $33.24 billion in revenue in the nine months ended September 30, up 1,200% from the same period last year.

Speaking at Nasdaq, Amodei highlighted AI's potential to advance medicine and help cure diseases, while reiterating the importance of responsible development.

"Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so, only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing," he said.

"If we can show it's possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company, others in our industry will be inspired to do the same," he added.
