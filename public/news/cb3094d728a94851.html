What is Nvidia without AI’s sugar daddy in charge?

✍️ By Elizabeth Lopatto

Following Jensen Huang's departure due to personal misconduct, interim CEO Colette Kress held her first all-hands meeting at Nvidia. To counter market concerns regarding supply chain stability without Huang, Kress confirmed supply agreements remain valid through the end of the decade and announced accelerated salary and equity packages to address internal unrest.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Interim CEO Colette Kress, Board Chair Harvey C. Jones (Nvidia all-hands meeting): Announced accelerated pay raises and equity, reaffirmed supply chain agreements
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Managing leadership crisis and potential brain drain following Huang's exit
  • [Key Fact Detail] Following Jensen Huang's departure due to personal misconduct, interim CEO Colette Kress held her first all-hands meeting at Nvidia.
  • [Industry Impact & Outlook] Kress's focus on fiscal discipline may signal the end of the company's historically aggressive customer-financing strategy, shifting Nvidia's supply chain dominance to rely more on legal contract enforcement rather than personal endorsements.

The Verge AI Full Report

If the glorious AI revolution rests entirely on the well-being of one man, AI is perhaps not as robust a technology as I had previously been led to believe. Nonetheless, chunks of Silicon Valley are now insisting he is indispensable. For instance, if Elon Musk is telling the truth, then Jensen Huang (the former CEO of Nvidia, a company with a market cap of about $3 trillion) has been making deals that were “a handshake with Jensen” with suppliers — “not a written contract,” Musk wrote.

Fascinating stuff to tell shareholders. But after listening to Nvidia’s first earnings call with its new CEO, Colette Kress, I do think something may have shifted. In her former role as CFO, Kress generally told Huang to stop spending so much money on financing its customers, because telling the CEO to stop spending so much goddamn money is the CFO’s job. Apparently he belittled her about this, but that’s the CEO–CFO relationship, baby! Anyway she’s the boss now, at least temporarily. I think it’s reasonable to assume that though Nvidia has for years been the AI industry’s sugar daddy, without Huang and his handshakes in the picture, it may be less liberal with its cash.

"Kress shot down the idea that Huang’s departure would lead to a brain drain within Nvidia"

Kress’ job was never going to be easy. Even without tantrums from the likes of Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney and Musk, both of whom seem to have a lot of free time for guys who are ostensibly running multibillion-dollar companies, having to steer a company after an event on the scale of Huang’s meltdown is a miserable task. But this woman also is now having to deal with a bunch of guys publicly insisting that Huang was the only reason Nvidia succeeded. It amused me on the call that she did the thing I often do when men decide they can bully me, which is to tap in another man who is theoretically or actually in charge of me — and so on the call, Nvidia board Chair Harvey C. Jones politely told these losers to get bent. The board does not intend to reinstate Huang, and the company won’t be taking questions about him.

On the call, Kress pointedly noted that Nvidia’s agreements with suppliers last through the end of the decade, so I suppose we must entertain the possibility that when it comes to the handshake deals, Musk is, once again, lying. This is an important thing, though — as SemiAnalysis noted in its bizarrely conspiratorial post on the situation, Nvidia is successful because it has choked off the supply chain.

Kress also shot down the idea that Huang’s departure would lead to a brain drain within Nvidia, or the kind of open internal revolt that got Sam Altman reinstated at OpenAI during “the blip” in 2023. She told investors that “our employees, we believe, are committed. They are committed to doing their life’s work at Nvidia.” Nvidia’s employees have “been working seamlessly on what they need to complete,” she added. “From a business perspective, it’s business as usual.” I’m sure we’ll hear more about this in the coming days, but unlike with Altman, Huang resigned, and we all know why. And while some employees support reinstating Huang, I notice that there’s a real dearth of “we’ll quit with him” in those sentiments.

I don’t think this is going to shut the Branch Nvidians up, but I do think the board doesn’t have another choice here. Hilariously, we learned this weekend that Nvidia did not buy complete directors’ and officers’ insurance. While the individual executives’ and board members’ personal assets are shielded from lawsuits that stem from its executives’ behavior, the company is not. So if someone, or perhaps several someones, were to file a suit because of, say, Huang’s personal life and its damaging effects on the stock price, Nvidia itself is on the hook.

"The scandal isn’t mainly about the affairs, as much fun as we all have been having with them"

This is to say nothing of the Securities and Exchange Commission’s investigation — which, given how cliqued up Huang was with President Donald Trump, may be investigating on his behalf rather than investigating him — or the California attorney general going on Bloomberg TV to say that he was going to start kicking the tires on an investigation, or Huang and general counsel Tim Teter’s upcoming congressional testimony. The three actual lawyers left at the Department of Justice may be doing their own investigation, too. Elizabeth Warren seems mad. Marco Rubio’s name has been publicly attached in stories on the leaks, which makes me a little suspicious about who’s been doing the leaking.

A reminder: The scandal isn’t mainly about the affairs, as much fun as we all have been having with them. It is about Nvidia customers allegedly circumventing a ban on selling chips to China. The scandal is already big enough that US policy has been altered; the Department of Commerce informed us all on November 16th that there were new restrictions on selling chips to countries such as Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil. Also, data centers around the world need licenses from the US to lease compute to companies headquartered in China.

“No one could tell them ‘no’ or put them in their place.”

You may be wondering how these restrictions will affect Nvidia’s sales. The company also addressed this, though somewhat obliquely: It raised its forecast, saying that revenue will grow by 75 percent next year. Still, given that Singapore alone was responsible for $104 billion of revenue in 2025, I am curious about the number; at the time, it was the second-biggest geographic area by revenue, dwarfed only by the US. I can’t tell you how much Singaporean sales matter to Nvidia now, because around the time that Singaporean sales reportedly started to represent half the company’s revenue, Nvidia switched its reporting methodology. The company had previously noted that “most shipments associated with Singapore revenue were to locations other than Singapore and shipments to Singapore were insignificant.” Notably, Megaspeed had a Singapore address — and was the subject of a truncated Commerce Department investigation into whether its chips were being diverted to China. Fun stuff!

You know what else is fun? Jensen Huang’s children are still at the company, at least as far as anyone knows. His daughter Madison, described as Huang’s “chief of staff” by The New York Times, even helped direct Nvidia’s response to inquiries about Megaspeed, which have been mounting now for a year, the Financial Times reported. Her husband, Nico Caprez, “was promoted to a role that covered international deals with cloud computing clients in Asia” around the time those inquiries were getting started. “They had a lot of power and influence and were allowed to operate unchecked,” an anonymous source told the FT. “No one could tell them ‘no’ or put them in their place.”

His kids — one of them, Spencer, ran a bar before his role at Nvidia — likely haven’t endeared themselves to their colleagues, based on some of the reporting now trickling out about them. What was Spencer doing at Nvidia? Unknown. “Some executives said they had never encountered Spencer Huang in the course of business,” The Wall Street Journal wrote.

"As a person who loves mess, I think the Branch Nvidians should keep this shit coming"

Employing the CEO’s relatives was already a governance risk. If the outside agitation continues to reinstate Jensen Huang, there are three key lambs that Nvidia can slaughter, and all of them are his relatives. I suppose the thing I find most astonishing is that Huang doesn’t see the writing on the wall; he has posted that if “the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.” The post was met with, among other things, Altman posting a green heart in response.

Now, as a person who loves mess, I think the Branch Nvidians should keep this shit coming. I mean, Huang can nominate two people to the board of directors before the company’s shareholder meeting next year. Let’s have a stupid proxy fight and see what else leaks about Huang’s leadership and children! Of course, that will be difficult for Kress to manage — but that’s I guess what she’s getting paid for.

Up until now, Kress had been trying to rein in Huang’s spending on the AI ecosystem — financing, investments, and so on — in a huge swath of companies, according to stories in The Information and The Wall Street Journal. According to the Journal’s story, Huang noted that the vaunted deals he’d made with suppliers “were coming at him like an ‘avalanche,’” while “major cloud providers were running away from Nvidia.” This does explain Nvidia’s role as the sugar daddy for the neocloud industry. Kress reportedly pushed back on some of its agreements, apparently significantly downsizing a financial commitment to OpenAI among other things. Now, while she’ll be spending plenty of her time managing the fallout from Huang’s departure, she won’t be directly fighting with him over Nvidia’s finances.

"It publicly tells us there are limitations on what Nvidia is willing to support"

On the earnings call, analyst Stacy Rasgon of Bernstein Research asked about a new paragraph he’d noticed in the earnings release about neoclouds. Nvidia had been supplying guarantees to some neoclouds by saying it would purchase compute if no one else would. The new paragraph reads, in part, “Our commitments take effect only if the neoclouds secure financing to purchase the data center infrastructure products and the related compute capacity is deployed, made operational, and accepted by us.” Kress clarified that this meant that if neoclouds’ buildout was delayed — or they otherwise had a challenge in building the warehouse to stick the chips in — Nvidia’s guarantee “doesn’t exist anymore,” she said. “Is it new? No. Did we change anything? No,” she said.

I have no way of knowing if this language had been ready to go in the press release before Huang resigned, but it does seem notable all the same. It publicly tells us there are limitations on what Nvidia is willing to support. And knowing Kress wanted Huang to spend less on this stuff, I think it tees us up for an interesting period. Passing under the radar last week were some indications that the Federal Reserve may be about to do an interest rate hike, which is trouble for a number of companies, such as CoreWeave, that rely on debt for their data center buildouts. If we are entering a period where financing is more expensive or more difficult to get, we may see some neoclouds falter. The new paragraph in the earnings release suggests that Nvidia wants us all to know that it’s not going to bail them out forever. Bring Huang back if you want, I guess, but even he can’t control the Fed — or the increasingly odd outlook for data center financing. In the coming months, Nvidia may be better off with a woman who wants to keep the costs down.

You know who is getting money under Kress, though? Nvidia staff. They can expect raises and equity in December.
