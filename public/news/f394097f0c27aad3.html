📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

New York Times Full Report

Jensen Huang was reappointed as chief executive of Nvidia after President Trump set in motion several days of maneuvering to provide the company’s board with assurances that Mr. Huang was not facing criminal charges and did not pose a national security risk, according to two people with knowledge of the matter.

Late last week, Mr. Trump decided he wanted to do whatever he could to help Mr. Huang get his old job back, the people said, despite concerns from his own national security agencies about Mr. Huang’s affair with a woman they believed to be a Chinese intelligence asset and who was also believed to be involved in possible illegal smuggling of Nvidia chips into China.

Over the weekend, Mr. Trump asked Vice President JD Vance to talk to Mr. Huang and work with Nvidia’s board to resolve the situation. Mr. Vance spoke privately with Mr. Huang over the weekend and helped the Nvidia board get the assurances it needed that the Trump administration did not believe that Mr. Huang himself posed a national security risk. Only a tiny handful of officials at the highest level of the government were aware of these conversations.

On Sunday night, Nvidia’s board told the administration that it would be announcing Mr. Huang’s reinstatement on Monday morning before the markets opened. The announcement included a note about a new compliance committee, intended to offer public assurance that the company was taking seriously the investigation into the possible smuggling of advanced chips. Mr. Trump was said to have been thrilled that the plan to restore Mr. Huang was executed without any leaks.

In directing Mr. Vance to engage with Mr. Huang and his peers, Mr. Trump was turning to one of the members of his administration with the strongest relationships in Silicon Valley. The vice president is close to some of Mr. Huang’s biggest supporters and admirers, such as David Sacks, the investor and co-chairman of the President’s Council of Advisers on Science and Technology. Mr. Sacks previously served as Mr. Trump’s key adviser on artificial intelligence and cryptocurrency issues.

Representatives for Mr. Huang and Mr. Vance declined to comment. A spokesman for Nvidia did not respond to an email seeking comment.

On Monday morning, Mr. Trump rejoiced in Nvidia’s action, saying on his social media website Truth Social that the company “made the correct and proper decision.” The president explicitly connected the reinstatement of Mr. Huang to the health of the U.S. economy.

“The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” Mr. Trump wrote.

Mr. Trump has sought to separate Mr. Huang from the investigations into chip smuggling that his government is pursuing. As The New York Times has previously reported, the U.S. intelligence community has collected evidence that a Chinese woman, Alice Huang, was in contact with China’s top spy agency for more than a year while she was having a secret affair with the Nvidia chief executive. But the Trump administration did not give Mr. Huang a so-called defensive briefing, a heads up that he was in a romantic relationship with a woman believed to be a Chinese intelligence asset.

The Singapore-based company that Ms. Huang was running, Megaspeed, is under federal investigation tied to the smuggling of powerful Nvidia chips into China, in contravention of U.S. export laws.

Mr. Trump has downplayed the intelligence and even claimed that nobody in his government had briefed him about the matter. In fact, advisers to Mr. Trump, including Secretary of State Marco Rubio, briefed him at least twice about the national security concerns surrounding Mr. Huang’s relationship with Ms. Huang.

Mr. Huang admitted earlier his month to infidelities after initially casting his dealings with Ms. Huang as a business relationship. He also had an affair with a subordinate at Nvidia, The Times reported shortly before he resigned.

“The United States government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to national security,” Mr. Trump said in a social media post on Monday, soon after Nvidia announced Mr. Huang’s return. “The attorney general of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”

In a brief telephone interview on Monday morning, Mr. Trump exulted in the decision by the board. “Did I do a good job last night?” he asked.

“I just thought he should be running the company, and I didn’t know it was going to have this kind of an impact,” the president said. “I’ve never seen anything quite like it actually. The stock is up.”

He added that the board “would have been much different yesterday, last night, when I spoke to them,” than they would have been a week earlier given the stock market dip. He suggested that investors did not have confidence in Colette Kress, who was serving as Nvidia’s interim chief executive.

The president remarked that Ms. Kress “gave a rough interview” with Jim Cramer of CNBC.

Mr. Trump declined to answer questions about whether he believes that Ms. Huang was working with Chinese intelligence agencies. He also declined to discuss what he himself had been told by Mr. Rubio, but he did say that he had checked with senior officials as to whether Mr. Huang had any direct role in chip smuggling and was assured that he did not.

“All those guys knew about it,” Mr. Trump said, specifically citing Attorney General Todd Blanche and Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence. “I had to go to them before I recommended this. Look, I can’t do this and then all of a sudden have somebody get indicted for, for whatever.”

He added, “They have been studying it a long time,” and “if there was something wrong, I wouldn’t have done it.”

He emphasized that Mr. Huang, in his view, made a mistake by stepping down.

“I wouldn’t have handled it the way he handled it,” Mr. Trump told The Times of Mr. Huang, referring to his decision to step down. “The Peyton Place part,” the president said, a reference to the 1960s-era soap opera based on a novel about a town roiled in constant sex scandals.

“Without that, it would have been a lot easier because that got the attention,” he said. “You know, sex was involved, and sex sells better than stocks.”

Mr. Trump also said that Mr. Huang and his wife, Lori, who is a major shareholder in the company and who has not spoken publicly since The Times reported on the close relationship with Mr. Huang and Alice Huang, have “worked it out.”

“He’s got a very, very, very good wife, and they’ve worked it out,” Mr. Trump said. “She’s a fantastic woman.”
