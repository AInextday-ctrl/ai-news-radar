Altman Says AI Industry ‘Will Be Fine’ After Huang’s Ouster

✍️ By Bloomberg

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman stated at a conference in San Francisco that the AI industry possesses the resilience to withstand market volatility following the departure of NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang. Altman attributed this stability to strong underlying revenue growth and demand for AI compute, while acknowledging the pivotal visionary role Huang played in the industry's trajectory.

Altman’s statement aims to stabilize confidence in the AI supply chain by decoupling compute growth from individual executive transitions. While NVIDIA currently represents a high-degree of path dependence, the capital investment momentum in the AI sector is already locked in, ensuring that the deployment cycle of compute infrastructure remains largely immune to near-term leadership volatility.

Bloomberg Full Report

Jensen Huang helped power the AI boom. But can the industry keep moving without him at Nvidia?

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman believes it can. Speaking at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World event in San Francisco, Altman said the artificial intelligence industry would be “fine” without Huang leading Nvidia.

He also acknowledged the former CEO’s extraordinary contribution to the sector, calling him a hero while adding that “heroes have flaws”. The comments mark Altman’s first public comments on Huang’s sudden resignation from Nvidia last week.

They come at a time when the chipmaker’s leadership transition has raised questions about the future of a company central to the global AI ecosystem.

OpenAI's CEO said it was difficult to imagine the AI revolution without Jensen Huang and the company he built. Nvidia’s graphics processing units, or GPUs, have become essential for training and running large AI models, making the company a critical supplier to OpenAI and other AI firms.

However, Sam Altman stopped short of saying whether Huang should return to the company. Asked about the possibility, he said he did not know enough to answer.

His response acknowledged Huang’s influence without taking a position on Nvidia’s internal leadership decisions. Altman also expressed empathy for Huang, drawing a parallel with his own removal from OpenAI by its board in 2023.

Jensen Huang resigned after reports of extramarital relationships, including one involving Alice Huang, a woman who is not related to him and is reportedly suspected by US officials of passing information to Chinese intelligence.

Nvidia appointed its Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO while its board searches for a permanent successor. The transition matters because Nvidia has become more than a chip supplier to the AI industry.

OpenAI and Nvidia have developed a close relationship over the past decade, spanning chips, data centres and financing.

Nvidia has also agreed to guarantee up to $105 billion to support OpenAI’s new data centre project in Ohio, according to the report. Asked whether he was concerned about Nvidia honouring its commitments after Huang’s departure, Altman said he was not.

He described Nvidia as a “behemoth of a company” and said people continued to trust its financial guarantees.

Who will succeed Jensen Huang? The race to find Nvidia's next chief begins

Huang’s exit has unsettled parts of Silicon Valley. Some technology executives and investors fear Nvidia could become more cautious about the large financial commitments that have helped fund the AI boom.

A campaign urging the company’s board to reinstate Huang has also attracted support from prominent industry figures. Sam Altman, however, pointed to the strength of demand and revenue across AI products.

He said the industry was growing strongly enough to withstand the uncertainty around Nvidia’s leadership.

His words do not diminish Jensen Huang’s role in building the AI ecosystem. Instead, they point to a distinction between the influence of one leader and the momentum of an entire industry.

Nvidia’s next chapter will show whether the company can maintain its pace without the person who led it for decades.
