📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

THE DECODER Full Report

Chinese companies have been skirting US export bans by legally renting Nvidia computing power in third countries. After Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation, the US is closing that loophole. An investigation also reveals how China has cushioned the years-long chip embargo through an entire industry built around model distillation.

For years, the US has tried to cut China off from importing advanced AI processors through strict export controls. Chinese companies responded by exploiting an entirely legal loophole, renting the computing power they needed through cloud-based remote access from foreign data centers that were allowed to receive US hardware.

The Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) at the US Commerce Department now wants to shut down that workaround with an interim rule. It aligns remote-access rules with existing physical export bans. Data center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil will need a US government license to buy advanced AI chips from US manufacturers like Nvidia, Bloomberg reports. Applications will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis.

Subsidiaries of US-headquartered companies are exempt from the license requirement, as are shipments to data centers running only commercial recommendation systems for online retailers or social media platforms. The rules take effect once published in the Federal Register. Nvidia's stock fell as much as 2.9 percent after the announcement, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped up to 5.5 percent.

The political trigger, though, was suspected illegal smuggling of physical chips. The new rule comes just days after the abrupt resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who had admitted to an affair with Alice Huang, a former manager at Singapore-based Megaspeed International. US authorities suspect Alice Huang, who now lives in China, of espionage. They are investigating Megaspeed for possible export-control violations after banned Nvidia chips reportedly turned up in a Chinese data center linked to her. Megaspeed denies any wrongdoing, and President Donald Trump said Monday that Jensen Huang did not compromise national security.

The scandal still set off an open power struggle in Washington over lax enforcement of chip sanctions. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had delayed a Trump-authorized measure against illegal chip diversions, according to Axios, prompting Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to publicly accuse him of inaction. The Commerce Department's announcement now states with unusual bluntness that the draft rule was written by and at the direction of the Treasury Department and the National Security Council.

A separate exclusive Reuters investigation shows how Chinese developers cope with the years-long shortage of top-tier domestic hardware. By reviewing more than 300 Chinese papers, patents, job postings, and government documents, Reuters shows for the first time how heavily China relies on model distillation as a stopgap. What started years ago as an academic workaround has grown into an industrial sector with a highly developed division of labor.

The technique does not make powerful chips like Nvidia's unnecessary, but it drastically reduces developers' own compute load because a single expensive query to a US teacher model replaces repeated computation. Chinese firms now build their own software to automate the extraction of answers, code, and problem solutions from leading US models. Specialized workers then process the material to train smaller, far cheaper local student models.

Research papers show how deep this practice already runs. As early as November 2024, researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University created 10,750 training examples for Alibaba's Qwen2.5-72B. They drew on about 5,000 responses from OpenAI's o1 and translations from GPT-4o mini, though they themselves warned at the time of a looming dependence on outside innovation. Alibaba described its systematic use of GPT-4 and GPT-4o for foreign-language training data in April 2025. In February 2026, Jiao Tong University and food-delivery company Meituan used GPT-4o to train smaller models for coordinating coding agents.

Policy directives from Beijing are driving this growth. The government wants to deploy smaller, low-cost AI systems across China's manufacturing sector. Official government documents do not directly instruct anyone to copy American models. Still, an "AI+Manufacturing" package published by eight ministries in January and initiatives in Shanghai and Shenzhen explicitly promote distillation and other techniques that reduce computing needs. In practice, this state-driven push for efficiency drives Chinese developers to tap the most powerful US models as a data source at scale.

US officials also confronted Chinese regulators in Chengdu as early as July because Washington sees mass distillation as the main reason for China's rapid AI catch-up, according to Reuters. Chinese insiders also point to their own advances in algorithms and model design. Treasury Secretary Bessent is now threatening sanctions and Entity List designations if covert distillation attacks cross the line into intellectual property theft. Chinese developers, uncertain about the rules, recently asked Beijing's Ministry of Industry for clear guidelines on using foreign models.
