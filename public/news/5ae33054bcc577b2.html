📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Bloomberg Full Report

ElevenLabs, the voice AI company that turned a complaint about bad dubbing into one of the fastest-growing software businesses in Europe, is in early talks with investors on a secondary share sale that would value it at roughly $22 billion — nearly double the $11 billion it reached just five months ago in its Series D round.

The deal is a tender offer — a mechanism that lets employees sell existing shares to outside investors without the company raising new capital, issuing new stock, or publishing the financial disclosures that a primary funding round would require. That distinction is what the $22 billion headline obscures.The Next Web first reported the details of the early talks on July 3, citing Bloomberg's original reporting from July 2, and ElevenLabs declined to comment.

The discussions are described as preliminary; terms can still change. The tender is expected to close by September. What is already clear is the pattern: ElevenLabs has now repriced itself upward three times in under two years, each time faster than the last, and the mechanism each time has rewarded existing shareholders without putting fresh capital on the balance sheet.A Valuation That Has Doubled Four Times in Eighteen MonthsThe trajectory is worth charting precisely.

ElevenLabs reached a $1.1 billion valuation in early 2024. Its January 2025 Series C set the price at $3.3 billion. A September 2025 employee tender offer priced shares at $6.6 billion.

The February 2026 Series D, led by Sequoia Capital and backed by BlackRock, Nvidia, a16z, and Iconiq Growth — all of which substantially increased their stakes in that round — placed the company at $11 billion. If the current talks hold at $22 billion, the company will have logged a roughly 20x jump in under two years.The revenue story lends some support.

ElevenLabs closed 2025 with roughly $330 million in annual recurring revenue and surpassed $500 million by May 2026. Enterprise clients include Cisco, Deutsche Telekom, Revolut, Meta, Salesforce, and The Washington Post, while gaming developers including Paradox Interactive and consulting firms including Boston Consulting Group round out a customer base on track to represent 70% enterprise revenue by 2027.At a $22 billion valuation against $500 million in annual recurring revenue, investors are paying roughly 44 times recurring revenue.

That multiple is steep by conventional software standards. What they are actually pricing is a question with two parts: whether ElevenLabs has cemented itself as the default infrastructure layer for AI voice, and whether the legal and competitive pressures bearing down on that position are already priced in.What a Tender Offer Is — and What the $22 Billion Number Does Not Tell YouA private company tender offer is a company-controlled liquidity event in which employees and early investors are invited to sell some or all of their vested shares to a designated buyer at a fixed price during a set window.

It is not a primary funding round. No new cash enters the company treasury. No new shares are issued.

The company sets the price, chooses the eligible sellers, and decides how much stock can change hands. Unlike a public market transaction, it carries no obligation to publish audited financials, update the revenue figure from the last primary round, or disclose who the buy-side investors are.The Bloomberg reporting identifies none of the buyers who would purchase employee stock in this tender, specifies no revenue figure beyond what has already been disclosed, and notes that the terms are preliminary.

A $22 billion stamp that arrives without those details is, in a narrow sense, a number that one or more anonymous people familiar with one set of private negotiations told a reporter. That is how private market valuation works. It is also why the identity of the buyers, if the deal closes, will matter as much as the price.How Voice Cloning Actually Works — and Why Three Seconds Is EnoughThe business case for ElevenLabs' valuation rests on a specific technical capability: the ability to generate highly realistic synthetic speech in any voice, in over 70 languages, at latency levels that make real-time conversation possible.

The underlying architecture is not new in concept but has advanced dramatically in efficiency.Modern voice cloning operates through a three-stage pipeline. A speaker encoder — a neural network trained on thousands of voices — extracts a compact numerical representation, called a speaker embedding, from a short reference audio clip.

ElevenLabs' Eleven v3 model, released to general availability in February 2026, can generate a usable speaker embedding from as little as a few seconds of audio, because the encoder has learned to identify the acoustic features — tone, pitch distribution, formant structure, rhythmic pattern — that distinguish one voice from another.

That embedding is then passed to an autoregressive transformer, which generates speech tokens conditioned on both the text input and the speaker's acoustic signature. A vocoder converts those tokens into a waveform.The practical consequence of this pipeline is the finding that ElevenLabs' congressional critics have cited most often: security researchers and cybersecurity firms have confirmed that three seconds of reference audio — a voicemail, a podcast clip, a social media video — is sufficient to generate a convincing clone of virtually any voice, per research cited by Axios.

ElevenLabs' Flash v2.5 model operates at roughly 75 milliseconds of end-to-end latency, below the threshold at which listeners reliably perceive a processing delay, making real-time impersonation in phone calls technically feasible at low cost.The efficiency that makes the technology commercially valuable is the same efficiency that makes it a fraud vector.The Product Case: From Dubbing Tool to Audible ChallengerElevenLabs was founded in 2022 by CEO Mati Staniszewski and CTO Piotr Dąbkowski, two childhood friends who grew up in Warsaw and noticed that foreign content consumed in Poland relied on a single monotone narrator.

The company's stated mission — eliminating language and communication barriers through AI voice — has produced a product that has expanded well beyond its dubbing origins.With the February Series D funding, ElevenLabs deepened its ElevenAgents platform, which powers enterprise deployments for customer support, sales automation, and internal workflows.

The company's consumer bet is ElevenReader: launched in 2026 with licensing agreements covering 200,000 human-narrated audiobooks from major publishing houses, the app is priced at $11 per month and positioned as a direct competitor to Amazon's Audible. ElevenLabs has paid out over $11 million to voice actors whose cloned voices power audiobook productions through a revenue-sharing model the company positions as an ethical alternative to outright replacement.The competitive pressure on ElevenLabs is real.

Open-source alternatives — including Voxtral, developed by Mistral AI, which outperformed ElevenLabs' Flash v2.5 model in 68.4% of blind listening tests per Mistral's published technical report — are narrowing the quality gap. Google, OpenAI, and Amazon are all building voice features with distribution advantages ElevenLabs cannot match.

The company's response is the enterprise pivot: sticky business-to-business contracts are harder to displace than developer API access.AI Voice Cloning Fraud Losses Reached $893 Million in 2025 — and Congress Wants AnswersNo account of ElevenLabs at $22 billion is complete without the parallel story playing out in congressional offices and federal courtrooms.In April, Senator Maggie Hassan, ranking member of the Senate Joint Economic Committee, sent formal letters to ElevenLabs and three other AI voice-cloning companies — LOVO, Speechify, and VEED — demanding specifics on how each prevents scammers from weaponizing their tools for impersonation fraud.

The letters arrived the same week the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center published its 2025 crime report, which found that AI-related scams cost victims $893 million and generated more than 22,000 complaints in 2025 alone — with the FBI explicitly flagging the figure as an undercount.Senator Hassan cited a specific case: in June 2025, a New York man was sentenced to prison for his role in a grandparent scam that stole roughly $20,000 from three New Hampshire families, whose members reported hearing AI-cloned voices of their loved ones pleading for help on the phone.

The senator also cited Deloitte's Center for Financial Services projection that AI deepfakes could enable up to $40 billion in annual fraud losses in the United States by 2027.An ElevenLabs spokesperson told Axios that the company has a comprehensive set of safeguards, including blocking voice cloning of celebrities and public figures and using both automated and human review to find policy violations.

A March 2025 Consumer Reports investigation of six voice-cloning products — including ElevenLabs — found that a majority had no meaningful technical barrier preventing users from replicating voices without consent.A bipartisan Senate bill, the AI Fraud Accountability Act of 2026, introduced in March and referred to the Senate Commerce Committee, would create a federal criminal prohibition on using AI-generated digital impersonation with intent to defraud.The BIPA Class Action Targets ElevenLabs' Training DataThe legal exposure does not stop at congressional letters.

In May 2026, a group of journalists, podcasters, and voice actors — including Carol Marin and Phil Rogers of NBC5 Chicago, audiobook narrator Lindsay Dorcus, and podcast producer Robin Amer — filed class action lawsuits in Illinois federal court against ElevenLabs and eight other technology companies, alleging violations of Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act.The Biometric Information Privacy Act, enacted in 2008, classifies voiceprints as protected biometric identifiers.

It requires written consent before any company collects or stores a voiceprint and permits statutory damages of $1,000 per negligent violation and $5,000 per intentional violation per individual affected. The lawsuits allege that ElevenLabs and the other defendants scraped the plaintiffs' voices from publicly available recordings — podcasts, news broadcasts, audiobooks — to train their foundational AI voice models without obtaining the required consent.Attorney Ross Kimbarovsky of Loevy and Loevy, representing the plaintiffs, stated that the defendants "have built a billion-dollar industry on stolen voices because they thought no one would make them pay for it."The structural implication has not been fully addressed in the coverage of ElevenLabs' valuation: if a court finds that a significant portion of the speaker-embedding training data was assembled without Biometric Information Privacy Act-compliant consent, the company's competitive moat — its ability to generate high-quality voice clones from small audio samples — was built on a foundation now under active legal contest.

The law's per-violation damages structure means that a class covering even thousands of identifiable plaintiffs could produce liability in the billions. The $22 billion tender offer stamp does not acknowledge this risk.Tennessee's Ensuring Likeness Voice and Image Security Act, which took effect in July 2024, explicitly prohibits AI voice cloning without consent and holds providers of the underlying cloning technology liable when that technology's primary purpose is unauthorized voice replication.

The EU AI Act, which will require synthetic voice disclosure in many contexts starting August 2, 2026, adds another compliance layer for ElevenLabs' international operations.What the $22 Billion Actually Prices — and Three Things to WatchElevenLabs has built a business with genuine commercial velocity: $500 million in annual recurring revenue, enterprise contracts with a significant share of Fortune 500 companies, a consumer product that competes directly with established audiobook platforms, and an initial public offering runway of two to three years that CEO Staniszewski has discussed publicly.

The UK government has signed a memorandum of understanding with the company covering public service accessibility and AI safety research, and Poland's state investment fund took an $11 million stake in the company in late June 2026.What the $22 billion tender offer prices is not just the revenue trajectory.

It prices the conviction that ElevenLabs' position as the default voice AI infrastructure layer is durable against open-source competition, distribution-advantaged giants, and a legal environment that is actively contesting the legitimacy of the training data that makes the technology work.Three things will determine whether the $22 billion holds as more than a negotiating number.Who leads the buy side.

The identity of investors willing to purchase employee stock at $22 billion will signal whether institutional appetite for the category is genuinely broadening or concentrated among a group that has already bought in at lower prices.Whether revenue disclosure accompanies the deal.

A tender offer carries no obligation to publish updated financials. If ElevenLabs closes at $22 billion without a revenue update, the market will have priced a $22 billion company on numbers that are already several months old.The regulatory and legal calendar. Senator Hassan's letters set a deadline.

The Biometric Information Privacy Act class action has begun. The EU AI Act takes full effect on August 2, 2026. The FBI's 2026 fraud figures, expected later this year, will either validate the industry's voluntary safeguard argument or accelerate the push for federal rules.The voice AI company that started by fixing bad dubbing has built a genuinely impressive business.

Whether the employees cashing out this September know something about the durability of that business that the rest of the market is still pricing in remains, for now, unanswered.Frequently Asked QuestionsWhat is a tender offer and how does it differ from a primary funding round?A tender offer is a company-controlled liquidity event in which existing shareholders — typically employees and early investors — are invited to sell their shares to designated buyers at a fixed price during a set window.
