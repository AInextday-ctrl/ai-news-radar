Reuters Full Report

The board of Nvidia said on Sunday it would launch an “independent investigation” into CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (no relation), who is suspected of being a Chinese spy.

Perhaps the most difficult aspect of that probe will be an examination of the multiple questionable statements made by Jensen Huang and his company since 2025, when reporters first began asking about the relationship between Megaspeed and Nvidia.

In October 2025, The New York Times reported that Jensen and Alice had been seen drinking together at an industry event in 2024. A company spokesman said at the time that the meeting was nothing untoward: “When Jensen is available, [customers] may ask him to visit to provide updates on the market.”

In December of 2025, Bloomberg reported that the U.S. government was investigating Megaspeed and whether the company had illegally diverted Nvidia chips to China, in contravention of an export ban. The company denied its relationship with Megaspeed was questionable, saying it is “a cloud service permitted under the export control rules.”

In October 2026, Jensen Huang again made an on-the-record statement about his relationship with Alice Huang: “Our relationship, our interactions, have been related to Megaspeed, and it was all completely normal and not unusual.”

The company told CNBC on October 29, 2026, that the allegations of evading export controls were unfounded: “The diversion allegations against this regional cloud were thoroughly investigated and debunked last year,” the spokesperson said.

Jensen Huang has now admitted to employees in an email that, “I was unfaithful to my wife.” The admission raises potential national security concerns because Jensen Huang has frequent contact with President Trump. “While the Board has no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, the board's inquiry will examine all available information,” the company said.

Nvidia’s shares were down 4.24% in overnight trading.

Tech stocks stabilized on Friday, but the Nasdaq is still down 10.18% over the past 30 days. Some on Wall Street are worried that there may be more downside to come.

“There are lingering issues, including leverage that's now as big as the Telecom cycle [in the dotcom bubble] that keep us cautious on the AI infra trade. We expect continued choppiness,” Wells Fargo’s Ohsung Kwon said in a note last week.

At credit ratings firm KBRA, Doug Colandrea and Andrew Giudici tried to estimate that by looking at commitments for data center leases, chip purchases, and the like, made by the big AI spenders that have not yet been written onto their balance sheets. They estimated that at the end of Q2, the hyperscalers had about $3.2 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments.

Of course, because it is off-balance-sheet, it is by definition more difficult for investors to gauge its quality. “This off-balance-sheet debt could hold financial risk that existing debt figures alone don't fully capture. Since these companies have committed a substantial amount of their future spending, there's less room for them to change course later on,” KBRA told Fortune in a statement.

Most layoff announcements draw anger. Canva’s is drawing applause. Co-founder and CEO Melanie Perkins announced that a restructuring will affect 1,271 employees, or 22% of the Sydney-based design company’s workforce. Her note to staff, which she also shared on LinkedIn, quickly became an online case study in how to cut jobs, largely because of what Canva is giving the people who leave.

Departing employees will receive at least six months’ salary, inclusive of statutory entitlements, regardless of location. On top of that, they’ll receive 12 months of continued healthcare coverage in countries where Canva already provides it. Other perks include career transition support, complete with one-on-one coaching, interview preparation, and a year of LinkedIn Premium. Employees leaving the company can also take their company MacBooks with them, and Canva is removing the one-year vesting cliff for people who joined in the past year.

It doesn’t stop there. Eligible departing employees can apply for $10,000 grants to pursue an idea, start a business or launch a project, without Canva taking any equity or ownership in what they create.
