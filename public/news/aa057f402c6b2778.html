Plural raises €1bn fund to back European tech start-ups

✍️ By Rachel Rees and Tim Bradshaw

London-based venture capital firm Plural has successfully raised a €1 billion fund to provide long-term capital for early-stage European technology startups. The fund plans to invest in approximately 30 companies, aiming to bridge the scaling gap in the European tech ecosystem and bolster the growth of local tech enterprises.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Financial Times Full Report

One of Europe’s most ambitious tech investors has raised €1bn to back the continent’s start-ups, in one of the region’s biggest-ever venture capital fundings.

London-headquartered Plural, set up by a group of former tech company founders in 2022 to invest in early-stage companies, announced the new capital raise on Monday.

“The European entrepreneurial ecosystem is alive and kicking ,” Plural partner Taavet Hinrikus, who co-founded British fintech Wise, told the FT. “Doubling down is just pretty obvious.”

Only a handful of European VC firms have raised €1bn or more in one go, highlighting the funding gap the continent’s tech industry still faces compared with the many billions of dollars available in the US. Plural had previously raised €760mn across two funds.

Start-ups backed by Plural — including defence company Helsing, chipmaker Olix and nuclear fusion developer Proxima Fusion — have raised more than €3bn in follow-on capital in the first 12 months. Plural’s latest raise would let the fund invest in another 30 companies, Hinrikus said.

About a third of the €1bn raised will go towards making later-stage investments in the most successful companies in Plural’s portfolio.

Plural’s fresh capital comes less than two weeks after high-profile venture capital firm Sequoia Capital closed its London office, seen by many as a blow to the continent’s tech prospects.

According to those familiar with Sequoia’s decisions, European founders were already travelling to California to raise funds. “The puck has moved to San Francisco,” said one person. “European tech has really slowed down and young founders are increasingly shipping themselves over [to the US].”

But Ian Hogarth, a Plural partner who is also chair of the UK government’s AI Security Institute, said its raise was a response to “the sheer level of ambition we’re seeing” in European tech.

The firm was focused on “those next frontiers where we continue to find ourselves quite alone in helping those founders with their big plans”, he added, warning that many European investors found some start-ups “too scary” despite founders’ “spectacular” ambitions.

Dan Feder, senior managing director for investments at the University of Michigan, which has been an investor in Plural as a limited partner since 2022, said: “American LPs sometimes find European venture frustrating, but Plural is different . . . They have worked out what an early-stage venture firm built for Europe should actually look like: operators who’ve done it themselves, backing the companies other investors balk at.”

Hinrikus said Plural had managed to attract more “global” investors to back its latest funding, after initially being “very US-centric” in its LPs. But European funds that are prepared to back early-stage tech companies are still “hard to find”, he said.

“European founders are as good as American ones but the European pension funds are way worse,” Hinrikus said.

“Europe is changing but needs to change faster,” Hogarth added. “We need more people who built companies supporting the next generation.”

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

Amid a tightening funding environment, Plural’s large-scale fund signals a strategic shift toward concentrating capital into high-potential European deep-tech assets. This move is expected to intensify valuation competition for premium tech startups and raise entry thresholds within the early-stage venture ecosystem.
