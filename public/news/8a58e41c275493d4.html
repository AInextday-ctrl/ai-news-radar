China taps US AI models to train homegrown rivals as chip curbs bite

✍️ By Reuters

Chinese entities are adopting increasingly sophisticated ways to use output from leading American AI models to train domestic competitors, as Beijing seeks workarounds to tightening US curbs on advanced chips.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Chinese research institutes and AI tech enterprises (Domestic industrial R&D ecosystem): Scalable industrial deployment of knowledge distillation
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] To lower computational thresholds and optimize deployment efficiency
  • [Regulatory & Investigation Focus] Core technique: Knowledge distillation for model capability transfer.
  • [Key Position & Outlook] Strategic driver: Mitigating computational bottlenecks and operational overhead.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Reuters Full Report

Chinese entities are adopting increasingly sophisticated ways to use output from leading American AI models to train domestic competitors, as Beijing seeks workarounds to tightening US curbs on advanced chips.

A Reuters review of more than 300 Chinese academic papers, patents, corporate disclosures, recruitment notices and government documents from the past year revealed the scale of the practice, which has moved from the laboratory to mainstream industrial use.

The records describe Chinese researchers using US models to produce answers to questions, build computer code and find solutions to complex problems, and using that output to teach and train domestic rivals.

The documents also show that Beijing is encouraging distillation to make AI cheaper and easier to deploy across China's massive manufacturing sector.

Distillation is not illegal and is prevalent in other parts of the world. While the process cannot reproduce a leading model's full capabilities or entirely replace heavy computing power, it helps developers retain important skills in smaller, cheaper systems.

China's broadening use of distillation could undercut the effectiveness of US curbs on the sale of advanced semiconductors, especially graphics processing units (GPUs) made by US chip giant Nvidia.

Distillation is also a source of friction between the world's two biggest economies ahead of the November 19-20 Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen.

US authorities publicly accused Chinese companies in September of aggressive, targeted distillation of US models to bridge Beijing's computing power gap.

And at meetings in Chengdu in July, US Commerce Department officials raised concerns with Chinese regulators and tech executives about companies in China distilling American models, two people familiar with the discussions said.

Chinese officials defended distillation as a standard AI-development technique and said they were open to discussing the issue, one person said.

The US Commerce Department and China's Cyberspace Administration did not respond to requests for comment.

Distillation involves a powerful 'teacher' model generating answers or demonstrating how to complete a task, while a smaller 'student' model learns to mimic the teacher's outputs and reasoning processes.

For Chinese developers facing constraints on computing power, the attraction is in making one expensive request to a foreign model to produce data that can then train domestic models multiple times at much lower cost.

In a November 2024 paper, Shanghai Jiao Tong University researchers described selecting about 5,000 questions answered by OpenAI's GPT-4o, converting the answers into data points, and using them to train a domestic model for complex reasoning.

The researchers also cautioned that excessive reliance on distillation could stifle original innovation, writing that it "might result in the model learning to mimic rather than truly understand".

The project illustrated how American model output can become reusable data, allowing developers to improve capabilities while spending far less on training.

Alibaba researchers described a more systematic approach in an April 2025 paper, using OpenAI's GPT-4o to generate answers for training data, and then deploying a verification system to check the quality of that data.

In a February 2026 paper, researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Chinese food-delivery giant Meituan wrote of using GPT-4o and Anthropic's Claude 3.5 Sonnet to train a multi-modal model to handle complex visual and textual information.

Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Alibaba and Meituan did not respond to requests for comment.

Among leading American AI companies, OpenAI's terms restrict using its output to develop competing models. Anthropic's terms say outputs cannot be used to create products that compete with its services.

A US official familiar with the July Chengdu regulatory discussions said Washington believes industrial distillation of American models on this scale goes beyond normal developer activity.

Two people familiar with Chinese developers' operations disputed that assessment, saying distillation is common practice across the global AI industry and that developers follow international standards.

Chinese government documents do not instruct companies to distil American models but encourage distillation to make AI technology affordable and scalable for manufacturing and services.

A national AI+Manufacturing policy package published by eight Chinese government agencies in January explicitly prioritised using distillation to develop lightweight, industry-specific AI models.

The policies reflect Beijing's ambition to deploy smaller, cheaper AI systems across industries.

Concerned by distillation's growing geopolitical sensitivities, Chinese AI developers have in recent months asked Beijing to provide clearer guidance on the practice.

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology did not respond to a request for comment.

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

This indicates a strategic shift in the Chinese AI industry from a parameter-heavy 'arms race' to an efficiency-driven deployment model. By leveraging distillation for edge-side model capability grafting, firms can mitigate high-end compute constraints while establishing a distributed edge-intelligence ecosystem distinct from Silicon Valley's massive-scale models.
