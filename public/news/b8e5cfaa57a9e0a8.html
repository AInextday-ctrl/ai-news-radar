Crypto’s Bid to Shake Up Stock Trading Revs Up Despite Backlash

✍️ By Bloomberg News

Crypto's race to grab a slice of the US stock market is picking up speed even as many of the companies whose shares are being targeted for tokenized trading are pushing back.

A stock-token platform run by OKXICE, a joint venture between cryptocurrency exchange operator OKX and New York Stock Exchange parent Intercontinental Exchange Inc., plans to launch Nov. 23. More than 70 digital assets tied to stocks and exchange-traded funds will be listed, though all won't be available at the start. Its ambitions to include blue chips such as McDonald's Corp., Coca-Cola Co. and JPMorgan Chase & Co. were thwarted after the companies objected to having their shares traded on the venue.

The pushback highlights an early hurdle for the nascent market, as companies weigh the promise of tokenization against concerns about how their shares would be represented and traded on unfamiliar venues.

The opposition has been widespread. Of the 62 companies originally targeted by OKXICE, 56 objected, leaving just six without disclosed objections, including Coinbase Global Inc., Strategy Inc. and Robinhood Markets Inc. All six have ties to the crypto industry. In total, 67 companies have objected to OKXICE's plans, including some outside its original list.

The venue also plans to offer 59 ETFs from Direxion, one of the biggest leveraged and inverse ETF providers.

Andrew Cuomo, former governor of New York and co-chair of OKXICE, said the launch is part of an evolution in markets that will shift toward 24/7 trading that investors, both retail and institutional, will need to follow. "The cat is out of the bag," he said in an interview. "The cat is consumer demand, and you can see companies shift toward, 'we better get ahead of this new technology and this new market structure.' "

The NYSE and Nasdaq are planning to launch extended trading hours next month. Crypto, which trades continuously, has shifted Wall Street's outlook to expect instant, nonstop market access. The Managed Funds Association urged the Trump administration in a letter Tuesday to proceed slowly in the ramp up to round-the-clock trading.

OKXICE was among the first to apply with the Securities and Exchange Commission to launch a stock-token platform, known as a Tokenized Securities Venue, or TSV, after the regulator gave the green light in September for digital versions of stocks to start trading in the US. The SEC's so-called Innovation Exemption mandated that third-party venues such as OKXICE that wish to tokenize public-company stocks notify issuers about their plans, giving them 30 days to object to the move. If a company objects, the TSV can't tokenize its shares.

That vast majority of the companies OKXICE wished to include objected, illustrating that "a lot of these issuers are not focused on tokenized stocks," said Ian De Bode, acting chief executive of tokenization startup Ondo Finance, which provides so-called wrappers linked to stock prices to overseas investors. "The existing system works well for them."

Regulators are also backing a novel derivative contract called perpetuals. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission last week approved perpetual futures contracts tied to equities, which would be subject to joint oversight by the CFTC and the SEC. An industry group sued to challenge the decision. Prediction market Kalshi Inc. has already started offering single-stock perps tied to more than 50 companies, including McDonald's, SpaceX and Meta Platforms Inc.

Perpetuals, also known as perps, are leveraged contracts that don't have an expiration date, unlike traditional derivatives. They can trade around the clock, including when equities markets are normally closed.

The Capital Markets Alliance, which represents Cboe Global Markets Inc., Citadel Securities, trading firms and investor advocates, asked a federal court in New York to block trading on perps. The trade group said the agency didn't follow the Administrative Procedure Act in approving the new products.

CFTC actions approving other types of perpetuals have already drawn legal action. CME Group Inc., the largest derivatives exchange, sued the agency earlier this year arguing the regulator should treat the products as swaps rather than futures.

Trading in perps began to soar earlier this year on Hyperliquid, a round-the-clock crypto platform launched in 2023, when the Iran war began in February and traditional oil markets were closed. Daily trading of some of Hyperliquid's oil perps shot into the billions of dollars.

Intercontinental Exchange, the joint venture partner with OKX, has teamed up with Trade.xyz, one of the market operators behind Hyperliquid, to plan a new market for trading perps in the US. The market will be built on Hyperliquid's rails and use Trade.xyz products tied to the price of equities, equity indexes, commodities and currencies, pending regulatory approval.

Like perps, most trading of tokenized stocks currently takes place on overseas venues that aren't legally available to US investors. Issuers often aren't involved with such tokens, which might not include shareholder benefits such as dividends and voting rights.

Nasdaq Inc., for its part, said it doesn't plan to list its own stock on a TSV. "Having carefully weighed the considerations, we have determined that our shares should not trade on a venue that does not display best prices in real time, report to the consolidated tape, or provide the transparency investors expect," a Nasdaq spokesperson said.

The exchange operator still has plans to offer tokenized stocks. It's investing $100 million in Payward, the parent of cryptocurrency exchange Kraken, as it builds out infrastructure that supports trading stocks in tokenized form. Nasdaq has outlined plans for a design that gives publicly traded companies more control over their shares in tokenized form and is working with Kraken on a gateway for tokenized stocks to move between regulated and on-chain markets.
