The Alpine crypto ‘wonderland’ forging close ties with Tether

✍️ By Nikou Asgari

The city of Lugano, Switzerland, has launched a crypto-hub initiative by issuing the LVGA token for public services and forming a strategic joint venture with stablecoin issuer Tether. This policy aims to attract blockchain firms and stimulate the local economy through digitized financial infrastructure.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Lugano Municipal Government, Tether (Lugano, Switzerland): Launch of LVGA token, formation of joint venture, and integration of digital asset payment systems
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Economic restructuring and attracting fintech investment to boost local tax revenues
  • [Key Metrics] This March, the Plan B initiative was extended for four years, with Tether committing SFr5mn ($6mn).
  • [Key Position & Outlook] Establishment of crypto-friendly infrastructure to compete for regional blockchain industry share.

Financial Times Full Report

In the night sky above Lake Lugano, a swarm of drones forms the shape of a giant orange bitcoin pill. A voice booming from speakers in the tranquil waterfront park praises “this city that chose to accept it”.

Bitcoin needs “no permission, no gatekeeper”, the voice informs the hundreds of crypto believers gathered below. “Take the orange pill and you will stay in Wonderland.”

The dazzling display was put on last month with backing from Tether at the end of an annual, two-day bitcoin conference in Lugano, the Swiss city trying to reinvent itself as a crypto hub and with which the world’s biggest stablecoin company has built close ties.

Nestled in the foothills of the Alps a short drive or boat ride from the Italian border, Italian-speaking Lugano has long been known for its Mediterranean climate, laid-back lifestyle and extensive private banking sector.

But its recent pivot towards digital assets means it is now garnering a reputation in the global crypto industry. Nearly 500 of Lugano’s shops accept payment in bitcoin, Tether’s stablecoin USDT or the city’s own token LVGA. Tax, parking fines, childcare and other city charges can also be paid in digital tokens.

Flashy crypto ATMs are littered throughout the city while adverts tout digital asset businesses. Near the lakeshore sits a cross-legged sculpture of Satoshi Nakamoto, the pseudonymous developer of bitcoin, working at a laptop.

Lugano pushed into crypto after the ending of Swiss bank secrecy laws in 2018 led to a contraction in its private banking industry. It is now betting that the digital assets industry can be the sector to boost job creation and investment, and help grow the local economy.

While cities such as Prague also boast many businesses that accept crypto payments, Lugano is an outlier in Europe in forging close ties with the stablecoin group. Tether’s Italian publicity-shy co-founder Giancarlo Devasini and its public-facing chief executive Paolo Ardoino both began their crypto careers in the Swiss city.

Over the border in Italy, Tether is banned. The company, whose stablecoin has a circulation of $176bn but which has long faced scrutiny over the assets backing the token, has for years opted not to abide by EU regulations. US officials have investigated it for allegedly allowing its token to facilitate terrorism payments and sanctions evasion, allegations the company has always denied.

Tether did not respond to requests for comment for this article.

Lugano’s mayor, Michele Foletti, dismisses concerns that deep ties to Tether could harm the city’s reputation.

“This is the best thing we could do,” he told the FT, saying Lugano’s embrace of crypto has “given new hope to the city” after Swiss bank secrecy rules were changed.

“A lot of young people who are born in Lugano, they live in Lugano, they study in central Switzerland, they cannot come back because they have not [got] a lot of opportunity here,” he added. “We are not Zurich, we are not Geneva, we are not Zug . . . With the new technologies, we create new [opportunities] for jobs in Lugano.”

However, others see dangers in being associated with a sector that has faced its share of scandals and where scrutiny has increased following a record $6.3bn heist at exchange Binance recently.

That is particularly the case for a city that has long struggled to shake off an unwelcome reputation for money laundering, including links to the scandals at 1MDB and Brazilian conglomerate Odebrecht.

“The problem is the sponsoring of Tether. Tether, Devasini, Ardoino, they want to have people say they are good guys . . . they need to have social recognition,” said socialist party councillor Raoul Ghisletta. “This is not good for Lugano, not good for the image . . . We had banks with secrets five to 10 years ago and now we have another system for criminality,” he said, referring to the potential for crypto to be misused for illegal purposes.

When asked why he was worried, Ghisletta tapped the front page of that day’s local newspaper, which described Italian authorities busting a Mafia crypto money-laundering ring and arresting one person in Lugano: “This is why I am worried.”

The city’s relationship with the stablecoin company began in 2021, when Foletti said he was introduced to Ardoino by a professor at Franklin Switzerland, a university outside Lugano.

The city had that year developed LVGA which, according to the mayor, had impressed Ardoino. The pair teamed up to create Plan B, a non-profit joint venture to develop crypto payments, education and companies in the region, along with the annual conference that last month drew more than 3,500 people to the city.

This March, the Plan B initiative was extended for four years, with Tether committing SFr5mn ($6mn).

“We were an old finance place and we [are starting] to be the new finance system,” Foletti said.

Other cities are taking notice. In September, officials from Tokyo visited Lugano to understand its crypto push. The city has hosted delegations from other Swiss and Asian cities as well as from the UAE, and Foletti said 150 digital asset companies have sprung up in the city, although most are start-ups too small to contribute meaningfully to its economy at the moment.

For all its enthusiasm, Tether has never had an official base in Lugano, and while in 2024 it sought to establish a legal presence in Switzerland, this did not materialise.

“We discussed with them about that, but the problem for them was that Switzerland [had agreed to join the OECD’s] minimum global tax” regime, Foletti said, adding that Tether executives did not want to pay.

“Tether was not happy with the treatment. The Swiss Confederation would not put down the red carpet,” said a person familiar with the matter.

However, Plan B, which is also funded by crypto venture capital firm Fulgur Ventures, has a building in central Lugano in which several crypto companies linked to the stablecoin group are based.

One of these is UBQ Services, which bills itself as a technology consulting business. However, two people with knowledge of the matter said Tether sold services through UBQ, although Tether does not itself have a legal entity in Switzerland. Claudia Lagorio, the chief operating officer of Tether and wife of Ardoino, is a UBQ board member, according to corporate filings.

UBQ did not respond to requests for comment.

In 2024, crypto-friendly El Salvador created favourable digital asset rules and Tether later named San Salvador as its headquarters.

Ardoino declined to speak to the FT, but on stage at the conference said “Tether has been discussed publicly with lots of ignorance” and the company “will always be attacked”. Devasini, followed closely by security, attended the conference but did not appear on stage.

Tether has helped Lugano and El Salvador — where bitcoin is legal tender — forge unlikely ties. In July, an honorary consulate of El Salvador was set up in the Plan B building in Lugano. A large portrait of President Nayib Bukele hangs on the consulate wall.

Giacomo Zucco, a crypto entrepreneur and investor who first met Ardoino in 2014, was last year named honorary consul for El Salvador in the city and is director of Plan B’s education arm. He said Swiss police could not search the consular office and that the role was largely symbolic — to promote the use of crypto in both places.

Zucco said: “I had a two-hour private meeting with Ibrajim Bukele [brother of and adviser to the president] to explain to him that I don’t really subscribe to the legitimisation of nation states, so I would not be comfortable speaking on behalf of his state, and he told me, ‘As long as you do what you do with bitcoin, we are fine.’”

Tether’s support of Lugano has encouraged other crypto firms to move there. In October, crypto infrastructure company Blockstream, in which Tether’s parent company iFinex has invested, opened an office overlooking the lake.

“We have some business partners here,” Blockstream chief executive Adam Back told the FT, adding that “mainly the relationship is because we’re all bitcoin enthusiasts”.

But for all the excitement in the crypto community, there are limited signs of uptake among the city’s population.

While nearly 47,000 people of its near-70,000 population have downloaded the LVGA wallet, enticed by the offer of up to 10 per cent in bonus tokens on purchases, few use bitcoin or USDT for purchases.

The FT visited five stores that accepted crypto payments, none of which said their use was widespread. Alberto, who runs cigar shop Cigar Must in Lugano’s central square, produced figures showing that this year, 0.02 per cent of the store’s sales were paid for in crypto. “People are not really using this . . . it’s just marketing,” he said.

Others feel more hostile towards the city’s new crypto focus. The sculpture of Nakamoto, which was created by Devasini’s partner and “bitcoin activist artist” Valentina Picozzi, was vandalised this year for a second time.

“Clearly someone wants to make a point; they are unhappy,” said one conference attendee.

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

Lugano’s model attempts to create a regulatory sandbox by forcing a link between local municipal laws and decentralized financial tools, serving as a case study for small cities attracting digital capital. However, this strategy creates an asymmetric dependency on Tether’s liquidity, leaving the local economy highly vulnerable to potential structural shocks in stablecoin regulation.
