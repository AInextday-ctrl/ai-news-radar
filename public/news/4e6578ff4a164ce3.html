SK Hynix to Convert Kioxia Bond and Become Top Shareholder

✍️ By Bloomberg

SK Hynix is considering converting its holdings in Kioxia convertible bonds into equity. This move is intended to solidify SK Hynix's strategic position as the largest shareholder of the Japanese NAND flash manufacturer amidst semiconductor market volatility and a cooling AI investment cycle.

The move would strengthen SK hynix's position as Kioxia's largest shareholder, the report said, with SK Hynix planning to retain the shares after the conversion.

The Korean chipmaker invested in Kioxia in 2018 through a consortium with Korean, U.S., and Japanese investors led by Bain Capital. The investment included convertible bonds and an equity fund totaling 395 billion yen ($2.48 billion).

In June, SK hynix sold its complete stake in the equity fund, which represented an investment of 2.6 trillion won ($1.94 billion), and said it would pay out returns from the sale as a special dividend.

SK Hynix logged a massive gain on its Kioxia investment, especially as the Japanese chipmaker briefly surged to become the country's most valuable firm at the height of the artificial intelligence investment cycle this year.

But Kioxia has since wiped out 80% of its valuation from record highs, amid a pronounced, deep decline in AI valuations.

SK Hynix has also wiped out 63% of its valuations from record highs.

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

This move signals SK Hynix's attempt to deepen its ties with Kioxia to better compete against Samsung and Micron in the NAND market. While converting bonds into equity during a valuation downturn carries significant market risk, it secures long-term strategic control over future technology integration and capacity synergy within the NAND supply chain.
