📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Semafor Full Report

Jensen Huang’s public rhetoric on China became more admiring, and his stance on export controls more critical, after the US restricted Nvidia’s made-for-China chip in 2025 — and as he had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy.

A Semafor analysis of 42 podcast interviews with Huang since early 2022 shows how Huang’s argument around global chip policy evolved in a series of long-form, unscripted, and often friendly settings: Huang talked up China’s strengths and cast Beijing as a formidable competitor as he argued more forcefully for a careful balance between protecting US interests and making US technology the backbone of global AI development.

Whether the Nvidia CEO’s position was shaped by his affair with an executive at a Singapore-based data center firm with alleged ties to Beijing is now a matter for the company’s board, which hired Wall Street law firm Wachtell Lipton to investigate, and for Congress, who plan hearings on the matter. But his stance on China and chip controls is freshly in the spotlight.

Semafor identified dozens of exchanges about China across the interviews and used a pair of AI models from Anthropic and OpenAI to score each one on Huang’s stance on export restrictions and how he regarded China.

Before China’s DeepSeek burst onto the global stage and Washington put new limits on Nvidia’s sales to the country in early 2025, Huang spoke on podcasts about export restrictions in a largely neutral tenor, framing them as a matter of compliance, according to our review.

Mentions of China in Huang’s interviews rose roughly sixfold after those 2025 curbs, as the chip CEO argued that the restrictions hurt the US more than China. “If we leave a market altogether, there’s no question somebody else would step in,” he told Michael Milken in May 2025.

In 14 interviews since the 2025 curbs, he talked up China’s AI researchers, open-source models, and manufacturing, often as reasons the US can’t afford to cut the country off, or rejected the idea of treating it as an enemy.

He invoked the “American tech stack” 34 times across eight interviews since July 2025, arguing that the world’s developers should build on US technology. In eight interviews he said that 50% of the world’s AI researchers are Chinese or in China, suggesting the country’s tech talent can’t be ignored.
