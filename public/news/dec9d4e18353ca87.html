Nvidia Agrees to Pay $290M to Settle Investor Lawsuit Over Crypto Mining Sales

✍️ By Reuters

Nvidia has agreed to a $290 million settlement to resolve a class-action lawsuit filed by investors, who alleged the company misled shareholders regarding the proportion of gaming GPU revenue driven by crypto mining during the 2017 boom. The case, which survived multiple legal challenges including Supreme Court dismissal of Nvidia's appeal, awaits preliminary court approval.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Reuters Full Report

Nvidia (NASDAQ: $NVDA) has agreed to pay $290 million to settle a long-running US investor lawsuit accusing the chipmaker of misleading shareholders about the role crypto mining played in its gaming business.

The proposed settlement follows a lawsuit filed in 2018 by investors who claimed Nvidia failed to properly disclose how much of its graphics processing unit (GPU) revenue came from crypto miners rather than video game players, according to a court filing on Tuesday,

The investors, led by Stockholm-based asset manager E. Ohman J:or Fonder AB, argued that Nvidia presented strong gaming revenue as evidence of demand from gamers while a significant portion of sales was driven by crypto mining.

Nvidia denied wrongdoing as part of the agreement. The settlement still requires preliminary approval from a US judge before it can move forward.

The case centers on Nvidia's sales during the 2017 crypto boom, when rising prices for Bitcoin (CRYPTO: $BTC) and Ether (CRYPTO: $ETH) encouraged miners to buy large numbers of powerful GPUs.

Although Nvidia introduced graphics cards specifically designed for crypto mining, investors alleged that miners also purchased GeForce gaming GPUs, helping drive reported gaming revenue higher.

The issue became more apparent in 2018, when crypto prices fell and demand for mining hardware weakened. Nvidia subsequently lowered its revenue forecast, citing a sharp decline in crypto-related demand and excess inventory of gaming GPUs. The company's shares also came under pressure after the news.

A federal district court initially dismissed the case in 2021, but an appeals court revived key claims in August 2023. The US Supreme Court later dismissed Nvidia's appeal in December 2024, allowing the case to return to the lower court.

In March 2026, the court certified the lawsuit as a class action, covering investors who bought Nvidia shares between August 10, 2017, and November 15, 2018.

Nvidia shares are currently trading at $142.97, down around 8% today.

This case underscores a critical compliance boundary for hardware manufacturers: conflating volatile speculative demand with steady consumer growth is a legal liability.
