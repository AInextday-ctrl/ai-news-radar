📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Reuters Full Report

Nov 16 (Reuters) - The US Department of Justice will review DoorDash's proposed acquisition of Instacart, according to two people familiar with the matter, bringing antitrust scrutiny to the multibillion-dollar grocery delivery tie-up.

DoorDash said in October it would buy Instacart in a deal valued at about $17 billion to create a dominant platform for ordering food and household essentials online.

"We can affirm that the Department under President (Donald) Trump's leadership will vigorously enforce antitrust laws across industries," a DOJ spokesperson said, declining to confirm the review of the specific merger.

Spokespeople for the two companies did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Large mergers and acquisitions in the US are required to undergo review by either the DOJ or the Federal Trade Commission, which have historically split scrutiny based on industry expertise.

But if a deal poses competition problems, authorities can negotiate settlements requiring the sale of assets to competitors, or they can sue in federal court to block it.

DoorDash has agreed to pay Instacart $1.25 billion if the deal does not clear antitrust scrutiny, according to a securities filing.

Among standalone delivery apps, Instacart competes with DoorDash and Uber Eats, both of which have been aggressively expanding into grocery and other retail delivery.

Instacart last year announced a partnership with Grubhub, another food delivery platform, to expand restaurant choices on its app and tap into suburban markets.

DoorDash has touted its lead in grocery and retail, saying in February that it had more of those orders than Instacart.

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

This case sets a high benchmark for antitrust scrutiny in the platform economy, with the DOJ aiming to curb extreme concentration in delivery services to prevent algorithmic price manipulation. Should the merger be blocked, it will force DoorDash and its peers to pivot toward organic growth or niche vertical acquisitions rather than horizontal market consolidation.
