How Jensen Huang’s family shaped Nvidia’s Singapore dealings

✍️ By Michael Acton, Stephen Morris, and Joshua Franklin

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang has departed following a scandal involving extramarital affairs and a board investigation into potential foreign intelligence targeting, creating a leadership vacuum. Madison Huang, Senior Director of Product Marketing for physical AI, has seen her internal influence surge, actively participating in critical decision-making regarding Megaspeed scrutiny and personnel changes within the communications division.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] Jensen Huang (Former CEO), Madison Huang (Senior Director of Product Marketing for Physical AI): Board probe into CEO conduct leading to resignation and management restructuring; Madison Huang implicated in crisis communications and personnel management
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] CEO faced ethical scandals and potential foreign intelligence targeting amidst critical technical transition
  • [Key Metrics] The Singapore company in total bought roughly $5bn of Nvidia hardware.
  • [Key Position & Outlook] Outlook: Reorganization of leadership structure is imminent, with family involvement vs. professional management remaining a core tension.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Financial Times Full Report

Jensen Huang’s daughter was closely involved in orchestrating the public defence of Nvidia’s relationship with the Singapore-based company at the heart of the scandal that led to her father’s resignation as CEO.

Madison Huang, a senior director of product marketing at Nvidia, assumed a broader role outside her formal remit, helping to direct the company’s response to increasing press scrutiny of Megaspeed, the Singaporean cloud provider, according to three people familiar with the matter.

As the $3.8tn chip giant’s relationship with the little-known company first started to draw attention late last year, Nico Caprez, a fellow executive at Nvidia whom Madison Huang recently married, was promoted to a role that covered international deals with cloud computing clients in Asia, the people said.

The former Nvidia CEO’s decision to install family members in senior roles at what was until recently the world’s most valuable public company had caused some concerns over governance even before Huang resigned last week, according to current and former employees.

Caprez and Madison Huang’s links to Nvidia’s relationship with Megaspeed will heighten scrutiny of the pair as Nvidia’s board conducts a probe, which is being handled by a former top US prosecutor.

“They had a lot of power and influence and were allowed to operate unchecked,” said one colleague. “No one could tell them ‘no’ or put them in their place.”

Megaspeed, which has been under US investigation since 2024 over whether it was involved in smuggling chips to China in violation of American export controls, is at the centre of questions about Nvidia in the wake of Jensen Huang’s departure. On Tuesday, Nvidia’s board established a new committee to oversee compliance with US national security and export rules.

The Singapore company in total bought roughly $5bn of Nvidia hardware.

Jensen Huang has been summoned to testify about Nvidia’s relationship with Megaspeed — which has financial ties to China’s Alibaba — before Congress at a closed-door session in December.

The former Nvidia CEO has agreed to appear before the House committees on foreign affairs, intelligence and China for transcribed interviews on December 1.

“We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security,” said the committee chairs Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford in a joint statement on Tuesday.

Nvidia general counsel Teter is expected to appear before the committees on December 2.

Treasury secretary Scott Bessent has, meanwhile, faulted commerce secretary Howard Lutnick and his department for failing to blacklist Megaspeed. The commerce department has delayed new inclusions on the blacklist.

The FT has reported that US intelligence believes Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, who officials believe is a Chinese spy. That affair, as well as a second alleged relationship with a subordinate at Nvidia, precipitated the founder’s resignation last week after 33 years in charge.

The first reports on Megaspeed were published in The New York Times in October 2025, saying that commerce officials were probing its potential ties to China.

That autumn, Madison Huang started to involve herself directly in the tech giant’s communications response to questions about Nvidia’s chip sales to Megaspeed, the people said.

Madison Huang was seen talking to Alice Huang at an event in Taipei in 2025

Madison Huang had already, in the spring of 2025, privately hired crisis PR firm Heller for the family. The firm is well-known for handling high-profile individuals involved in sexual misconduct scandals, such as former congressman Anthony Weiner.

In the autumn, Heller then started to work directly for Nvidia.

Around the same time, Madison Huang temporarily excluded one of Nvidia’s top corporate communications executives from the group of people inside Nvidia who were discussing how to respond to queries about Megaspeed. The remaining group involved in crafting their responses included Nvidia general counsel Teter.

Nvidia told The New York Times at the time that it had visited its customer’s facilities multiple times and concluded it had no shareholders from China, and found no evidence of chip smuggling.

That same October, Madison Huang’s then partner and future husband, Caprez, who joined the company in 2024, was promoted to a director role overseeing Nvidia’s work with international cloud partners.

Two people said Caprez flew to Asia to oversee deals with cloud providers in the region and had been involved in deals with Asian cloud partners months before his official title changed. Megaspeed was one of Nvidia’s largest customers in south-east Asia.

By January of this year, Caprez — a Swiss former professional skier and consultant, who met Madison Huang when both were studying at London Business School — had been promoted yet again to vice-president of global AI infrastructure growth.

Nvidia, representatives for the Huang family and Heller declined to comment. Nvidia has previously stated that it added Megaspeed to its “block list” in June 2025, “prohibiting any further sales or shipments”.

The rapid advancement of Huang’s daughter and son-in-law alarmed some inside Nvidia. Several current and former employees said Madison was also difficult to work with.

Not all employees agreed with that assessment. One current Nvidia employee who worked closely with Madison Huang said her advancement reflected her talent and understanding of technical detail that resulted from “growing up inside Nvidia”.

Nor was her relatively undefined role compared with her job title unusual, they said, given Nvidia’s flat organisational structure, which leads to people unofficially taking on different roles.

Madison Huang has worked at Nvidia since 2020, initially joining as an intern in enterprise marketing and strategy, according to her LinkedIn profile. Before Nvidia, she trained as a chef and worked in Paris for LVMH in marketing and development.

She was promoted to her current role of senior director of product marketing for physical AI in 2025.

Jensen Huang’s son, Spencer Huang, has been an Nvidia employee since July 2022 and is currently a director of product management in its robots division. He ran a bar prior to his role at Nvidia.

Beyond Jensen Huang’s roughly 3.5 per cent stake in Nvidia, the founder now has no formal role. He has admitted to being unfaithful to his wife and acknowledged that his actions had hurt his wife and children.

Interim CEO Colette Kress is tasked with running the chipmaker at the heart of the AI boom and will oversee a business with about 40,000 employees, including Madison, her husband and brother.

“The new CEO is going to have to look at them like you would look at any other employee and see if they are an asset to the company or not,” said Nell Minow of ValueEdge Advisors, a corporate governance consulting firm.
