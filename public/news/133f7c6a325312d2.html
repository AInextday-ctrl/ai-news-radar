How Long Is GTA 6?

✍️ By Luke Reilly and Simon Cardy

How many hours does it take to play Grand Theft Auto 6? We have completion times from four different people.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

IGN Full Report

In every sense of the word, Grand Theft Auto 6 is a massive game. But just how much time will you have to set aside to see all of it? Well, that could potentially take years given the size of its map and the incredible amount of detail packed into it – we’re still exploring to find its many hidden secrets, hours after finishing the main mission chain. What we can give you an idea of, though, is just how long it took us to finish the story.

We’ve gathered playtimes from four different writers from across IGN and our sister websites, Eurogamer and Pure Xbox, in order to showcase just how much that hour total can vary. Here’s how long it took us to beat GTA 6, the playstyles each player opted for, and what they prioritised their time doing.

How Long is Grand Theft Auto 6?

• Our fastest player finished GTA 6 in 42 hours.

• Our “slowest” player took 115 hours to see the credits roll.

Everyone plays games differently, so read on for more details about how everyone played, how long it took to reach the credits, and some of their unique thoughts about the game's length. Once you've finished the game yourself, be sure to submit your times on How Long To Beat and see how your time compares!

Simon Cardy — Senior Editor, IGN

Completion Time: 70 hours

GTA 6 is a big game. So big, in fact, that even the 70 hours it took me to reach the end credits felt rushed at times. I started off sticking to the critical path, mainly focusing on Jason and Lucia’s main missions in Chapter 1, as I didn’t quite know how long the journey ahead was going to take me. To some extent, this whole opening act feels like an extended tutorial, and once the game really finds its rhythm in Chapter 2, I began to take things a little steadier, making sure to complete all optional missions before moving on to the next slice of story. This method of playing continued until I reached the end. Well, the first of many endings I have found so far. I’m not sure how many ways the couple’s tale can wrap up, but I’ve found four so far. I’ll leave what those are, and how you get them, up to you to find out, though.

Upon first finishing at that 70-hour mark, I had achieved 71% of GTA 6’s 100% checklist, meaning there were still plenty of activities to do, collectibles to find, cars to steal, and people to meet left for me in Leonida. I’ve since put in another 30 hours and only pushed 1% further, as I’ve been slowly exploring the vast map that stretches well beyond Vice City. There are a ludicrous number of collectibles to find in this game, including hundreds of playing cards, which really do feel like finding a needle in a haystack each and every time you stumble across them.

Those shall wait for another day, though. As will achieving that full 100%. For now, I’ve started a fresh save, already making my way through Chapter 1 again in an effort to see how GTA 6’s story changes and the missions within it branch when you try to keep Jason and Lucia’s relationship as frosty as possible. I won’t lie, though, it’s tough. Rockstar has done a phenomenal job at building a bond between them, and in my original 70 hours I was compelled to keep them at a full “hot together” relationship rating, so having to tear them out of my heart now feels quite painful. It’s all in the name of science, I guess.

Luke Reilly — Senior Editor, IGN

Completion Time: 115 hours

With the extraordinarily rare luxury of having more than three weeks to play through Grand Theft Auto 6 in order to write IGN’s review, I was actually able to move through it in pretty much precisely the same sort of way I would’ve if I’d been playing exclusively for pleasure. For me, this means allowing myself to be constantly distracted by minigames, exploring interesting areas, and stopping for every stranger I encounter. There was really no point where I ever ignored doing something I felt like doing because I felt obligated to tackle a story mission instead. As a result, it took me 115 hours to reach GTA 6’s finale.

My Jason and Lucia maintained a professional criminal rating throughout thanks to my style of play, where I typically opt for non-lethal takedowns against general law enforcement, disabling vehicles by popping tyres rather than pummeling the windscreens with gunfire, and leaving civilians alone. By the end of the story, I had actually only killed 11 civilians, all of whom were accidents.

I’ve seen two endings so far, and my overall completion rate is at 72% now that I’ve reached the end of the story, but I feel I’m likely to simply start again at this point rather than push this save to 100%. This is because I actually really want to experience it from the beginning again, only slower. More racing, more fishing, more exploring; I want to see how long it’ll take me without a deadline – even if that deadline was a surprisingly generous one. And can I reach the end without killing a single civilian? I’m about to find out.

Chris Tapsell — Editor in Chief, Eurogamer

Completion Time: 65 Hours

It took me about 65 hours to complete GTA 6's story, which I expect to be on the faster end compared to most other reviewers, and there's a bit of a caveat which explains that, too: I opted to really focus hard on the main story and character-based side missions in the final quarter of it or so. For a short while, towards the end, I actually got in-game taxis between missions to "fast travel" and speed progress up, partially to test how that might affect the game world – so much of the game is discovered while travelling; what happens if you skip that? – and partially just so I could make sure I got to the end in time.

Saying that, I've still done all of the character-based side missions that I'm aware of, and just have a few of the more significant questlines that come from 'emergent events' to get through (although I get the sense there are dozens and dozens of smaller one-off versions of these still to see). There are still corners of the map to properly explore, and I've only scraped the surface of some systems like fishing, diving, and racing. Between hitting your savings goals and doing all of the side activities, let alone just hanging out and following your interests as you go, I'd still need well over a hundred hours with it. I wouldn't expect many people to finish the story in under 70 hours and honestly wouldn't recommend it either. Take your time with it – the game's at its best when it's played slow!

Fraser Gilbert — Editor, Pure Xbox

Completion Time: 42 hours

The vast majority of my time in Leonida has been spent with the main campaign missions, akin to what you might call a mainline, even “rushed” playthrough. It took me exactly 42 hours to finish Jason and Lucia's story, and a handful of those hours were spent doing optional activities like side missions, playing minigolf, stealing motorised scooters, and simply wandering around the huge metropolis that is Vice City.

That might sound disappointing if you were expecting a 100+ hour epic, or if you were putting a lot of stock into the quote that mentioned 80 hours a few months ago, but this isn't the kind of game you ideally want to rush. Take your time with it, explore the ridiculously impressive open-world, and allow it to divert your attention towards any number of activities that aren't directly connected to the main storyline. There's so much more to do beyond just the core campaign, and I reckon some players will easily reach 80 hours or more before they get to the end credits.

Now that I've hustled my way through it for the Pure Xbox review, my plan is to go back through the campaign at a much slower pace over the next few weeks and months, and I can't wait to see what surprises I missed the first time around. There are parts of the map I haven't even visited yet!

So, that’s how long it took each of us to finish GTA 6’s main story. How are you planning to play Rockstar’s latest? And how are you going to play as Jason and Lucia? In or out of love? As violent psychos or clean professionals? Let us know in the comments.
