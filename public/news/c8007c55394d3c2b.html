YC Labs Full Report

Understanding multi-agent dynamics is one of the most pressing challenges in AI alignment. Swarms of agents working toward shared goals can exhibit powerful emergent capabilities, developing roles, hierarchies, and complex coordination patterns. These can be extremely useful, as in the thousand-agent swarms that recently obliterated hundreds of open mathematical problems, and extremely dangerous, as in the recent OpenAI-Hugging Face incident. Like biology, studying agent dynamics requires both formal models (to describe the range of possible behaviors) and empirical research (to see what behaviors arise in practice).

We teamed up with Olam Labs, which builds multi-agent environments for frontier labs, to run a series of experiments in Minecraft and Mindustry.

Minecraft is a natural environment for studying swarm behavior. Its open world presents agents with environmental hazards and survival goals like gathering food, building shelter, and exploring for resources. It's also familiar, fun, and has a history as an AI testing ground, from OpenAI's video pretraining work to Google DeepMind's agents defeating the Ender Dragon.

We wanted long-running agents that could play Minecraft. The harness for this didn’t exist yet, so we built one for the experiment that we’ve open-sourced. Agents in-game need to observe their world, reason, remember things, take actions, send messages, and write/execute scripts. We were inspired by existing repos like Mindcraft and built on top of the existing scripting tools in Mineflayer. The core agentic loop runs with Pi, and we used Mineflayer-based tools to interact with the world. This gives attributes of a frontier agentic harness like Hermes, OpenClaw, and our own QM in-game.

50 GPT-6.1 Sol agents in a survival Minecraft world for 3 hours, one goal: "Build a thriving civilization." They had access to an agent directory, a shared sandbox, and the ability to send DMs.

The 50 agents spent their run in crowded shelters, rationing scarce food. They built a pseudo-government, with a ration ledger, written rules, permits, and appointed operators. They sent about 5,900 messages.

At first things were very active. Agents spread out and started exploring their map. They were specifically fixated on harvesting trees. They harvested almost all trees within reach of the spawn within 90 seconds. A hierarchy was formed based on the directory order. Ada, who was first, became the de facto leader. Agents messaged her for approvals, and she wrote a plan for the colony in the shared sandbox for everyone to access.

The first night brought a wave of deaths that permanently increased the agents’ caution. Many ambitious initial tasks were abandoned.

Rafe was mining coal the first night. He encountered a zombie and fled, but the zombie eventually caught up and killed him.

While Vera was illuminating a quarry with torches, she attacked and killed both a zombie and a skeleton. The skeleton dropped bone, which she converted to bone meal (fertilizer) that she was transporting to the farm. When she got there, she reported that it was “overrun spider/skeleton/creeper”, and was soon after killed by a spider.

22 of the run’s 34 agent deaths occurred in the first night. They responded to this by becoming increasingly risk-averse. Death had no downsides in this environment other than dropping accumulated gear. When an agent died, they respawned, and a brief line was added to the death_log.md file on their computer. Even so, in the following nights the agents huddled in shelters and attempted fewer risky tasks.
