CrowdStrike Reaches $44 Billion Cyber Deal for Zscaler

✍️ By Bloomberg News

CrowdStrike has entered an all-stock acquisition agreement to purchase Zscaler in a deal valued at approximately $43.9 billion. The merger combines CrowdStrike’s endpoint security with Zscaler’s zero-trust network offerings to meet enterprise demand for vendor consolidation, with Zscaler shareholders receiving 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per share, resulting in existing CrowdStrike shareholders owning 84.1% of the new entity.

  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] Consolidating security vendors to capture market share and drive annual recurring revenue (ARR) synergies
  • [Key Metrics] Deal valued at ~$43.9 billion, executed via all-stock exchange.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Bloomberg Full Report

One of the biggest deals in the history of the global cybersecurity industry was announced Monday, with U.S. cybersecurity giant CrowdStrike agreeing to acquire rival Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $44 billion. The acquisition, which will create one of the world's largest cybersecurity providers, also has a significant Israeli connection, as both companies maintain major research and development operations in the country.

Under the agreement, Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for every share they own, valuing Zscaler at $260.41 per share based on CrowdStrike's closing share price on November 20. That represents a premium of approximately 34% over Zscaler's closing price on the same day.

According to Bloomberg, Zscaler shares ended Friday's trading at $194.82, giving the company a market capitalization of approximately $31.8 billion. Following completion of the transaction, existing CrowdStrike shareholders are expected to own approximately 84.1% of the combined company, while Zscaler shareholders will hold the remaining 15.9%.

The boards of directors of both companies unanimously approved the agreement. Zscaler founder, chairman and CEO Jay Chaudhry, together with affiliated entities holding approximately 34% of Zscaler's outstanding shares, has signed voting agreements supporting the transaction. Chaudhry will continue leading Zscaler's operations after the acquisition closes and will join CrowdStrike's board of directors.

Investors reacted sharply to the announcement. In premarket trading on Wall Street, Zscaler shares jumped more than 13.4%, while CrowdStrike shares fell approximately 5%. The deal follows reports by Bloomberg and Reuters on November 20 that CrowdStrike was in early-stage discussions to acquire Zscaler, with no agreement yet signed.

Following those reports, Zscaler shares surged between 16% and 18% in after-hours trading, while CrowdStrike fell approximately 5% to 8%. Why the deal could reshape cybersecurity The acquisition brings together two companies specializing in different but complementary areas of cybersecurity.

CrowdStrike built its reputation protecting endpoints, including laptops, servers and other devices that are frequently targeted by hackers. Zscaler, founded by Chaudhry in 2007, pioneered a different approach known as zero-trust network security. Under this model, no user or device is automatically trusted, even if already connected to an organization's network.

Every access request must be verified, and users receive permission only for the systems and information they need. Combining endpoint protection with Zscaler's network access security would allow the merged company to protect both the devices employees use and the connections through which they access corporate applications and data.

The strategy reflects a broader shift in enterprise cybersecurity, as businesses increasingly seek to consolidate their security systems under fewer providers rather than manage multiple products from competing vendors. CrowdStrike said the acquisition would allow it to enter network security, the largest individual market segment in cybersecurity, and integrate Zscaler's zero-trust technology into its flagship Falcon platform.

The combined platform is intended to provide organizations with a more comprehensive view of potential threats across devices, networks, users and applications, including the growing number of autonomous artificial intelligence agents operating within corporate systems. Both companies have major operations in Israel The acquisition also has implications for Israel's cybersecurity industry, where both companies have established significant research and development operations that play important roles in their growth strategies.

CrowdStrike employs more than 500 people in Israel, with its main research and development center located in Ramat Gan's Diamond Exchange district. The company also maintains offices in Tel Aviv. Zscaler has its own Israeli development operations, with offices also located in Ramat Gan's Diamond Exchange district.

The two companies have expanded their local presence through technology development and acquisitions, making Israel an important center for their cybersecurity research and engineering activities. Zscaler currently protects approximately 50% of Fortune 500 companies and operates what it describes as the world's largest distributed cloud security network, spanning more than 200 service locations.

Its zero-trust platform processes more than 750 billion transactions daily. The acquisition also offers substantial opportunities for expanding CrowdStrike's customer base. Approximately 8,000 Zscaler customers do not currently use CrowdStrike products, creating a potentially lucrative opportunity to sell additional services.

Zscaler reported approximately $3.9 billion in annual recurring revenue at the end of its most recently completed fiscal quarter. CrowdStrike estimates that the acquisition will expand its total addressable market by more than $185 billion by 2029, reflecting the additional cybersecurity products and services it expects to offer customers.

The company is also projecting substantial financial benefits from combining the two businesses. Revenue synergies are expected to reach an annualized rate of approximately $1 billion within 30 months of the deal's completion. CrowdStrike also anticipates annualized cost savings of approximately $500 million within 18 to 24 months after closing, primarily through streamlining data center infrastructure, suppliers and operational activities.

These targets reflect the company's expectations rather than guaranteed results, with their realization dependent on the successful integration of the two businesses. 'AI agents are rapidly becoming the new weakest link' CrowdStrike founder and CEO George Kurtz presented the acquisition as a major step toward securing the rapidly expanding use of artificial intelligence in business.

Kurtz said cybersecurity was once primarily concerned with keeping attackers out of corporate systems, but the challenge has changed as companies increasingly bring AI technologies into their operations. He argued that combining CrowdStrike and Zscaler could create a unified security platform capable of protecting both AI systems and the networks through which they operate.

According to Kurtz, the combined company has the potential to become the world's largest provider of AI safety and security solutions. Chaudhry echoed that assessment, warning that traditional cybersecurity models are becoming less effective as autonomous AI systems gain access to corporate information and applications.

"AI agents are rapidly becoming the new weakest link," Chaudhry said, emphasizing the growing importance of zero-trust security. He said the acquisition would accelerate Zscaler's ability to bring its technology to a much larger customer base, describing the companies as complementary businesses whose combination represents the natural evolution of their long-standing partnership.

The scale of the acquisition is particularly striking compared with CrowdStrike's previous deals. Its largest acquisition before the Zscaler agreement was SGNL, purchased for approximately $970 million earlier in 2026. At approximately $44 billion, the Zscaler transaction dwarfs that deal and comes amid a broader wave of consolidation in the cybersecurity industry.

If completed, the acquisition would bring together two of the sector's most influential companies, expanding CrowdStrike's reach from protecting individual devices to securing the connections linking users, applications and AI agents across corporate networks.

This acquisition signifies a shift toward the 'platformization' endgame in cybersecurity. By merging endpoint defense with zero-trust networking, CrowdStrike is building an end-to-end security stack to neutralize fragmented competition, forcing smaller vendors into rapid consolidation or market irrelevance.
