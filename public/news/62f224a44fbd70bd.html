📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Bloomberg Full Report

The US Department of Justice has accused a Russian national of spying on employees of a Silicon Valley technology and defense company in an alleged effort to gather intelligence for Russia.

On Wednesday, the justice department announced that Igor D’iu had been indicted on charges of conspiring to act as an agent of a foreign government, acting as an agent of a foreign government without notifying the attorney general, interstate stalking and fraud involving identification documents.

According to officials, D’iu allegedly entered the US unlawfully this summer after being recruited by co-conspirators through Telegram. His alleged mission was to “carry out clandestine assignments targeting employees of a large technology and defense company with an office in Palo Alto, California”, the justice department said, without identifying the company or the alleged co-conspirators.

Bloomberg reported that the company in question was Palantir Technologies, citing an unnamed source familiar with the matter. The firm has a large office in Palo Alto.

The department added that D’iu had reportedly received instructions, equipment and financial support from the co-conspirators, who told him that his work was for “the Motherland”.

They also allegedly instructed him to gather information about the company because it was supplying weapons used against Russia in Ukraine.

The Russian embassy was contacted for comment.

In June, D’iu allegedly traveled from Istanbul to Mexico City, where his co-conspirators connected him with an individual who helped him cross the US-Mexico border. After entering the US, he was allegedly instructed to purchase laptops, cellphones, wireless networking equipment and remote access software to “facilitate his assignments”.

D’iu’s co-conspirators also allegedly directed him to visit a cafe near the company’s Palo Alto office, identify employees and photograph their laptop screens as they worked. They further instructed him to “position his laptop at designated locations near the company so they could remotely access it and attempt to collect information from company employees in the vicinity”, the justice department said.

The alleged surveillance gradually expanded, with D’iu allegedly instructed to monitor employees and their residences. The justice department said he reportedly conducted online searches for multiple employees’ home addresses, including that of a senior executive.

In early September, D’iu allegedly followed a female employee in a silver Lexus SUV for nearly 30 miles (48km) as she drove from the company’s Palo Alto office toward San Francisco. According to the justice department, the employee repeatedly attempted to evade him, at one point driving into a grocery store parking lot.

Officials said D’iu “allegedly aggressively continued following her, including through U-turns and illegal left turns, and ran a red light to remain behind her vehicle”. The employee eventually called 911 and was instructed to drive to the San Francisco police department’s Mission station.

D’iu allegedly abandoned the pursuit after the employee contacted a police officer. The justice department added that investigators had also obtained evidence indicating he had followed other employees on previous occasions.

The justice department also accused D’iu of obtaining fraudulent identification documents to conceal his identity. Investigators reportedly intercepted two international packages containing concealed ID documents, including a fake California ID card and an altered US passport. Officials said the passport was authentic and had originally been issued to another individual in the US before being modified to display D’iu’s photograph and assumed identity.

Investigators also discovered a social security card bearing the name “Hugo Landry Johns”. During an FBI search of D’iu’s East Palo Alto residence in October, investigators recovered approximately $18,000 in cash, two laptops, two iPhones, two Russian passports, wireless networking equipment and additional fraudulent ID documents.

They also found a notebook containing 10 handwritten addresses, several of which belonged to the residences of company employees. The notebook also included detailed profiles of at least three employees, documenting their family members’ names, educational and employment histories, and an “assessment of their strengths and weaknesses”.

In response to the indictment, M Scott Proctor, acting principal deputy assistant attorney general for national security, said: “The Americans who build our defense technology help keep this country safe by arming our warfighters with the tools and weapons they need to prevail against our adversaries and enemies.

“As alleged, D’iu entered our country illegally at the direction of foreign handlers to surveil, follow and gather information about some of those who work in this vital industry. Such conduct, if proven, is a flagrant violation of US sovereignty and an unacceptable attempt at intimidation,” he added.
