SEC Is Probing Nvidia Amid Departure of CEO Jensen Huang

✍️ By Dave Michaels and Anissa Gardizy and Robbie Whelan

The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is investigating Nvidia to evaluate whether its prior disclosures provided shareholders with an accurate depiction of its business. The inquiry remains in the preliminary fact-finding stage, focusing on whether the company's reporting on US chip export controls and related risks meets material disclosure requirements under securities laws.

⚡ Executive Summary & Key Facts

  • [Core Event] US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), Nvidia: Reviewing the accuracy and completeness of investor disclosures
  • [Root Cause & Mechanism] To verify if the company fulfilled its obligations regarding risk disclosure amidst export control challenges
  • [Key Metrics] The civil investigation adds to the crisis facing Nvidia, whose shares have fallen more than 10% since Huang was ousted.
  • [Industry Impact & Outlook] Key compliance risk centers on the disclosure of export control impacts.

📰 Full In-Depth Coverage

Wall Street Journal Full Report

The Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia, whose star chief executive Jensen Huang recently resigned after revelations of extramarital affairs, one with a suspected Chinese spy and another with an employee, according to people familiar with the matter.

The civil investigation adds to the crisis facing Nvidia, whose shares have fallen more than 10% since Huang was ousted. The focus of the investigation couldn’t immediately be learned but includes whether the company’s disclosures misled investors, some of the people said,

Nvidia posted a stronger-than-expected third quarter and a bullish outlook in its first earnings report after Jensen Huang was ousted as CEO in a scandal that has reportedly spawned an SEC investigation.

On the company's earnings call, newly appointed chairman Harvey Jones said the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," despite a movement among some employees and investors to bring him back.

Why it matters: Nvidia remains squarely at the center of the AI economy — and investors are anxious for guidance on the company's strategic roadmap following Huang's sudden exit.

Zoom in: Nvidia recorded quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion and net income of $65.3 billion, both more than doubling its performance from a year earlier.

S&P Capital IQ analysts had expected revenue of $108.7 billion and net income of $60.5 billion.

The company's data center revenue — its largest segment — rose 110% from a year ago to $107.6 billion.

The showing — which included a whopping gross profit margin of 74.8% — immediately calmed investors who have shaved more than $1 trillion off of Nvidia's market cap since the Huang scandal broke.

The stock was up as much as 5% in after-hours trading Tuesday following the company's earnings call.

It marks the first moment for interim CEO Colette Kress — who is also still serving as CFO — to exert her leadership during the most tumultuous moment in the company's history.

On the earnings call, she struck a steady tone, saying the company's momentum and innovation remain substantial.

Kress didn't mention Huang by name on the call but acknowledged the turbulent time and the "diverse" opinions — an apparent reference to the campaign to bring back Huang.

What we're watching: WSJ reported Tuesday afternoon that the SEC has opened a civil probe into the company.

An SEC spokesperson declined to comment.

Nvidia reps did not immediately respond to a request for comment, and the executives didn't discuss it on the call — but they said their quarterly 10Q filing with the SEC will be delayed.

The earnings call came hours after Huang posted a public letter saying he'd be "honored" to return as CEO.

Tech analyst Dan Ives earlier said the board could face a special shareholder meeting called by at least 15% of shareholders.

Jones said the company would not take questions on Huang's exit. He said the board would consider external and internal candidates and wants to move "as quickly as possible" but won't make a move until it finds "exactly the right person."

The intrigue: Huang reportedly faced pushback from Kress as he pushed financing deals to backstop the AI ecosystem.

On Tuesday's call, Kress signaled that the company would continue to provide financing where appropriate but would be "selective" and ensure that guarantees are necessary.

Editor's note: This story was updated to include comments from Nvidia chairman Harvey Jones and interim CEO Colette Kress made on the company's earnings call.

🤖 AI Radar Technical Deep-Dive

The core of this inquiry lies in whether Nvidia’s disclosures regarding supply chain fragility and market loss under geopolitical sanctions have adequately informed shareholders. Should regulators prove intentional obfuscation of the impact of export controls, the firm risks administrative penalties and shareholder class-action lawsuits, potentially exerting pressure on the valuation premium built upon its aggressive market growth projections.
