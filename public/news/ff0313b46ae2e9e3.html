Wall Street Journal Full Report

Revenue increased 10% to RMB271.519 billion ($40.498 billion), according to the company’s earnings release. Non-GAAP net income rose 92% to RMB19.909 billion, while adjusted diluted earnings per American depositary share increased 86% to RMB8.10 ($1.21). Each ADS represents eight ordinary shares.

The stock traded at $83.00, down $1.37, or 1.62%, in a premarket quotation timestamped 6:54 a.m. EST on November 19, 2026. Its November 18 closing price was $84.37 at 4 p.m. EST. Research and market data were checked at 7:04 a.m. EST; premarket prices can move sharply in thinner trading.

Reuters reported that revenue exceeded an LSEG consensus of RMB269.81 billion, but adjusted earnings per ADS missed expectations of RMB11.16. The mixed result helps explain why faster cloud growth did not immediately lift the shares.

Reported net income attributable to ordinary shareholders rose 207% to RMB64.535 billion, benefiting from investment gains excluded from adjusted earnings.

AI Cloud and Compute Services revenue increased 53% to RMB60.973 billion. External-customer recurring revenue grew 67%, excluding compute-equipment solutions. The segment’s adjusted earnings before interest, tax and amortisation increased 121% to RMB8.838 billion, with a 14.5% margin.

That improvement did not cover the losses in a separate AI business. AI Labs and Applications recorded an adjusted EBITA loss of RMB10.503 billion, against RMB3.435 billion a year earlier. Revenue in that unit increased 51% to RMB4.435 billion, but investment in AI capabilities and higher Qwen application inference costs weighed on profitability.

Capital expenditure reached RMB50.477 billion. Operating cash flow increased 55% to RMB15.690 billion, yet company-defined free cash flow deteriorated to an outflow of RMB34.472 billion from RMB21.840 billion. Over six months, free-cash outflow totalled RMB79.142 billion.

Alibaba retained RMB487.929 billion in cash and other liquid investments at September 30. Its balance-sheet bank borrowings and listed categories of notes and exchangeable bonds totalled a calculated RMB267.209 billion. The broad liquidity measure includes treasury investments; it is larger than cash alone.

Financing also carries a shareholder cost. The company raised net proceeds of HK$80 billion through new shares during the half year. Quarterly weighted-average diluted ordinary shares used for non-GAAP earnings increased to 19,777 million from 19,168 million, showing dilution alongside the investment programme.

The commerce picture remained uneven. China e-commerce revenue declined 5%, although customer-management revenue would have risen 1% excluding a subsidy-related accounting effect. International e-commerce revenue fell 8%, partly because EU customs reforms affected AliExpress. Alibaba E-commerce Group’s adjusted EBITA nevertheless increased 243%, helped by improved quick-commerce operations.

Broker targets remain much higher than the share price. Dated records show Goldman Sachs at Buy/$177 on November 19, Bernstein at a provider-normalised Buy/$165 on November 18, Bank of America at Buy/$178 on November 11, and Mizuho at a provider-normalised Buy/$200 on November 3.

Earlier assessments include J.P. Morgan’s Overweight/$200 on October 12, Jefferies’ Buy/$192 on October 8 and UBS’s Buy/$206 on September 22. Most predate these results; Goldman’s same-day entry does not establish whether its assessment followed the release.

Alibaba’s stronger cloud economics are offset by AI development losses and negative free cash flow. Its published trailing earnings multiple of 19.26 compares with 18.99 at fiscal 2026 year-end and JD.com’s (Nasdaq:JD) 15.23. Investment gains, different business models and database timing limit those comparisons.

Over the next 12 months, monitor external cloud growth, AI spending relative to cash generation, and commerce profitability. Slower cloud demand, persistent application losses or further dilutive financing without better cash returns would weaken the recovery case.
